# 01d — What athletes need: protein, carbs and fibre (secondary research)

**Date:** 8 Oct 2026 · **Method:** web search of position stands, systematic reviews and surveys. Figures were read via search-engine extracts; open the originals before quoting exact numbers on slides. Sources are listed at the end and in `research/sources.md`.

## The short version (for a slide)
1. **Protein per recovery snack: ~0.25–0.3 g/kg, about 20–25 g for most athletes.** Our T20 bar (20.9 g) is one full dose for a ~70–80 kg athlete.
2. **Carbs are what athletes actually miss.** Professional and semi-professional team-sport athletes generally **meet or exceed protein targets but fall short on energy and carbohydrate** (systematic review, 21 studies). Most "protein bars" add more protein and few real carbs; T20 adds both.
3. **Fibre is the forgotten nutrient.** **Only ~4% of UK adults reach 30 g a day.** Sports scientists now suggest athletes build to ~30 g/day for gut health. One T20 bar gives ~7 g (≈ a quarter of that).

## 1. Protein

| Guidance | Amount | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Per meal / after exercise | **~0.3 g/kg** of high-quality protein, spread through the day | ACSM / Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics / Dietitians of Canada joint position (2016) `[EVIDENCE: secondary summary]` |

**What that means per bar:**
| Athlete weight | 0.25 g/kg | 0.3 g/kg |
|---|---|---|
| 60 kg | 15 g | 18 g |
| 70 kg | 17.5 g | 21 g |
| 80 kg | 20 g | 24 g |
| 100 kg | 25 g | 30 g |

**→ 20 g covers a full recovery dose for most athletes up to ~80 kg.** Heavier athletes (rugby forwards) would want ~25–30 g: a bar plus milk, or 1½ bars.
**Interview link:** protein focus came up in 4/10 interviews; the professor's guide (~10 g protein per 100 kcal) and the S&C coach's "high protein:carb ratio" match this `[EVIDENCE: inputs/customer-persona.md]`.

## 2. Carbohydrate

| Guidance | Amount | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Per day, by training load | **3–10 g/kg** (up to 12 g/kg for extreme endurance) | ACSM/AND/DC (2016) `[EVIDENCE: secondary summary]` |
| Fast refuelling (another session within ~8 h) | **1.0–1.2 g/kg per hour** for the first 4 h | Contemporary guidelines, e.g. 2025 recovery review; ≥1.2 g/kg/h maximises glycogen (Alghannam et al. 2018) `[EVIDENCE]` |
| When carbs are lower than that | **Adding protein helps glycogen recovery** when carbs are ≤0.8 g/kg/h | Alghannam et al. (2018) `[EVIDENCE]` |

**What athletes actually eat:**
- **Jenner et al. (2019), *Nutrients*:** systematic review of 21 studies (511 professional and semi-professional team-sport athletes). **Protein and fat met or exceeded recommendations; energy and carbohydrate did not.** The authors called for recommendations focused on energy and carbs `[EVIDENCE]`.
- **Female field-based team-sport athletes** (systematic review): most studies found **insufficient energy, carbohydrate and iron**, mainly because carbohydrate targets weren't met `[EVIDENCE]`.
- **Knowledge gap:** NCAA Division III athletes (n = 331) scored **36.9%** on a sports-nutrition knowledge test. Their top information sources were **social media, coaches and athletic trainers** `[EVIDENCE]`. That supports reaching athletes **through coaches** (our S&C-coach channel).

**What that means for the bar:**
- A 75 kg athlete refuelling fast needs ~75–90 g carbs per hour. **No bar does that alone.** T20's ~26 g is a kick-start (~0.35 g/kg) to pair with a meal or milk.
- Because carbs are lower than the fast-refuel target, **the 20 g of protein is doing useful work** (Alghannam 2018).
- **Our angle:** lab protein bars give 20 g protein but only 15–22 g carbs, often as polyols that don't count as recovery carbohydrate under GB claims law. T20 gives **20 g protein + 26 g real carbs (oats)**, filling the carb shortfall athletes actually have.

## 3. Fibre: digestion and gut health

| Fact | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| UK recommendation | **30 g/day** for adults (raised in 2015) | SACN / NDNS `[EVIDENCE]` |
| UK reality | **Only ~4% of adults meet 30 g/day**; 96% don't | NDNS 2019–2023 via Food Foundation and gov.uk `[EVIDENCE]` |
| For athletes | If habitual intake is **<20 g/day**, ramp up to **~30 g/day, including ~2 g oat beta-glucan, over ~6 weeks** | Mancin, Rollo & Burke (2025), *Sports Medicine* `[EVIDENCE]` |
| Why: gut health | Fibre helps preserve **gut microbiome diversity and stability** and **intestinal barrier function**, and feeds bacteria that make **short-chain fatty acids** | Mancin et al. 2025 `[EVIDENCE]` |
| Why it matters for high-protein eaters | High-protein diets are linked to fewer SCFA-producing "good" bacteria; **enough fibre may offset this** | Review / secondary summaries `[EVIDENCE: secondary]` |
| Why: digestion | Regularity and stool health (general fibre evidence) | `[ASSUMPTION: general nutrition knowledge]` |
| Caution | **Keep fibre low *before* and during exercise** to avoid gut trouble (ACSM). 30–50% of athletes report gut complaints around exercise | ACSM via Mancin 2025; de Oliveira et al. 2014 `[EVIDENCE]` |
| Evidence strength | **No randomised trials** yet on athlete fibre targets; 20 g and 30 g are expert-proposed | Mancin et al. 2025 `[EVIDENCE]` |

**What that means for the bar:**
- One T20 bar ≈ **6.9 g fibre**: about 23% of 30 g/day, from **oat bran, oats and chia** (no chicory/inulin, which are high-FODMAP).
- **Oat bran is ~5–8% beta-glucan** `[ASSUMPTION: typical values; check the pack]`, so the 25 g in a bar gives roughly **1.3–2 g beta-glucan**, close to the ~2 g/day suggested.
- **Position it as an after-training bar, never pre-workout.**
- **Say:** "~7 g fibre from oats for everyday gut health." **Don't** claim it improves digestion, the microbiome or immunity: there's no authorised GB health claim for that. (The oat beta-glucan *cholesterol* claim exists but needs ≥1 g per portion plus mandatory wording, and it's off-message for athletes.)

## 4. What athletes say they want (preferences)
- **Carey, Doyle & Lucey (2023)**, n = 405: product priorities were **nutritional profile (3.4/4), taste (3.2), accessibility (3.1)**. "Enhanced muscle recovery" was the most sought claim (25% first choice). **Whole-food products were the most preferred** `[EVIDENCE]`.
- **Forsyth & Mantzioris (2023)**, n = 140: everyday foods rated **tastier, cheaper, lower doping risk**; sports foods won on **convenience** `[EVIDENCE]`.
- **Your interviews:** high protein (4/10), whole foods (4/10), taste (3/10), price (3/10). Coach: "high protein:carb ratio and some fibre for digestion". Professor: ~10 g protein per 100 kcal with high carbs `[EVIDENCE: inputs]`.
