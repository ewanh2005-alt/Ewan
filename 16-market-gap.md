# 16: The gap in the market (for the pitch)

## The gap in one line
**In the UK you can get a snack that's high in protein, or made from real food, or low in sugar, but not one that's all three and grab-and-go.**

---

## 1. Supply side: what's on the shelf (25 products scored)

**The four tests**
1. **≥10 g protein** per serving.
2. **Protein from real food:** egg, dairy or nuts, not isolates or powders.
3. **No sweeteners or sugar alcohols.**
4. **≤5 g sugar per 50 g.**

| Test | Products passing (of 25) |
|---|---|
| ≥10 g protein | 20 |
| Low sugar (≤5 g per 50 g) | 11 |
| No sweeteners or polyols (confirmed) | 14 |
| Protein from real food | **4** (Veloforte Forza, 33Fuel, Nākd Protein, RXBAR) |
| High protein **and** low sugar | 10, all using powders, and most use sweeteners or polyols |
| Real food **and** ≥10 g protein | 3 (Veloforte Forza 19.7 g, 33Fuel 19 g, RXBAR 13.5 g sugar per 50 g) |
| **All four** | **0** |
| Graft v6 (calculated) | ✅ passes all four: ~14.6 g protein, ~3.8 g sugar per 50 g |

`[EVIDENCE: 02-competitors.csv; 02d WR40–WR52; RXBAR US/CA label]`

**What the chart shows** (`16-gap-chart.png`):

- The real-food bars (green) all sit far to the right, with **13–20 g of sugar per 50 g**.

|---|---|---|

**So the gap is UK-specific,** and it's for *fresh* real food.

## 2. Demand side: people want what's missing
- **Athletes prefer whole food:** it was the #1 preferred product type among 405 athletes `[EVIDENCE: S7, Carey 2023]`.
- **Real food wins on everything but convenience:** athletes rate everyday food tastier, cheaper and safer `[EVIDENCE: S6, Forsyth 2023]`.
- **Coaches say eat whole food**, and boxes of Grenade bars went uneaten `[EVIDENCE: E1, C7]`.
- **80% of athletes say their schedule limits cooking** `[EVIDENCE: S29]`.
- **71% of UK adults try to avoid ultra-processed food** `[EVIDENCE: WR53, Mintel 2025]`.
- Bars are seen as "a necessary evil" `[EVIDENCE: R06, weak]`.
- 7 of 10 interviewees liked the concept `[EVIDENCE: 03-persona.md]`.

## 3. Why the gap exists (have this ready for the judges)
1. **Shelf life.** Shops want bars that last 6–12 months. Whey, polyols and glycerol keep bars soft for that long; real food doesn't `[EVIDENCE: Food Manufacture 2017]`.
2. **Physics.** Real food holds water, so protein per gram is lower: about 15 g per 50 g at most for a moist bar `[EVIDENCE: 04e]`.
3. **Cost and safety.** Fresh egg and dairy cost more and need tighter food safety controls.
4. **How big brands work.** They make months of stock in big factories, so bake-to-order doesn't fit.
5. **What happened to others:**
   - RXBAR went shelf-stable at 12 g protein `[H1]`.
   - Perfect Bar and Mooski proved fridge bars sell, but only in the US `[H2, H9]`.
   - JNCK struggled to sell "healthier" fresh bakes through supermarkets `[H3]`.

**How Graft fills it:** don't fight shelf life, design around it. Bake to order, chill or freeze, and sell direct to squads, which doesn't need supermarket shelf life.

## 4. Caveats (say them before a judge does)
- Graft's numbers are **calculated, not lab-tested**, and the recipe isn't proven yet. The taste test fixes that.
- 25 products is a sample, not every bar in the UK. Sugar figures were missing for some, which were left out.
- "Real food" is our own test: we classed protein crisps, isolates, dried milk and egg powders as powders.

## Say it (30 seconds)
> "Protein bars give you protein from powders and sweeteners. Natural bars give you real food with lots of sugar. Quark gives you real protein, but in a pot. I scored 25 snacks on four tests: high protein, real-food protein, no sweeteners, low sugar. Not one passed all four. That's not because nobody thought of it. It's because real food doesn't last a year on a shelf. Graft doesn't try to. We bake to order and deliver fresh to squads."
