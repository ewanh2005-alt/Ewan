# 10 — From home kitchen to national: scale and impact plan

**Date:** 8 Oct 2026 · **Numbers from:** `research/10_scale_model.py` (every input tagged EVIDENCE or ASSUMPTION) · **Sources:** `research/sources.md`
**Covers judging criteria:** Impact & Scale, Feasibility & Business Model, and "next milestone and what do you need" (`inputs/judging-criteria.md`).

> **The scale story in one line:** start where the need is sharpest and demand is predictable (squads that train together), prove people re-order, then copy the squad model university by university, and only then go national with a chilled weekly box, the same model as Simmer Eats.

---

## The four stages at a glance

| | Stage 0: Prove it | Stage 1: Durham | Stage 2: North East unis | Stage 3: National weekly box |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **When** | Now to Week 13 | Months 1–6 after the pitch | Months 6–18 | Months 18–36 |
| **Made where** | Your home kitchen, registered with the council | Hired commercial kitchen (community, church or university kitchen) | Small rented unit or long-term kitchen contract | Bigger unit, or a contract bakery (co-manufacturer) |
| **Sold to** | Friends, one Team Durham squad trial, your MMA club | Team Durham clubs, Durham combat and strength gyms, weekly boxes ordered online | Squads at Newcastle, Northumbria, Sunderland, Teesside, York; club and gym fridges | Anyone in mainland UK, ordering a chilled weekly box online |
| **Delivered by** | You, with a cool bag | You plus a helper: squad drops at training | Weekly van round to campuses, with cool boxes | Next-day chilled courier (dispatched Mon–Wed) |
| **Bars per week** | ~60 | ~400 | ~2,500 | ~10,000 |
| **Move on only when** | 1 paid squad order; 20+ people re-order; taste ≥7/9 | 400 bars/week; ≥60% re-order; lab-backed use-by ≥5 days | 2,500 bars/week across 3+ unis; a positive margin each month | Proven demand outside universities (waitlist from other cities) |

**Why this order works:**
- **Squads first.** A coach orders for 20–30 athletes at once, every week. That's predictable demand, which a fresh product with a short shelf life needs. It's also the cheapest way to deliver: one drop instead of 25.
- **National last.** National delivery is the most expensive step (insulated box, ice packs, courier), so it only pays once the brand and the price are proven.

---

## Stage 0: Prove it (home kitchen, now to Week 13)
**Goal:** prove people will **pay and re-order**, not just say they like it.

**What you do:**
1. **Register your home kitchen** as a food business with Durham County Council. It's free, must be done 28 days before you start selling, and applies to home kitchens and online sales too `[EVIDENCE: R14]`. The council will inspect and give you a food hygiene rating.
2. **Food safety basics:**
   - a Level 2 Food Hygiene certificate (about £10–25 online) `[EVIDENCE: P12]`
   - a written food safety plan using the FSA's free "Safer Food, Better Business" pack
   - public and product liability insurance `[EVIDENCE: P13]`
3. **Allergen controls:** bake the peanut bar last and clean down between bars, and label every bar with its allergens.
4. **Sell in small, real amounts:**
   - a squad taste test with Team Durham, then a paid trial week
   - your MMA club
   - waitlist sign-ups from the landing page
5. **Track:**
   - re-order rate (the most important number)
   - taste scores
   - which flavour sells
   - what price people actually pay

**Capacity:** about 60 bars a week takes roughly 4 hours of baking. `[ASSUMPTION: 6 hours per 100 bars at home]`

## Stage 1: Durham (hired kitchen, months 1–6)
**Goal:** a steady **400 bars a week**, mostly squad orders, with proper labels and a tested use-by date.

**What changes:**
- **Kitchen:**
  - hire a commercial kitchen for two 4-hour sessions a week
  - community kitchens list about **£13–19 an hour** `[EVIDENCE: P30]`
  - ask Durham University and the colleges about kitchen space too `[GAP]`
- **Evidence for the label:**
  - lab nutrition analysis, to replace the calculated numbers
  - a shelf-life test, to support a longer use-by (target 5–7 days instead of 3) `[EVIDENCE: R11–R13]`
- **People:** one student helper for baking and drops. `[ASSUMPTION: about 4 hours per 100 bars at the National Living Wage]`
- **Packaging:** move from greaseproof paper and a sticker to a printed wrapper, using the pack designs in `05-brand`.
- **Sales:**
  - every Team Durham club with a coach
  - Durham combat and strength gyms
  - a weekly box ordered online, delivered by hand around Durham
- **Price:** settle on a price using the waitlist answers and pilot sales (testing £2.00–2.75).

---

## Stage 2: North East universities (months 6–18)
**Goal:** **2,500 bars a week** across 3–5 universities by copying the Durham squad model.

**How you copy it:**
- **A campus ambassador at each university:** a student athlete paid commission to sign up squads and gyms. They know the coaches and run taste tests at training.
- **The same offer everywhere:** a squad taste test, then a weekly squad order, plus individual weekly boxes.
- **The market is big:** over **100,000 student athletes** competed for UK universities in 2021/22 `[EVIDENCE: D4]`.
- **Production:**
  - a small rented unit or a long-term kitchen contract
  - equipment: a commercial convection oven, a **blast chiller** (cools the bars fast, for safety and shelf life) and a heat sealer or flow-wrapper
- **Certification:** **SALSA**, the food safety certificate many retailers and wholesale buyers ask small producers for. Older UK figures were about £450–500 for an audit, and current Irish pricing is €1,250 `[EVIDENCE: P32]`, so get a current UK quote `[GAP]`.
- **Delivery:** a weekly van round to campuses, with cool boxes.

---

## Stage 3: National weekly box (months 18–36)
**Goal:** about **10,000 bars a week**, sold online across mainland UK. This is the Simmer Eats model for recovery bars.

**How it works:**
1. **Order:** customers subscribe to a weekly box of 12 bars, choose flavours, and can pause or skip.
2. **Make:** bake to order, either in your own bigger unit or with a **contract bakery** once volume justifies it. That's usually at 10,000+ bars a week, but get quotes `[ASSUMPTION]`.
3. **Ship chilled:**
   - next-day courier, dispatched Monday to Wednesday so nothing sits in a depot over the weekend `[EVIDENCE: P31 forum advice]`
   - a recyclable insulated box (e.g. wool liners) with ice packs `[EVIDENCE: P31]`
4. **Freezer packs:** a frozen option for people who want to stock up. This cuts waste and makes delivery easier.
5. **Keep squads and gyms:** university and club orders carry on alongside. They're the cheapest channel and the best marketing.

## The money: what the model shows

| Scenario | Stage | Bars/week | Cost per bar | Margin per bar | Weekly contribution after fixed costs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0. Home kitchen | 60 | £1.23 | £1.02 | £56 |
| A | 1. Durham | 400 | £1.97 | £0.28 | £76 |
| A | 2. North East unis | 2,500 | £1.82 | £0.43 | £722 |
| A | 3. National box | 10,000 | £2.15 | **£0.10** | **–£338** (loses money) |
| B | 2. North East unis (£2.50) | 2,500 | £1.82 | £0.68 | £1,347 |
| B | 3. National box (£2.50 + £4.95 delivery fee per box) | 10,000 | £2.15 | **£0.77** | **£6,287** |

*Scenario A: price stays at £2.25 and we pay for delivery. Scenario B: price £2.50 from Stage 2, and national boxes charge £4.95 delivery (Simmer charges about £7 `[EVIDENCE: C40]`). Ingredients average £1.08 a bar at Tesco prices; bulk buying is assumed to cut that by 15–30% as volume grows. Most inputs are assumptions: see the script.*

**Three lessons:**
1. **Squad orders are the profitable core.** One drop serves 25 athletes, so delivery costs pennies per bar.
2. **National delivery only works if the customer pays for it** (as Simmer does) **and the price rises to about £2.50.** At £2.25 with free delivery, the national box loses money.
3. **A small home or hired kitchen won't pay you a wage.** Stage 1 is about proving demand. The money comes from Stage 2 onwards.

## Impact: why this matters beyond sales

### Social impact: real food instead of ultra-processed food
- **Over half the UK adult diet is ultra-processed food:** about **54–57% of energy**, and about **59% for 19–29-year-olds** `[EVIDENCE: S30]`. Most protein and recovery bars are ultra-processed (protein powders, sugar alcohols, sweeteners, syrups).
- **Athletes eat these bars often**, sometimes daily, despite being told to eat whole food (the S&C coach; C7) and preferring whole-food products when they can get them `[EVIDENCE: Carey 2023, 01d]`.
- **Every fettle bar replaces one ultra-processed bar** with eggs, quark and oats. That's the impact you can count.
- **Stay careful:** say "a real-food alternative to ultra-processed bars". Don't claim health outcomes the research can't support for one product.

### Industry impact
- **It challenges a category built on long shelf life:** a fresh, chilled, made-to-order recovery bar. If it works, it shows sports nutrition can be real food at scale.

### Environmental and local
- **Baked to order, so very little food is wasted**, and frozen packs reduce it further.
- **British ingredients:** free-range eggs, oats and dairy.
- **Recyclable insulated packaging** for national delivery (wool and cardboard).

### Numbers to report as you grow
| Measure | How to count it |
|---|---|
| Ultra-processed bars replaced | Bars sold (each replaces one) |
| Squads and clubs switched | Number of squads with a weekly order |
| Waste | Bars baked minus bars sold, as a % |
| Local sourcing | % of ingredient spend with UK suppliers |

## Beyond the first market
1. **More universities:** 100,000+ student athletes nationally `[EVIDENCE: D4]`, reached through the same ambassador model.
2. **Clubs and gyms:** semi-pro and pro football and rugby clubs, combat-sports gyms and CrossFit boxes, through coach and S&C relationships.
3. **Anyone who trains hard and can't always cook**, via the national weekly box.
4. **Later options (only if earlier stages work):** chilled retail (campus shops, gym fridges, then a regional supermarket trial, which needs SALSA or BRC certification), and new flavours or a lower-fibre option for competition day.

## What can go wrong, and how the stages protect you
| Risk | How the plan handles it |
|---|---|
| People like it but don't re-order | Stage 0's gate is re-orders, not compliments. Don't spend on Stage 1 until 20+ people re-order |
| A 3-day use-by is too short | Stage 1 shelf-life test; frozen packs as a backup |
| Delivery costs eat the margin | Squads first (one drop); national box charges delivery; price £2.50 |
| Taste fails against Barebells | Blind taste tests before any spending; iterate the recipe |
| Food safety or allergen incident | Registration, HACCP, Level 2 certificate, allergen order, lab tests, insurance before selling |
| One person can't bake 400+ a week | Hired kitchen and a paid helper at Stage 1; a contract bakery at Stage 3 |
