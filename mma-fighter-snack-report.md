## 1. Executive summary

**The problem.** UK amateur and semi-pro MMA fighters often train twice a day while managing weight:
- 97% of men and 100% of women cut weight `[EVIDENCE: Hillier et al. 2019, B]`.
- Measured carbohydrate intake is ~3.3 g/kg/day against 6–10 g/kg guidance `[EVIDENCE: Bueno et al. 2024, B; Thomas et al. 2016, A]`.
- Quick snacks force a choice between sweetener/polyol "lab" bars and sugar-heavy "natural" bars. **No UK product we audited passes all five fighter tests** (§3), yet all 8 fighters asked for exactly that `[PRIMARY]`.

**The solution.** A fighter-made, portion-fixed, bag-stable snack:
- dried egg white and oats, no sweeteners or sugar alcohols
- sold in portions that match a single or double training day
- sold first through Ewan's gym and his 25k-subscriber MMA YouTube channel.

**The honest weakness.** Several fighters said current options are "fine" `[PRIMARY]`. Today the evidence supports a **strong vitamin with migraine moments**, not a proven migraine:
- double days
- the week of a cut
- after the weigh-in.

The market is small:
- There is **no official UK MMA participant count** `[GAP]`.
- The safest serviceable market to quote is **~£2–3m a year** (top-down). The draft £23m should be dropped (§2).

**The single riskiest assumption:** *"Fighters will pay £2–2.50 per snack, repeatedly, for this instead of what they already eat."*

**How to test it this month (by 20 Nov):**
1. Run the landing page through free channels (YouTube Community, gym QR, WhatsApp).
2. Then ask for **≥20 paid pre-orders** of a 5-pack (£10), collected at the gym once registration and a lab check are in place.
3. Set the bar in advance:
   - Pass: ≥20 paid, and ≥40% of the 8 fighters pre-order.
   - Fail: <10 paid.
   - On a fail, reposition around the post-weigh-in and double-session moments, or around gyms as the buyer.

## 3. Section 1 — The problem and the need (Criterion 1)

### 1a. What fighters need after hard training

| Need | Evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Protein per feed:** ~0.25–0.3 g/kg (≈20–40 g) every 3–4 h; daily 1.4–2.0 g/kg | `[EVIDENCE: Jäger et al. 2017, ISSN, A]`; `[EVIDENCE: Moore 2018 review, PMC6746967, A/B]` | A |
| **Carbohydrate after exhausting training:** ~1.0–1.2 g/kg/h for 4–6 h when the next session is soon (e.g. double days); daily 3–5 g/kg (moderate), 6–10 g/kg (high volume) | `[EVIDENCE: Kerksick et al. 2017, ISSN nutrient timing, A]`; `[EVIDENCE: Thomas, Erdman and Burke 2016, A]` | A |
| **Combat sport specifics:** individualise to weight class and the making-weight phase; avoid chronic under-fuelling | `[EVIDENCE: Reale, Slater and Burke 2017, B (review)]`; `[EVIDENCE: Burke et al. ACSM expert consensus on weight-category sports, A — verify citation]` | A/B |
| **Digestibility before rolling or sparring:** no combat-specific trial found. General exercise-GI literature links high fibre, fat, FODMAPs and polyols to symptoms | `[EVIDENCE: Lenhart and Chey 2017, A]`; `[GAP: searched "grappling/sparring AND gastrointestinal"; no study]` | A / GAP |

**What a 50 g snack can realistically do** (70 kg fighter) `[ASSUMPTION, arithmetic]`:
- **Protein:** 15 g protein = 0.21 g/kg, about two-thirds of a full feed.
- **Carbohydrate:** 22.5 g = 0.32 g/kg, about a quarter to a third of one hour's target on a double day.
- **So it bridges; it is not the whole answer.** That is fine and honest. A "Double Session" pack of two portions gets close to a full protein feed (30 g = 0.43 g/kg).

### 1b. How weight management shapes snacking

- **Cutting is near-universal.**
  - 97.2% of men and 100% of women cut weight `[EVIDENCE: Hillier et al. 2019, B]`.
  - Pros lost more in the final week than amateurs: 5.9% vs 4.2% of body mass (men) `[EVIDENCE: Hillier et al. 2019, B]`.
- **UK data.** Seven UK MMA athletes lost 8.0 ± 1.8% of body mass in the week before weigh-in, then regained 11.7 ± 4.7% in ~32 h `[EVIDENCE: Matthews and Nicholas 2017, B]`.
- **Harms.** A UK case study documented relative energy deficiency, dehydration and acute kidney injury from extreme weight making `[EVIDENCE: Kasper et al. 2019, B — single case]`. See also `[EVIDENCE: Barley, Chapman and Abbiss 2019, B]`.
- **Who advises.** Coaches were the most-cited influence (men 29.3%, women 48.1%) `[EVIDENCE: Hillier et al. 2019, B]`.
  - **Correct the claim:** the data shows coaches lead. It does *not* show dietitians are never used. Say "most fighters take nutrition advice from coaches".
- **Implication for everyday snacking** `[ASSUMPTION — test in interviews]`:
  - Out of camp, fighters want to stay near fight weight, so they ration energy-dense snacks.
  - In camp, the daily question is portion control, which favours **fixed, known portions** over "eat until full" options.
  - Fighters said low sugar and low saturated fat matter for weight management `[PRIMARY]`.

### 1c. Under-fuelling evidence

- **Carbohydrate.** 3.3 ± 1.6 (amateur) and 3.3 ± 1.4 (pro) g/kg/day carbohydrate, with an estimated energy deficit, in 24 Brazilian MMA fighters outside competition `[EVIDENCE: Bueno et al. 2024, B — non-UK, small n, 24-h recalls under-report]`.
  - **Do not blend** this with UK data. Say "a 2024 study of MMA fighters".
- **REDs.** It affects male and female athletes, and weight-class sports are a named risk group `[EVIDENCE: Mountjoy et al. 2023, IOC consensus, A]`.
- `[GAP]`: no UK prevalence of low energy availability in amateur MMA. Searched "low energy availability" AND MMA/"combat sport" AND UK.

### 1d. Digestion

- **Polyols.** They cause dose-dependent GI symptoms (bloating, laxation) `[EVIDENCE: Lenhart and Chey 2017, A]`. Several UK bars carry the "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects" statement `[EVIDENCE: pack and retailer listings, 6 Oct 2026, C]`.
- **Exercise and the gut.** FODMAPs, including inulin/chicory fibre, are linked to exercise-related gut symptoms `[EVIDENCE: Monash FODMAP research update, C]`. **UFC's Main Event uses chicory and cassava-root fibre** `[EVIDENCE: SupplySide FBJ 2026, C]`.
- **Fighter-specific evidence:** none. No study of eating before or after grappling was found `[GAP]`. **This is a primary-research opportunity:** ask "When did food last make a session worse?" and log symptoms in the taste test.

### 1e. Anti-doping

- UKAD says no supplement is guaranteed free of prohibited substances, and athletes are strictly liable `[EVIDENCE: UKAD, B]`.
- **Relevance is limited** `[ASSUMPTION]`:
  - Most UK amateur MMA is not under a UKAD testing programme. MMA is not an Olympic sport, and testing in UK promotions is patchy `[GAP: no published UK MMA testing programme found]`.
  - Fighters who also compete in **BJJ (IBJJF), judo, boxing or wrestling** can be tested.
- **Use it lightly:** "made from foods, not supplement powders". **Never** say "drug-free", "clean" or "safe for tested athletes" without batch testing.

### 1f. Migraine or vitamin? An honest verdict

| For "migraine" | For "vitamin" |
|---|---|
| 8/8 fighters named the same unmet combination `[PRIMARY]` | Some said current options are "fine" and the idea "would be nice" `[PRIMARY]` |
| Some would trade taste for nutrition, a sign of a functional job `[PRIMARY]` | No one has yet paid, pre-ordered or described a costly workaround |
| Documented weight-making and carb-deficit context `[B]` | The sport nutrition professor saw "no obvious dissatisfaction" with existing options `[PRIMARY]` |
| 0 of 22 UK products pass all 5 tests (§3) `[C]` | A gap in a matrix is not proof of demand |

**Verdict.** Today this is a **vitamin with specific migraine moments** `[ASSUMPTION]`. Lead the pitch with those moments, not a generic "after training":

1. **Double-session days in camp.** The window between sessions is too short to cook. This is Ewan's own origin story.
2. **Fight week and post-weigh-in.** Carbohydrate is needed, but fighters want known, controlled portions.
3. **Travel to shows or away sessions.** Nothing suitable is sold at the venue or service station.
4. **Late evening after the second session**, when the alternative is takeaway.

**Test:** in the 8 follow-ups (§8d), ask for the *last time* each moment happened and what they did. If ≥5/8 describe a workaround they dislike (skipping food, a meal deal, a bar that upset their stomach), the migraine claim is defensible for that moment.

**So what for the pitch:** don't say "fighters can't fuel". Say **"in fight camp, on double days, there's nothing to grab that fits how fighters eat"**, and show the 8/8 result plus the pre-order test.
**Confidence:** Medium on the need (science), Low–Medium on the pain (customers).
**Evidence grade mix:** A ×5, B ×6, C ×3, PRIMARY ×4, GAP ×3.

**Top-down cross-check**
- UK grocery protein bars, drinks and powders ≈ £225m `[EVIDENCE: Circana 2025 via trade press, C]`. The 6 Oct report cited £230m for bars + shakes, so the figures differ by definition.
- If combat-sport trainers buy ~1% of that, the combat-sport share is £2.3m. If MMA is ~10–15% of that share, the MMA share is ~£0.3m.
- That gives a **top-down combat-sport serviceable market of ~£2–3m**, the same order as the draft's £2.6m.

**Reconciling £23m vs £2.6m** `[ASSUMPTION]`:
- The £23m figure equals ~25% of a £92m TAM. It reads as "all combat sports, at ~£200/yr each", not MMA.
- The £2.6m figure is a top-down slice of protein-snack spending.
- Both rest on unverified participant counts.
- The bottom-up MMA SAM (£2–9m) overlaps the top-down figure only at its low end.

**Safest figure to say to judges** `[ASSUMPTION — recommended]`:
> "There's no official count of UK MMA fighters, so we sized it both ways. Top-down, the serviceable market is about **£2–3 million** a year. Our three-year goal is **~£40k**: about 300 regular customers, roughly 360 snacks a week. Boxing, BJJ and women who lift take it into the tens of millions."

This is modest, defensible, and shows you understand the sizing.

  - ≤ 0.70 also limits most moulds and yeasts.
  - ≤ 0.60 stops microbial growth.
- **Target for a 1–3-month ambient bite:** aw ≤ 0.65 `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- v13 at ~16% moisture **may or may not** reach this. Moisture % does not predict aw reliably `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- **Egg white must be fully cooked** (Salmonella). Bake to a validated core temperature `[ASSUMPTION — confirm with Durham EHO]`.

## 10. Section 8 — Testing the market

### 8a. Can a landing page with Meta ads produce real evidence?

**What a smoke test proves:**
- People in a defined audience **click** on a message.
- They **give an email**.
- They **state** pre-order intent and a price.

**What it can't prove:**
- That they will **pay**.
- That they will **reorder**.
- That they will **like the taste**.

Sign-ups are a cheap signal of interest. **Paid pre-orders and reorders are evidence of demand.** Treat the landing page as rung 3 of 6 (§8c).

**Indicative UK benchmarks** (directional; third-party datasets, not Meta's own figures):

| Metric | Figure | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPC, GB "Fitness & Training Centers" | ~0.75 average (12 months to Dec 2025); range 0.18–1.92 | Superads (currency as displayed; check) | C |
| CTR, GB fitness | ~0.84% (Oct 2024–Apr 2025) vs 1.72% global | Superads | C |
| Fitness CPC / CTR / conversion (global, 2025) | $1.90 / 1.01% / 14.3% | TrendTrack | C (non-UK) |
| Health and wellness CPM (2025) | $20.70, +38% | Influee | C (non-UK) |

- Interests: Mixed martial arts, UFC, Brazilian jiu-jitsu, Muay Thai, Boxing, Wrestling, Cage Warriors.
- Optionally narrow by "Fitness and wellness".
- **Lookalikes only after ≥100 sign-ups**, and note Meta's 2025 limits on health-related custom audiences `[EVIDENCE: agency summaries of Meta policy, C]`.

**Meta and UK pitfalls:**
- **Age.** Dietary/weight-loss products must target 18+. Protein food is generally exempt, but **target 18+ anyway** `[EVIDENCE: Meta Health & Wellness policy, D]`.

A study of over three hundred MMA athletes found ninety-seven percent of men and every woman cut weight before fights, and most of their advice comes from coaches. Another study found fighters eat around three grams of carbohydrate per kilo of body weight a day, when guidelines for heavy training say six to ten.

**[Customer, 45 s]** I spoke to eight fighters at my gym. Every one asked for the same thing after training: something natural, high in protein, high in carbs and low in sugar. I'm finding the moments where it matters: double-session days, fight week, and the drive home after training late.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Summarise landing-page sign-ups exported as CSV (standard library only).

Usage:
    python3 analyse_signups.py signups.csv
    python3 analyse_signups.py signups.csv --visits youtube=420 poster=60 meta=900

--visits gives page visits per utm_source (from your hosting or Meta stats) so
the script can print visit -> sign-up conversion. Without it, only sign-up
counts per source are shown.

Column names are matched loosely, so a Google Forms export with question text
as headers works as long as each header contains one of the keywords in
COLUMN_KEYWORDS below.
"""
import argparse
import csv
import re
import statistics
import sys
from collections import Counter

COLUMN_KEYWORDS = {
    "email": ["email"],
    "role": ["role", "fits you best", "compete"],
    "current_snack": ["current_snack", "eat after training"],
    "struggle": ["struggle"],
    "preorder": ["preorder", "pre-order"],
    "price_too_cheap": ["too_cheap", "doubt the quality"],
    "price_bargain": ["bargain"],
    "price_expensive": ["price_expensive", "getting expensive"],
    "price_too_expensive": ["too_expensive", "too expensive"],
    "utm_source": ["utm_source"],
    "utm_medium": ["utm_medium"],
    "utm_campaign": ["utm_campaign"],
    "landing_variant": ["landing_variant", "variant"],
}

# Simple keyword themes for free-text answers. Extend after reading the raw answers.
SNACK_THEMES = {
    "protein bar": ["bar", "grenade", "barebells", "fulfil", "phd"],
    "shake": ["shake", "whey", "protein powder"],
    "milk / yoghurt": ["milk", "yoghurt", "yogurt", "skyr", "quark", "yazoo", "arla"],
    "fruit": ["banana", "fruit", "apple"],
    "meal": ["meal", "chicken", "rice", "pasta", "sandwich", "wrap"],
    "nothing": ["nothing", "don't", "dont", "skip", "none"],
    "fast food / shop": ["meal deal", "greggs", "mcdonald", "takeaway", "crisps"],
}
STRUGGLE_THEMES = {
    "time / prep": ["time", "prep", "cook", "busy"],
    "weight cut / making weight": ["weight", "cut", "cutting", "scales"],
    "hunger": ["hungry", "hunger", "appetite"],
    "energy / tired": ["energy", "tired", "fatigue", "flat"],
    "cost": ["cost", "money", "expensive", "price"],
    "stomach / digestion": ["stomach", "bloat", "gut", "digest", "sick"],
    "carbs": ["carb"],
    "sugar / sweeteners": ["sugar", "sweetener"],
}


def find_columns(headers):
    mapping = {}
    for key, words in COLUMN_KEYWORDS.items():
        for h in headers:
            hl = h.lower()
            if any(w in hl for w in words) and h not in mapping.values():
                mapping[key] = h
                break
    return mapping


def pct(n, d):
    return f"{100.0 * n / d:5.1f}%" if d else "  n/a"


def to_price(value):
    if value is None:
        return None
    m = re.search(r"\d+(?:\.\d+)?", str(value).replace(",", "."))
    if not m:
        return None
    v = float(m.group())
    return v if 0 < v <= 20 else None


def theme_counts(answers, themes):
    counts = Counter()
    for a in answers:
        al = a.lower()
        hit = False
        for theme, words in themes.items():
            if any(w in al for w in words):
                counts[theme] += 1
                hit = True
        if not hit:
            counts["other / unclear"] += 1
    return counts


def crossing(grid, rising, falling):
    """First price where the rising curve meets or passes the falling curve."""
    for p, r, f in zip(grid, rising, falling):
        if r >= f:
            return p
    return None


def van_westendorp(rows):
    """Return (n, medians, OPP, IDP, PMC, PME) from complete, consistent answers."""
    keys = ["price_too_cheap", "price_bargain", "price_expensive", "price_too_expensive"]
    sets = []
    for r in rows:
        vals = [to_price(r.get(k)) for k in keys]
        if None not in vals and vals == sorted(vals):
            sets.append(vals)
    n = len(sets)
    if n == 0:
        return 0, None, None, None, None, None
    medians = [statistics.median(s[i] for s in sets) for i in range(4)]
    grid = [round(0.05 * i, 2) for i in range(1, 201)]  # £0.05 to £10.00
    too_cheap = [sum(s[0] >= p for s in sets) / n for p in grid]
    cheap = [sum(s[1] >= p for s in sets) / n for p in grid]
    expensive = [sum(s[2] <= p for s in sets) / n for p in grid]
    too_exp = [sum(s[3] <= p for s in sets) / n for p in grid]
    opp = crossing(grid, too_exp, too_cheap)
    idp = crossing(grid, expensive, cheap)
    pmc = crossing(grid, [1 - c for c in cheap], too_cheap)
    pme = crossing(grid, too_exp, [1 - e for e in expensive])
    return n, medians, opp, idp, pmc, pme


def main():
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser(description=__doc__, formatter_class=argparse.RawDescriptionHelpFormatter)
    ap.add_argument("csv_file")
    ap.add_argument("--visits", nargs="*", default=[], help="source=visits pairs, e.g. youtube=420")
    args = ap.parse_args()

    with open(args.csv_file, newline="", encoding="utf-8-sig") as f:
        reader = csv.DictReader(f)
        headers = reader.fieldnames or []
        cols = find_columns(headers)
        raw = list(reader)

    if "email" not in cols:
        sys.exit("No email column found. Headers seen: " + ", ".join(headers))

    # Normalise rows to our keys and drop duplicate emails (keep the first).
    rows, seen = [], set()
    for r in raw:
        row = {k: (r.get(h) or "").strip() for k, h in cols.items()}
        email = row["email"].lower()
        if not email or email in seen:
            continue
        seen.add(email)
        rows.append(row)
    n = len(rows)
    print(f"Sign-ups (unique emails): {n}   (rows in file: {len(raw)})")
    missing = [k for k in COLUMN_KEYWORDS if k not in cols]
    if missing:
        print("Columns not found (skipped):", ", ".join(missing))
    if n == 0:
        return

    print("\n== Sign-ups by source ==")
    visits = {}
    for pair in args.visits:
        if "=" in pair:
            k, v = pair.split("=", 1)
            try:
                visits[k.strip().lower()] = int(v)
            except ValueError:
                print(f"Ignoring bad --visits value: {pair}")
    by_source = Counter((r.get("utm_source") or "direct").lower() for r in rows)
    print(f"{'source':<18}{'sign-ups':>9}{'share':>8}{'visits':>8}{'conv.':>8}")
    for src, c in by_source.most_common():
        v = visits.get(src)
        conv = pct(c, v) if v else "  n/a"
        print(f"{src:<18}{c:>9}{pct(c, n):>8}{(v or ''):>8}{conv:>8}")

    if any(r.get("utm_campaign") for r in rows):
        print("\n== Sign-ups by campaign ==")
        for camp, c in Counter(r.get("utm_campaign") or "(none)" for r in rows).most_common():
            print(f"{camp:<30}{c:>5}")

    if any(r.get("landing_variant") for r in rows):
        print("\n== Sign-ups by landing-page variant ==")
        for var, c in Counter(r.get("landing_variant") or "(none)" for r in rows).most_common():
            print(f"{var:<10}{c:>5}")

    print("\n== Compete / train / watch ==")
    roles = Counter((r.get("role") or "no answer").lower() for r in rows)
    for role, c in roles.most_common():
        print(f"{role:<12}{c:>5}{pct(c, n):>8}")
    trainers = [r for r in rows if (r.get("role") or "").lower() in ("compete", "train")]

    print("\n== Pre-order intent: 5-pack at £10 ==")
    for label, group in (("everyone", rows), ("compete + train only", trainers)):
        po = Counter((r.get("preorder") or "no answer").lower() for r in group)
        g = len(group)
        parts = "  ".join(f"{k}: {po.get(k, 0)} ({pct(po.get(k, 0), g).strip()})"
                          for k in ("yes", "maybe", "no", "no answer"))
        print(f"{label:<22} n={g:<4} {parts}")

    for title, key, themes in (("Current post-training food", "current_snack", SNACK_THEMES),
                               ("Biggest food struggle in camp", "struggle", STRUGGLE_THEMES)):
        answers = [r[key] for r in rows if r.get(key)]
        print(f"\n== {title} (answered: {len(answers)}) ==")
        for theme, c in theme_counts(answers, themes).most_common():
            print(f"{theme:<28}{c:>5}{pct(c, len(answers)):>8}")

    print("\n== Price (Van Westendorp, £ per snack) ==")
    vw_n, medians, opp, idp, pmc, pme = van_westendorp(rows)
    if vw_n == 0:
        print("No complete, consistent price answers yet.")
    else:
        labels = ["too cheap", "bargain", "getting expensive", "too expensive"]
        print(f"Complete, consistent answers: {vw_n}")
        for lab, m in zip(labels, medians):
            print(f"  median '{lab}': £{m:.2f}")
        fmt = lambda x: f"£{x:.2f}" if x is not None else "n/a"
