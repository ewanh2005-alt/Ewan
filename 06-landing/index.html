The trade-off

Low sugar usually means a lab bar.

The low-sugar bars get there with protein isolates and sugar alcohols. The "natural" bars use real food, and carry about 10–28 g of sugar. We wanted both.

Protein bars

Low sugar, long ingredient lists

Most get under 2 g sugar using protein isolates or blends plus polyols (sugar alcohols) or sweeteners. Some carry a laxative warning.

"Natural" & recovery bars

Real ingredients, lots of sugar

Dates, syrups and dried fruit hold them together, so 10–28 g of sugar per bar is normal.

fettle

Baked, not pressed

Eggs and milk bind and set the bar in the oven, so it doesn't need sticky sugars to hold together.

Low sugar

About 3 g per 90 g bar, from the banana and the milk. No honey, no syrup, no dates, no sweeteners.

Protein and carbs

About 14 g protein from eggs, milk and peanuts, and 28 g carbs, mostly from oats. A source of protein: protein contributes to a growth in muscle mass. Enjoy as part of a varied, balanced diet and a healthy lifestyle.

When to eat it

After training, when a proper meal is still a way off. It's baked fresh, so keep it in the fridge and eat it within a few days, or freeze it.

Contains peanuts, milk, egg and oats (gluten). Not suitable before or during exercise. Not a meal replacement.

FAQ

Questions

What allergens does it contain?

Cocoa & Peanut contains peanuts, milk, egg and oats (a cereal containing gluten). Raspberry & Almond contains almonds, milk, egg and oats. Toasted Oat & Cinnamon contains milk, egg and oats. All are made in a kitchen that handles peanuts and almonds. Not suitable for vegans, or anyone with these allergies.

Where is it made?

In small batches in [Durham / North East England, PLACEHOLDER: name the registered kitchen]. We're registered with [council name, PLACEHOLDER] as a food business.

How much will it cost?

We're planning £2.75 a bar, with discounts on 4-packs and boxes for clubs. Tell us what you'd pay in the form below. It genuinely shapes the price.

When does it launch?

We're running small taste tests and pilots with local gyms and clubs this term. Waitlist members get the first batch and a say in the flavours.

Is it a recovery supplement?

No. It's food: a snack for after training, with protein and carbohydrate. We don't claim it does anything a balanced diet doesn't.

Is it tested for banned substances (e.g. Informed Sport)?

Not yet. If you're a drug-tested athlete, please check with your support team before eating any product that isn't batch-tested.

How is it so low in sugar without sweeteners?

It's baked, not pressed. Most "natural" bars are held together by dates, syrup or honey. Ours is set by eggs and milk in the oven, so the only sugar is the little that comes from the banana and the milk: about 3 g per bar. Its carbs come mostly from oats.

How long does it keep?

It's a fresh bake with real eggs and milk, so keep it in the fridge and eat it by the use-by date on the label (a few days). You can freeze it on the day you buy it.

Is it high protein?

No, and we won't call it that. It has about 14 g protein per bar from eggs, milk and peanuts. Lab bars reach 20 g using protein isolates. We chose kitchen ingredients and low sugar instead.
