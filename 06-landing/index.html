The problem

After training, every option makes you give something up.

You need protein and carbs to recover. When you can't sit down to a proper meal, this is what's left.

Meal prep

Right food, hard to keep up

Chicken and rice works, but it means cooking ahead and carrying a tub around, and it falls apart on busy weeks.

Fruit

Quick, but no protein

A banana is real food and fast carbs, with about 1 g of protein.

Protein bars

Protein, plus a long label

Most reach 20 g with protein powder, sweeteners and sugar alcohols. Some carry a laxative warning.

"Natural" bars

Real food, mostly sugar

Dates, syrups and dried fruit hold them together: 10–28 g sugar and around 12 g protein is normal.

80%

of Canadian university athletes say their schedule limits their ability to cook (Morassutti, York University, 2024).

Carbs

are what team-sport athletes miss: most hit protein targets but fall short on carbohydrate (Jenner et al., 2019, 21 studies).

Tastier

Athletes rate everyday food tastier, cheaper and safer than sports foods, which win only on convenience (Forsyth & Mantzioris, 2023).

How it works

Baked fresh, like a fresh-meal delivery.

Real eggs and quark don't last for months on a shelf, so we bake to order, the same way fresh-meal services do.

Order

FAQ

Questions

Why does it need to be kept in the fridge?

It's a fresh bake made with real eggs and quark, with no preservatives. Like a fresh meal, it's chilled with a use-by date. You can freeze it on the day it arrives and defrost it overnight in the fridge.

How long does it keep?

For our test bakes, we're using a 3-day use-by while we gather evidence for a longer one. The date will always be on the label.

Where does the protein come from?

Egg whites, eggs, quark (a soft cheese) and oat bran. No whey, soy or pea protein powder.

Why is there some sugar?

Half a teaspoon of honey (or a little banana in Banana Bread) adds a touch of sweetness and quick energy. Each bar has about 5.5 g of sugar, around half of it the natural sugar in quark and oats.

What allergens does it contain?

All bars contain egg, milk and oats (a cereal containing gluten). Chocolate Peanut Butter also contains peanuts, and all bars are made in a kitchen that handles peanuts. Not suitable for vegans or anyone with these allergies.

How much will it cost?

We're testing £2.00–2.75 a bar, with weekly boxes and squad orders cheaper per bar. Tell us what you'd pay in the form below: it genuinely shapes the price.

Where do you deliver?

Durham to start, hand-delivered and chilled. If you're further away, join the waitlist and tell us where; it helps us plan.

Is it tested for banned substances (e.g. Informed Sport)?

Not yet. If you're a drug-tested athlete, check with your support team before eating any product that isn't batch-tested.

Is it a supplement or meal replacement?

No. It's food: a snack for after training with protein and carbohydrate. We don't claim it does anything a balanced diet doesn't.
