- **Moment:** **after heavy training** (often double sessions).

**Evidence already gathered (build on it; don't repeat it):**

- 3 experts:
  - Team Durham S&C coach: athletes struggled to eat boxes of Grenade bars.

  - Sport and exercise nutrition professor: bars are "dense" and hard to digest; carbs get a bad rep; a snack that keeps in a bag all day is the way to go; uni athletes are a good target.

  - UK supermarket protein bars, drinks and powders worth ~£225m (Circana via Asian Trader, 2025).

## 3. RESEARCH DESIGN (state this at the top of your report)
- **Approach:** mixed-methods desk research, synthesised with the founder's primary interview data; explicitly **abductive**: form the best explanation, then state how to test it.
- **Triangulation:** every key claim (market size, the need, the gap) should rest on **at least 2 independent source types** (e.g. peer-reviewed + industry data; or industry data + customer interviews). Mark claims with a single source as such.
- **Evidence grading:** give every claim a grade:
  - **A:** peer-reviewed systematic review, meta-analysis or position stand
  - **B:** peer-reviewed primary study, or official statistics (ONS, Sport England, FSA)
  - **C:** reputable industry data (Mintel, Circana, Kantar, Euromonitor) or quality trade press
  - **D:** company or brand statements, sponsorship announcements, marketing
  - **E:** anecdote, forums, social media, single interviews
- **Claim tags:** `[EVIDENCE: ref, grade]` / `[ASSUMPTION]` / `[GAP]`.
- **Geography:** UK first. Label US or global data and never blend it into a UK figure.
- **Recency:** prefer 2022–2026. Justify older sources (e.g. landmark studies).
- **Reflexivity:** note any bias in the founder's data. For example, the interviews were recruited from his own network, there are few MMA fighters, and YouTube polls are self-selected.

## 4. RESEARCH QUESTIONS

### B. The need (the science and the lived problem)
- **B1.** Post-exercise carbohydrate and protein recommendations, and how they apply to **intermittent, high-intensity, multi-session** combat training (ISSN, ACSM/AND/DC, IOC).
- **B2.** **Weight management in MMA:** prevalence, magnitude and methods of rapid weight loss; chronic "staying lean"; the role of coaches vs practitioners. Health and performance consequences (e.g. repeat-effort impairment with large acute losses).
- **B3.** **Low energy and carbohydrate availability (REDs)** in combat athletes, including males; evidence of chronic under-fuelling outside camp.
- **B4.** **GI tolerance:**
  - GI symptoms in athletes.
  - Polyols (maltitol, sorbitol) and GI effects.
  - The GB labelling rule for >10% added polyols.
  - Any grappling-specific evidence; if none, say so and call it a primary-research opportunity.
- **B5.** **Supplement contamination and anti-doping:**
  - UKAD's strict liability.
  - Informed Sport.
  - Fighters' attitudes.
  - Does "food-first" reduce perceived risk? (Don't overclaim.)
- **B6.** **The status quo:** what UK fighters eat after training today, and why. Separate peer-reviewed findings from anecdote (fighter interviews, nutritionist content, podcasts, YouTube).

### H. Hypotheses to test (from a FICTIONAL practice set: NOT evidence)
These came from 40 imagined fighters Ewan used for practice. **They are not findings.** For each one, use secondary evidence to rate it **supported / contradicted / unknown** (with grades), then write **one Mom Test question** to test it with real fighters.
- **H1:** fighters doing double sessions around work lack time and a portable snack.
- **H2:** stomach comfort around sparring and rolling matters; sugar alcohols get blamed for bloating.
- **H3:** exact, trustworthy macros matter for making weight.
- **H4:** tested fighters worry about supplement contamination and prefer real food (likely a small segment).
- **H5:** price (£2–2.50) puts off students and novices.
- **H6:** a meaningful share of fighters don't need this at all (happy with Grenade, bananas and milk, home cooking). How big is that share?
- **H7:** adjacent needs (nutrition advice and content, shakes, post-weigh-in food) may matter more than a bar.
- **H8:** gyms want a product to stock with a margin.
- **H9:** dietary needs (dairy-free, halal, no artificial sweeteners) are a reason to choose this bar.
- **H10:** gyms need an ambient (no-fridge) product; chilled won't work.
- **H11:** fighters trust other fighters and YouTubers more than brands; creator-led distribution works in this niche.
- **H12:** not every fighter cuts; heavier divisions want more calories. Target lighter divisions and fighters in camp first.
- **H13:** women fighters feel underserved by fight-nutrition brands.
