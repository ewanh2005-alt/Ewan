### 1c. Recommendation: **fettle**
- **Why:** it's short, warm and ownable. It suggests "back in good shape" without making a health claim, and it gives you a North East founder story (Durham) judges will remember. It works for a rugby forward and a gym-goer alike.
- **Before using it publicly (you, ~30 min):**
  1. Search [UK IPO trade marks](https://trademarks.ipo.gov.uk/) for "FETTLE" in classes **29, 30, 5 and 32**.
  2. Search [Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/) for "fettle".
  3. Check eatfettle.co.uk on a registrar.
- **What would change my mind:** an existing FETTLE mark in classes 29/30, or a UK food or fitness brand already trading as Fettle. Fallback: **Afta**.
- **Claims note:** a tagline like "Back in fettle" could be read as an implied recovery claim. Keep the name, but use the taglines in §2 that describe the *occasion*, not an *effect*, until Trading Standards confirms.

## 2. Strategy

**Positioning statement**
> For people who train hard and can't always cook after a session, **fettle** (working name) is the fresh-baked recovery bar with ~20 g protein and ~30 g carbs from eggs, quark and oats: no protein powder, no sweeteners, no sugar alcohols. *(Updated 8 Oct for the final test bars in `04d-recovery-bars.md`.)*

**Brand promise:** *Sugar like a protein bar. Ingredients like home baking.*

**Three values**
1. **Honest:** we show every number and every ingredient, and we never overclaim.
2. **Real:** food you'd recognise in a kitchen.
3. **Grounded:** for people who train, not just athletes on podiums.

**Tagline options**
1. **Real food for after training.** *(recommended: safest and clearest)*
2. Low sugar. Real food. After training.
3. 20 g protein. Eggs, quark, oats. Nothing you can't read.
4. Made for the walk home. *(occasion-led)*
5. Back in fettle. *(needs a Trading Standards check: implied effect)*

### Tone of voice
Plain, warm, a bit dry. Northern, not jokey. Numbers first, adjectives last.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "20 g protein from eggs, quark and oats. Baked this week." | "Unleash your gains with our clean superfood fuel!" |
| "For after training, when dinner's still an hour away." | "Speeds recovery and reduces muscle soreness." (illegal claim) |
| "8 g sugar, from honey and milk. We tell you where it comes from." | Overstating protein vs lab bars, or "guilt-free" |
| "Whether you're on the first XV or you went to the gym before a lecture." | "For elite athletes only." / "Beast mode." |
| "No powders, no sweeteners, no syrups." | "Other bars are full of chemicals." (disparaging, misleading) |

### 3b. Typography (Google Fonts)
- **Headings:** *Archivo* 700–800, tight tracking (−1%). Sporty but not shouty.
- **Body and UI:** *Inter* 400/500/600.
- **Numbers (macros):** Archivo 800 with tabular figures. The numbers are the hero.
- The logo is drawn with strokes, not typed, so it never depends on a font.

### 3c. Logo
- **Wordmark:** lowercase monoline "fettle" with round stroke ends: friendly, simple, legible at small sizes. Files: `logo-wordmark-light.svg` / `-dark.svg`.
- **Mark:** one bar scored into **two halves** (Ember + Ink). It is the product's portion idea: half after a light session, whole after a hard one. Files: `logo-mark-*.svg`.
- **Lockup:** mark + wordmark, `logo-lockup-*.svg`.
- Clear space = the height of the mark on all sides. Minimum wordmark width 72 px on screen / 18 mm in print.
- Generated by `build_logos.py`. Edit the script, not the SVGs.

### 3d. Iconography
Monoline, 2 px stroke at 24 px, round caps and joins, matching the wordmark. Simple literal objects (oat, egg, milk bottle, clock, kit bag). No lightning bolts, flames or muscles.

### 3e. Photography and illustration
- **Photography:** real people straight after training (muddy boots, gym bag, bike in a hallway), daylight, unposed. The bar is shown **broken in half** to reveal real oats and dates. Ingredients shot raw on oat-coloured surfaces.
- **Avoid:** oiled physiques, stock "fitness models", dark gym neon, splashes and explosions.
- **Illustration (if needed):** flat shapes in Oat / Ink / Ember only.

## 4. Packaging concept
See `packaging-front-*.svg` and `packaging-back-*.svg` (one pair per flavour; `packaging-front.svg`/`packaging-back.svg` = Chocolate Peanut Butter) (generated from the Phase 4 numbers by `build_packaging.py`).

**Front-of-pack hierarchy:**
1. Brand (fettle)
2. Flavour (Chocolate Peanut Butter · Vanilla · Banana Bread), with the flavour colour band (Cocoa `#5A3A29` · Honey `#8A5A0B` · Ember `#B93D0B`)
3. Key numbers: **protein · carbs · sugar** per bar (e.g. 21 g · 28 g · 8 g)
4. "No protein powder · No sweeteners" + badge **HIGH PROTEIN** (no "low sugar": ~5–7 g/100 g)
5. "Real-food recovery bar · Baked fresh" and **KEEP REFRIGERATED**
6. Net weight (approx. 106–126 g, estimated until test bakes are weighed)

**Back of pack:** full ingredients with **allergens in bold**; UK nutrition table per 100 g and per bar with %RI; how-to-eat guidance; "High protein." with the authorised protein claim and balanced-diet statement; allergy advice; **keep refrigerated, use-by date**, home-freezing advice; lot; and **placeholders** for business name and UK address (legally required).

**Claims deliberately left off pack:**
- "High fibre": v4 doesn't qualify (5.4–5.7 g/100 g).
- "No added sugar": the banana adds sweetness, so it's not safe to claim.
- The carb recovery claim: needs long mandatory wording; website only for now.
- "Natural", "whole food", "clean".
