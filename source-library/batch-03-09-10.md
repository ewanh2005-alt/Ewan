## Batch 3: UPF concern and sweetener scepticism (9 of 50; A 2 · B 3 · C 4)

**What it establishes**
- **UPF concern is mainstream and stable.** It is UK consumers' **#2 food concern after price**, at **~76–78%**, April 2024 to March 2025 (FSA tracker, B3-001/002). 60% of shoppers *say* they cut UPF intake (GlobalData, B3-004). 73% know the term and 58% say it affects their choices, **but most can't identify UPFs** (B3-003). So the ingredient list has to *look* obviously real.
- **Sweeteners:** WHO (2023) conditionally recommends **against non-sugar sweeteners for weight control** (B3-006). **Polyols are outside that guideline.** Keep "no artificial sweeteners" and "no sugar alcohols" as two separate statements.

**Corrections**
- **The "documentary" was Channel 4's *Joe Wicks: Licensed to Kill* (6 Oct 2025)**, and trade press says its effect on Grenade's −15.9% is **contested**. Grenade blames "normalisation" after 2023, and bars were struggling before it aired (B3-007/008). **Never say "Grenade fell because of a UPF documentary."** Say: "Protein bars came under public scrutiny in 2025, and the market leader's volumes fell 15.9%."
- The founder brief's "78% (June 2026)" isn't matched to a specific FSA release yet. Use **"~78% (FSA, March 2025)"**.

**Steelman:** experts called the documentary "scaremongering" (B3-008). The UPF evidence is mostly observational and low certainty (Lane et al. 2024, B3-005). **Ride the consumer trend; don't make health claims or disparage rivals.**

## Batch 9: Post-exercise and multi-session nutrition (10 of 50; A 6 · B 3 · C 1)

**What it establishes**
- **Between sessions, carbohydrate is the priority.** Guidance is ~1.0–1.2 g/kg/h over the first 4 h, **starting immediately**, since a 2 h delay lowers glycogen (B9-001, B9-008). A meta-analysis found **adding protein doesn't clearly speed glycogen refilling** (B9-007).
- Twice-daily trainers should eat **soon after session 1** (B9-009).
- **Fighters under-eat carbs, not protein:** MMA ~3.3 g/kg/day (B9-004); combat-sport SR ~4.1 g/kg/day (17 studies, n = 500; B9-005). The guideline range is 3–10 g/kg/day, scaling with load (B9-003).
- Protein per feed is ~0.25 g/kg (≈17–20 g for a 70–80 kg fighter) (B9-002). **v13's 15 g is a partial feed.**

**Implication for the product (a real insight)**
For the *between-sessions* job, the evidence favours a **carb-forward** snack. v13 is ~15 g protein / 22.5 g carbs, which leans protein. Consider:
- **(a)** a 2-bar "session pack" (30 g protein / 45 g carbs); or
- **(b)** a higher-carb variant (e.g. 10 g protein / 30 g carbs) for between sessions, keeping v13 for after the last session.

**Test it in fighter interviews:** "What do you eat between sessions vs after the last one?"

**Correction:** the report's "6–10 g/kg/day for heavy training" band needs checking against the ACSM table. The search confirmed "3–10 (up to 12)" overall. Until checked, say **"well below the 3–10 g/kg range most athletes are advised, scaled to training load."**

## Batch 10: GI tolerance and polyols (7 of 50; A 3 · B 2 · C 1 · D 1)

**What it establishes**
- **30–50% of athletes report exercise GI complaints** (landmark review, B10-001).
- Beyond endurance sports the range is 11.5–80%: ~1 in 2 women and 1 in 3 men around competition (B10-002).
- **Low-FODMAP eating reduces daily GI symptoms in symptomatic athletes.** Polyols are FODMAPs (n = 11 RCT, B10-004).
- **Weight cutting itself causes GI symptoms** in combat athletes (B10-007).
- **Combat-specific gut research is thin** (B10-006). **No study on eating before or after grappling was found.** That remains a primary-research opportunity.

**Counter-evidence (important)**
A maltitol tolerance trial found the **diarrhoea threshold at ~92 g a day** (B10-003). Secondary sources cite ~60 g per sitting, and the EU says symptoms are unlikely below ~20 g/day (B17-008). **One lab bar (13–18 g polyols) is well below those levels.**

**So:** "Lab bars give fighters stomach problems" is **overstated**. The defensible line is: *"Many lab bars carry a laxative warning; two a day approach the level regulators flag; some athletes avoid sugar alcohols. We use none."* **Test H2 properly** with a gut-comfort question in fighter interviews.
