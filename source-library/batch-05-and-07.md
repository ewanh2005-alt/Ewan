## Batch 5: Combat-sport and MMA participation

**Shortfall: 14 of 50.** There is little credible free data: Sport England's sport-level tables, the British Judo figures and Statista values are in spreadsheets or paywalled PDFs I couldn't open, and no MMA register is public.
**Grade mix:** B 4 · C 7 · D 2 · E 1 (A–B 29%).

### Reasoning summary
**What the batch establishes**
- **Official base:** Active Lives 2023–24 (released 24 Apr 2025) defines regular participation as ≥2× in 28 days (B5-001). The founder's ~293k combat-sport figure (2022–23) comes via Statista (B5-002). The 2023–24 value is still to be extracted.
- **Boxing is at a record high:** 1,086 clubs and >36,000 members (2024/25; B5-003 to B5-005), plus 8,850 members in Northern Ireland (B5-009).
- **BJJ is bigger than the brief assumed:** UKBJJA's 2025 AGM reports **1,000+ clubs and an estimated 100,000+ participants, ~50% of them members** (B5-006). This replaces the weak "~40k / 300+ clubs" figure. It's an NGB estimate, so grade it C.
- **MMA:** still **no participant count**. But amateur fighters now register with **Safe MMA** for annual medicals under **English MMA (EMMAA)** (B5-011, B5-012). **That register is the route to a real number: email EMMAA and Safe MMA.**

**Triangulation:** boxing growth is supported by the NGB register (B5-003/004) and a news data story (B5-014). The BJJ size is **single-source**. The MMA count is a **[GAP]**.
**Contradictions:** England Boxing gives 1,086 vs 1,084 clubs (same season, different snapshots; trivial). BJJ is 40k (older, weak) vs 100k+ participants (2025 AGM). Trust the AGM figure, labelled as an estimate.
**What it does *not* show:** how many people train MMA itself; how many train twice a day; any Scotland or Wales combat breakdown.
**So what for the pitch:** "Combat sports are growing: boxing clubs are at a record high, and BJJ reports 1,000+ clubs and ~100k participants. MMA has no official count, which is why I'm asking the amateur MMA register directly."

## Batch 7: Weight cutting and rapid weight loss

**Shortfall: 8 of 50.** Excluded for incomplete metadata: two MMA studies whose authors weren't shown (CSAC 512-fighter regain paper; IJSNEM 2019 29(1) "weight regain related to success"), a PMC combat-nutrition review without visible authors, and Barley and Harms (2025).
**Grade mix:** A 4 · B 4 (A–B 100%).

### Reasoning summary
**What the batch establishes**
- **Weight cutting is near-universal in MMA:** ~97% in Hillier et al. 2019 (n = 314; B7-001) and 97% in Connor and Egan 2019 (n = 30, Ireland; B7-003), averaging ~7.9% of body mass. UK fighters cut ~8% and regained ~11.7% within ~32 h (n = 7; B7-002).
- **Across combat sports:** weight loss is highly prevalent, most cut <5%, and **sports scientists have little influence** on practices (Zhong et al. 2024 SR, 26 studies, n = 3,994; B7-004). Combined with Hillier's finding that **coaches** are the main influence, this means the channel to fighters is coaches, not dietitians.
- **Harm:** rapid weight loss raises kidney-stress markers (Lakicevic et al. 2021 SR; B7-006).

**Triangulation:** MMA prevalence rests on 3 independent primary studies plus 2 reviews. **Triangulated.**

**Counter-evidence (steelman)**
- **Moderate cuts (≤5% in <7 days) don't impair performance** in Olympic combat athletes (De Albuquerque Mauricio et al. 2022 meta-analysis; B7-007).
- **Post-weigh-in regain doesn't predict winning** in pro MMA (Kirk et al. 2020; B7-008).
- So "fighters need this to perform better" is **not** supported. The defensible angle is **day-to-day fuelling while managing weight, and health**, not a performance claim.

**Contradictions:** prevalence 50% (Franchini 2012, all combat sports) vs 88–97% (MMA-specific, 2019+). Trust the newer MMA-specific data.

**What it does *not* show:** what fighters eat **between sessions out of camp**; whether a snack changes cutting behaviour. Don't imply the product makes weight cutting safer.

**So what for the pitch:** "Around 97% of MMA fighters cut weight, and coaches, not nutritionists, guide them. Fighters live in a constant weight-managed state, which is why low sugar matters. But I'm not claiming a performance or safety benefit."
