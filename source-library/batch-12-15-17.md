## Batch 12: Competitors (12 of 50; grade mix C 4 · D 8)

**What it establishes**
- **Lab bars run on polyols.** Pack data:
  - Grenade: 20 g protein, 18 g carbs **including 15–18 g polyols**, ~1 g sugar, laxative warning (B12-001/002).
  - Barebells: maltitol and sucralose, ~20 g protein (B12-003).
  - Fulfil: **13–18 g polyols** per bar (B12-004).
  - Misfits: **15 g protein, 18 g carbs, 0.6 g sugar in 50 g**, the same macros v13 targets, but with maltitol (B12-005).
- **The EU regulator puts the laxative threshold at ~20 g polyols a day** (B17-008). Two Grenade or Fulfil bars a day can exceed it. **This is the strongest factual "gut" argument, and it's compliant:** say *"no sugar alcohols"*, not "better for your gut".
- **Nearest real-food bars fail on sugar:** Veloforte Forza has 27.6 g sugar per bar (39 g/100 g) (B12-006); TORQ is £2.50 RRP with 22.7 g sugar (B12-007).

**New precedent (important)**
- **RXBAR** (egg white, short ingredient list) sold to Kellogg for ~$600m (2017; B12-009).
- Kellogg launched it **in the UK via D2C and 200+ gyms** (2018; B12-010), then **quit the UK within ~2 years** (B12-011).

That is the closest precedent to this venture, and it's **counter-evidence**. A judge may know it. **Prepare the answer:** "RXBAR was a US brand imported with a US recipe (dates, nuts: high sugar) and general-gym positioning. We're starting from one community (fighters), with a low-sugar recipe, and we're testing demand before scaling." Then find out *why* it exited `[GAP]`.

**Correction:** JNCK Bakery went into liquidation (Sept 2025) **after** a Tesco listing. A Tesco *delisting* is **not confirmed** (B12-008). Fix the wording in the report and Q&A.

**So what for the pitch:** "Every big UK protein bar gets there with 13–18 g of sugar alcohols per bar. The real-food ones have 23–28 g of sugar. And when RXBAR tried UK gyms without a specific community, it left within two years."

## Batch 15: Food safety and water activity (10 of 50; grade mix B 5 · C 1 · D 4)

**What it establishes**
- **UK framework:** FSS shelf-life guidance (March 2025) sets out how to meet Reg. 2073/2005 across the shelf life, with aw as an intrinsic factor (B15-001). Bacteria generally need **aw ≥0.9** (B15-002).
- **Thresholds, triangulated** (Manitoba government, MSU/FDA chapter, Food Safety Magazine):
  - *S. aureus*: 0.86
  - most moulds: ~0.80
  - xerophilic moulds: ~0.60–0.62
  - industry rule of thumb for bars: <0.70 (B15-003 to B15-006)
- **Two new risks:**
  - aw **rises in storage** if packaging lets moisture in (B15-007), so packaging is a safety control.
  - Egg-white proteins **aggregate and harden** over storage (B15-010). v13 may go rock-hard by week 2–4.
- **Route:** Campden BRI runs storage trials, priced on enquiry (B15-008).

**Recommendation for the lab plan:** measure aw at day 0. Then run a 4-week ambient trial checking aw, yeasts and moulds, and **texture** at days 0, 7, 14 and 28. Target **aw ≤0.65** for a ≥4-week life; at aw 0.65–0.80, use a short shelf life.

**So what for the pitch:** "I'll only call it bag-stable once a lab has measured water activity below the mould threshold and run a 4-week trial."

## Batch 17: GB regulation (10 of 50; grade mix B 6 · C 3 · D 1)

**What it establishes**
- **Only claims on the GB NHC register are lawful** (B17-001).
- **Thresholds confirmed:** low sugar ≤5 g/100 g; high protein ≥20% energy (B17-002/003). v13 at 5.8 g/100 g **can't say "low sugar"** yet.
- **ASA/CAP rules cover the landing page, YouTube and Instagram** (B17-004).
- **Free samples can still make you a food business.** Occasional supply may not; a regular, organised gym pilot almost certainly does (B17-005). Registration is free, **≥28 days ahead** (B17-006).
- **Polyol rules:** the >10% laxative statement (B17-007), ~20 g/day threshold (B17-008, EU).
- **HFSS placement rules** exempt businesses with <50 staff (B17-009). **PPDS / Natasha's Law** applies if bars are packed where they're sold (B17-010).

**So what for the pitch:** "I've checked the claims register: today we can lawfully say 'high protein'. We'll earn 'low sugar' by reformulating and lab-testing, not by wording."
