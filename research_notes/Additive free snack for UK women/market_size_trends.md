# UK Protein Bar, Snack Bar and Better-for-You Snacking Market: Size, Growth and Trends (research date: 23 Oct 2026)

Method note: Most UK trade press (thegrocer.co.uk, foodnavigator.com, nielseniq.com, grocerygazette.co.uk) could not be fetched directly from this environment (blocked by the network proxy). Figures below come from search-engine extracts of those pages plus accessible sources. Each figure is quoted as the search extract gave it. Where possible, check against the original article before quoting externally. Paid market-research figures (Grand View, Mordor and others) are modelled estimates and less reliable than till/panel data (Circana, NIQ, Worldpanel).

## 1. How big are the UK protein bar and snack/cereal bar markets, and how fast are they growing?

### Takeaway
UK snack bars are a mature category worth roughly £0.4–0.5bn. Value is growing only through price inflation and volume is falling: Mintel reports 2025 value +4.6% and volume −3.3%. Protein bars, and protein-claim cereal bars, are the growth pocket. Sports nutrition and protein bar value was up around 16% in The Grocer's 2025 ranking, and NIQ saw about 20% growth for cereal bars with a protein claim in early 2026. The market is fragmenting: the leader Grenade is losing share to Barebells, Huel, Warrior and supermarket own-label.

### Cited Findings
**Wider UK snack / cereal bar market**
- UK snack bars and breakfast biscuits: price inflation drove **value sales growth of 4.6% in 2025, while volume fell 3.3%**. Mintel expects ongoing ingredient-cost pressure to drive **12% value growth over 2025–30**. It also says "Nature Valley retains top position" and "leading brands struggle to make strong gains". 16–34s are the most engaged age group (Mintel UK Snack Bars and Breakfast Biscuits Market Report 2026) — [Mintel store](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-snack-bars-and-breakfast-biscuits-market-report)
- UK cereal bars retail value: **£434.65m in 2024, +1.78% vs 2023** (GlobalData) — [GlobalData](https://www.globaldata.com/store/report/uk-cereal-bars-market-analysis/)
- The Grocer (March 2025): snack bar profits are "crumbling" because of soaring wheat, nut and cocoa prices ("Going, going, gone? Trends in snack bars 2025") — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/analysis-and-features/going-going-gone-trends-in-snack-bars-2025/702331.article)
- Modelled estimate: UK snack bar market **USD 1.66bn in 2025**, with protein bars forecast as the fastest-growing segment at an **8.11% CAGR to 2031** (Mordor Intelligence; modelled, scope wider than till data) — [Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/united-kingdom-snack-bar-market)

**UK protein bars**
- Circana, 24 weeks to 24 Nov 2024: **protein bars and shakes combined worth £230m, +7.5% YoY**. Grenade was cited as the leading protein bar brand with a **57% share** (share basis unclear) — [Asian Trader](https://www.asiantrader.biz/protein-products-uk-sales-2025)
- Kantar: UK **protein product sales (bars, bites, drinks) were 47% higher YoY in January 2025**, with over 2 million households buying that month — [Asian Trader](https://www.asiantrader.biz/protein-products-uk-sales-2025)
- The Grocer, Sports Nutrition 2025 ranking (Circana data, published 2025): **value sales of protein bars and other sports nutrition up 16%, units up 5.2%**. Market leader **Grenade's value fell 16.2% and units 13.1%**, and Fulfil and PHD also declined. **Huel value rose 60.9%** on distribution gains and new products. **Warrior value +15.8%, units +4.4%**, helped by low pricing — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/rankings/sports-nutrition-2025-protein-bars-surge-despite-upf-debate/712930.article)
- Per The Grocer's Top Products 2025 (reported by FoodNavigator, 6 Jan 2026): **Optimum Nutrition volume +11.7%, Huel +31.1%**, while Grenade struggled. FoodNavigator frames the category as "more selective" rather than in decline — [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/01/06/did-joe-wicks-kill-off-demand-for-protein-bars/)
- Grenade's sponsored content in The Grocer (2026) puts the UK protein bar market at **"over £164m in sales"** and calls Grenade the UK's no.1 protein bar brand. This is a brand claim and does not match the Circana decline above — [The Grocer (promotional)](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/promotional-videos/why-protein-has-a-long-and-healthy-future/718650.article)
- NIQ (Feb 2026, 26 weeks to late Jan 2026): NIQ's Mike Watkins reported a **20% boost to sales of cereal bars with a protein claim** — [NIQ](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2026/uk-shoppers-shift-to-healthier-diets-in-2026-fuelling-growth-in-protein-and-fibre-sales/); [Convenience Store](https://www.conveniencestore.co.uk/products/uk-shoppers-plan-shift-to-healthier-diets-fuelling-growth-in-protein-and-fibre/714896.article)
- Grenade launched a three-SKU "Soft Core" protein bar (2026), targeting "growing demand for 10–19g protein snacks", which is a lower-protein, more snack-like tier — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/grenade-taps-demand-for-textured-snacks-with-soft-core-protein-bar/721430.article)
- Modelled estimates: UK protein bar revenue **USD 1,383.4m in 2024, forecast USD 1,959.0m by 2030 (6% CAGR)** (Grand View Research). These look high compared with UK till data and probably include wider channels or definitions — [Grand View](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/protein-bar-market/uk)
- Unverified aggregator claim: UK protein bar sales **£320m in 2023, +6.4%**, and UK retail volume of 22–25k tonnes in 2025, with whey bars at about 60–65% of volume and plant-based bars growing faster. No primary source is given — [IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/united-kingdom-kw-protein-bar-snack-840-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)

**US comparison (not UK)**
- Circana US, 52 weeks to 22 Mar 2026: snack and granola bars **$10.9bn, +7.1%**. Barebells **$482.9m, +142.3%**. The "breakfast/cereal/snack bars" subcategory fell **6.4% to $2.3bn** — [Snack & Bakery](https://www.snackandbakery.com/articles/115955-state-of-the-industry-2026-bars-with-bfy-focus-thrive)

### Inferences
- Size estimates for the UK protein bar market range from about £164m (Grenade claim, likely grocery-only) to over £1bn (modelled USD figures), so definitions differ widely. The till-data anchor is about £230m for bars and shakes combined (Circana, late 2024). A reasonable working assumption is that grocery protein bars are in the low hundreds of £m, growing high single to mid-teens percent, inside a roughly £0.4–0.5bn cereal/snack bar category that is flat to declining in volume.
- Growth is shifting from the incumbent gym brand (Grenade) to taste-led (Barebells), mainstream or "nutrition" (Huel) and value (Warrior) players. Protein claims are migrating into conventional cereal bars. A new entrant competes with protein-fortified mainstream bars as much as with sports nutrition brands.
- Volume decline across the wider bar category (Mintel, −3.3% in 2025) means a premium natural bar must win share or justify its price, not rely on market growth.

### Gaps
- No full-year 2025 or 2026 Circana/NIQ/Worldpanel total for UK protein bars alone was found. The Grocer's 2026 sports nutrition ranking was not accessible.
- No verified UK Barebells, Fulfil or Grenade financial results with category data were found. Barebells' UK share is unknown.
- No brand-level UK data was found for Nakd, Kind, Trek or Nature Valley in 2025–26.

## 2. Is protein mainstreaming beyond gym-goers, and is there evidence of saturation or fatigue?

### Takeaway
Protein is clearly mainstream in the UK. NIQ reports 57% of shoppers following a high-protein diet to some extent, protein foods were up 9.6% in H2 2025, and supermarkets launched dedicated high-protein ranges in 2026. Backlash and fatigue signals are growing: "protein washing" criticism, nutritionists saying UK adults already eat enough protein, the UPF critique of bars, and attention shifting to fibre. However, no evidence of an actual protein sales decline was found.

### Cited Findings
- NIQ (Feb 2026): **protein-based foods +9.6% and fibre-based foods +14.1%** over the 26 weeks to late January 2026, against about **4.1% total FMCG growth** — [NIQ](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2026/uk-shoppers-shift-to-healthier-diets-in-2026-fuelling-growth-in-protein-and-fibre-sales/); [Convenience Store](https://www.conveniencestore.co.uk/products/uk-shoppers-plan-shift-to-healthier-diets-fuelling-growth-in-protein-and-fibre/714896.article)
- The Grocer, "Protein mania: are shoppers getting too much of a good thing?" (about Aug 2026), citing NIQ: **57% of UK shoppers are following a high-protein diet to some extent**. It also cites the British Nutrition Foundation that average UK adults already eat more than enough protein, and nutritionists say brands have a "responsibility" for honest claims — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/analysis-and-features/protein-mania-are-shoppers-getting-too-much-of-a-good-thing/722201.article)
- Sainsbury's (Feb 2026) launched a High Protein range of **19 new SKUs** plus "Small but Mighty" meals (at least 31g protein, under 500 kcal). Online searches for "high protein" were **up 57% YoY** — [Sainsbury's](https://corporate.sainsburys.co.uk/news/press-releases/sainsbury-s-launches-all-new-high-protein-range-and-small-but-mighty-meals-to-support-customers-nutritional-goal/); [Food Manufacture](https://www.foodmanufacture.co.uk/Article/2026/02/03/sainsburys-launches-a-raft-of-new-high-protein-products-alongside-its-new-small-but-mighty-range/)
- Kantar, Jan 2025: protein product sales **+47% YoY**, with more than 2m households buying in the month — [Asian Trader](https://www.asiantrader.biz/protein-products-uk-sales-2025)
- Babybel Protein-commissioned survey (reported 16 Jul 2026): **42% of UK women have increased protein intake over the past five years**, and **51% look for protein when choosing snacks**. Brand-commissioned with no methodology given — [Female First](http://www.femalefirst.co.uk/health/women-choosing-protein-part-healthy-balanced-diet-study-finds-1447580.html)
- Fatigue and backlash: Oxford Online Pharmacy analysed **88 high-protein snacks** across the six biggest UK supermarkets and found many less healthy or cost-effective than consumers believe. Google searches for **"protein washing" rose 488%** in a year (2026) — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/analysis-and-features/protein-mania-are-shoppers-getting-too-much-of-a-good-thing/722201.article)
- In July 2026, US brand Purely Elizabeth ran a campaign mocking "proteinmaxxing" (US example of brand-level backlash) — [TrendHunter](https://www.trendhunter.com/trends/purely-elizabeth-campaign)
- Joe Wicks' UPF criticism of protein bars (2025) prompted the question "did Joe Wicks kill off demand for protein bars?". The Grocer and FoodNavigator conclude the category grew anyway but became more selective — [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/01/06/did-joe-wicks-kill-off-demand-for-protein-bars/); [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/rankings/sports-nutrition-2025-protein-bars-surge-despite-upf-debate/712930.article)
- "Benefit stacking" critique (Oct 2026): snacks combining protein, fibre, probiotics, collagen and adaptogens, with the question whether functional snacking is "going too far" — [BakeryandSnacks](https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/10/15/benefit-stacking-is-functional-snacking-going-too-far/)

### Inferences
- Protein is now a table-stakes claim in snacking, not a differentiator. In a crowded field, a moderate protein level (about 10–15g, matching Grenade's "10–19g" positioning) with honest, clean credentials may stand out more than chasing 20g+.
- Fatigue is mostly in commentary and credibility ("protein washing"), not yet in sales. Brands with inflated or heavily processed protein claims face the most reputational risk.

### Gaps
- No hard data showing UK protein bar sales slowing in 2026 was found. Saturation evidence is qualitative.
- The number of UK high-protein product launches (e.g. Mintel GNPD counts) was not found.

## 3. How significant are UPF backlash, clean label, gut health and fibre trends in UK food in 2024–2026, and what is their impact on bars?

### Takeaway
UPF concern is high and stable: around three-quarters of UK adults are concerned (FSA tracker, 74–79% across Apr 2025–Mar 2026; 78% in June 2026, second only to food prices). Fibre is the fastest-growing health claim: NIQ fibre foods +14.1%, and Lidl and Sainsbury's have set fibre targets. Gut health interest is high but becoming more specific. Sales impact on bars is mixed. Protein bars kept growing despite the 2025 UPF debate, but "fibremaxxing" is not yet strongly visible in snack choices (Worldpanel servings for fibre/vitamins +1.1%).

### Cited Findings
**UPF backlash**
- FSA Consumer Insights Tracker (England, Wales and NI; about 2,000 adults a month): in **June 2026, 78% were concerned about UPF**, the second-highest concern after food prices (91%) and above food quality (76%) — [FSA](https://science.food.gov.uk/article/165444-consumer-insights-tracker-april-2026-june-2026)
- FSA end-of-year report (Apr 2025–Mar 2026): UPF concern stayed in a range of **74%–79%** — [FSA](https://science.food.gov.uk/article/164522-consumer-insights-tracker-end-of-year-report-insights-from-april-2025-to-march-2026)
- FSA Food and You 2 (fieldwork May–Aug 2025, reported 2026): **49% of those who had made health-related diet changes said they were eating less processed food**, and UPF fears were the biggest factor in diet changes — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/ultra-processed-food-fears-biggest-factor-in-diet-changes-finds-fsa-survey/719921.article)
- Vypr survey (Jan 2026 fieldwork, commercial): **61% of UK consumers plan to cut back on UPFs in 2026**, and 71% are worried about UPFs — [Vypr](https://vyprclients.com/blog/shoppers-avoiding-ultra-processed-foods/)
- UPF "dominates consumer concerns" (Jul 2025) — [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2025/07/10/ultra-processed-foods-dominate-consumer-concerns/)
- Protein bars criticised as UPF (e.g. Barebells classed as UPF for maltitol and sucralose by a ranking site). Commentators argue "protein bars solved taste; the next wave will solve trust" — [foodfor.you](https://foodfor.you/en/blog/are-protein-bars-healthy-we-ranked-top-10-brands-2026-edition/); [The Food Stack](https://thefoodstack.substack.com/p/protein-bars-solved-taste-the-next)

**Fibre / fibremaxxing**
- NIQ (Feb 2026): **fibre-based foods +14.1%** over 26 weeks, against 4.1% total FMCG — [NIQ](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2026/uk-shoppers-shift-to-healthier-diets-in-2026-fuelling-growth-in-protein-and-fibre-sales/)
- FDF (2026): Action on Fibre members have launched **500 new or reformulated fibre-source products since 2021**, adding about **2bn portions of fibre**. In **2025 alone, 100 launches added 130m portions** — [FDF](https://www.fdf.org.uk/fdf/news-media/press-releases/2026/fibremaxxing-food-manufacturers-add-2-billion-portions-of-fibre-to-nations-plates-in-last-five-years/)
- Lidl GB (2025): first UK supermarket with a fibre sales target, aiming for **+20% tonnage of fibre sold by 2026** and wholegrains at 25% of grains sold by 2030. Lidl cites **90% of adults not getting enough fibre** and says promotions nearly doubled sales of fibre-rich products — [Lidl](https://corporate.lidl.co.uk/media-centre/pressreleases/2025/lidl-is-first-uk-supermarket-to-introduce-fibre-boosting-sales-targets); [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/lidl-claims-a-uk-supermarket-first-with-fibre-food-sales-target/700040.article)
- Sainsbury's (June 2026): "Full on Fibre" labelling on **500+ products**, new products, and a pledge to add thousands of tonnes of fibre to UK diets by 2030 — [Food Manufacture](https://www.foodmanufacture.co.uk/Article/2026/06/09/sainsburys-promises-to-up-fibre-in-uk-diets-with-new-products-and-labelling-that-flags-fibre/)
- HelloFresh survey: **65% of Britons** are trying to eat more fibre than five years ago — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/category-reports/the-brands-capitalising-on-fibremaxxing/718536.article)
- Caveat (The Grocer, 2026, Worldpanel): snack servings chosen for containing fibre and vitamins rose **only 1.1% YoY to 2.9bn**. Online chatter is not yet matched in snacking behaviour — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/category-reports/the-brands-capitalising-on-fibremaxxing/718536.article)
- Shoppers buying more plants as fibremaxxing takes hold (Sept 2026) — [Food Bible](https://www.foodbible.com/health/diet/shoppers-buying-more-plants-fibremaxxing-trend-too-much-919511-20260904)

**Gut health / functional**
- Tastewise (2026, vendor social and menu data): gut health as a consumer need **+44.2% YoY**. Between Aug 2024 and Jul 2026 most gut-health associations declined except hormone balance, so the claim is becoming more specific. **11.1% of UK consumers link gut health to anti-bloating** — [Tastewise](https://tastewise.io/blog/uk-food-trends)
- Danone / Grocer Vision study (2026): **61.2%** are more interested in food and drink offering extra health benefits such as protein, fibre, gut health or hydration — [The Grocer whitepaper](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/whitepapers/how-to-cater-to-the-new-functional-consumer/722824.article)
- Mintel 2026: gut-healthy ready meals appeal to 77% of ready-meal eaters. Mintel calls gut health a "substantial whitespace" (shows broad appeal of gut claims) — [Mintel](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-ready-meals-market-report)
- FoodNavigator, "Gut health: consumer expectations" (15 Sep 2026) — [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/09/15/gut-health-consumer-expectations/)

### Inferences
- Combining moderate protein, real-food fibre (not isolated fibres or sugar alcohols that can cause bloating) and no additives directly addresses the three most active UK health concerns: UPF, fibre and gut. The "gentle on the gut" angle also counters a known complaint about many protein bars (polyols, sucralose).
- Fibre is at the "retailer and manufacturer push" stage, with labelling and targets, ahead of strong snack-level consumer pull. That makes it an early-mover window but not yet proven demand in bars.
- UPF concern is high, yet protein bars still grew, so stated concern overstates actual switching. A clean-label claim needs to come with taste and price parity.

### Gaps
- No UK data quantifying clean-label or "no additives" bar sales growth (e.g. Nakd, Kind, Deliciously Ella) in 2024–26 was found.
- No UK market size for gut-health snacks was found. The FMCG Gurus UK Digestive Health 2025 report is paywalled.

## 4. What is the effect of GLP-1 weight-loss drug adoption in the UK on snacking and protein demand?

### Takeaway
GLP-1 adoption in Britain is growing quickly. Worldpanel puts it at 6.3% of households in 2026 (vs 2.3% in 2024); IGD/NIQ put it at 12% of households by mid-2026, using a different method. Users cut snacks and confectionery sharply but say they eat more protein and fibre. This shrinks indulgent snacking and supports small, nutrient-dense, protein-and-fibre snacks.

### Cited Findings
- Worldpanel by Numerator (published about June to Sept 2026): **6.3% of British households include at least one GLP-1 user, up from 4.1% in 2025 and 2.3% in 2024**. User households spent about **£780m less on groceries** (about **£418 per household**) in the year after starting treatment. The Grocer describes the period as two years, so the time frame is unclear. The hardest-hit categories were **crisps, confectionery, processed food and alcohol**, and chocolate and fresh bakery also fell — [Grocery Gazette (11 Sep 2026)](https://www.grocerygazette.co.uk/2026/09/11/mounjaro-effect-deepens-as-weight-loss-drugs-reshape-uk-grocery-spending/); [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/analysis-and-features/the-true-impact-of-weight-loss-drugs-six-key-takeaways/720034.article)
- GLP-1 use in the UK has nearly tripled in two years to **about 1.9m adults** (2026). Other outlets say more than 2m — [Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/business/companies/arid-41860326.html)
- IGD and NIQ (Jul 2026): households with at least one GLP-1 user **rose from 9% to 12% in six months**. **80%** of these households say the drug affects the eating habits of everyone in the home. **42% of users eat more protein** and **34% eat more fibre** — [NIQ](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2026/80-of-uk-households-with-a-glp-1-user-say-it-affects-eating-habits-of-everyone-in-the-home/); [IGD](https://www.igd.com/articles/glp-1-research-with-niq/73702)
- PwC survey (Jun 2026, via Bloomberg): UK GLP-1 users buy more high-protein foods and cut sugary snacks, with about **70% spending less on snacks and confectionery** — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-21/britain-s-glp-1-users-are-buying-healthier-food-survey-finds)
- Kantar Worldpanel (2025): **64% of UK GLP-1 users said they were snacking less** — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/analysis-and-features/exclusive-data-glp-1s-impact-on-uk-food-and-drink-consumption/705652.article)
- US NIQ: GLP-1 users drove **43% of sales growth for products with muscle-health claims** (US, not UK) — [NIQ](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/insights/analysis/2026/health-and-wellness-consumer-trends-the-rise-of-the-self-directed-health-consumer/)

### Inferences
- GLP-1 users are a natural target: they snack less but want protein (to preserve muscle) and fibre, and GLP-1 gastrointestinal side effects make "gentle on the gut" relevant. UK GLP-1 users skew female, especially through private online pharmacies, but no UK source confirming that was found in this research and it should be verified.
- The effect spreads beyond the user: 80% of user households report whole-household change (NIQ/IGD).

### Gaps
- Household penetration figures conflict (Worldpanel 6.3% vs IGD/NIQ 12%), probably due to definitions and methods.
- No data was found on GLP-1 users' bar purchasing specifically, or on the gender split of UK GLP-1 users.

## 5. What share of protein/snack bar buyers are women, and how does purchasing differ by age?

### Takeaway
No current (2024–26) UK panel data was found giving women's share of protein bar buyers. The best available survey evidence (The Grocer "10 charts", likely about 2019) shows protein bar use skewed male and young: a third of men eat them weekly vs 14% of women, and 54% of 25–34s vs 10% of 55–64s. Mintel says 16–34s are the most engaged snack bar users. Recent brand surveys suggest women's protein interest is rising.

### Cited Findings
- The Grocer, "Gender bias, health concerns and sports-shy consumers: 10 charts explaining UK attitudes to protein" (undated in extract; article ID suggests about 2019): **about a third of men eat protein bars at least weekly vs 14% of women**. **54% of 25–34s eat protein bars weekly or more vs 10% of 55–64s** — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/analysis-and-features/gender-bias-health-concerns-and-sports-shy-consumers-10-charts-explaining-uk-attitudes-to-protein/598311.article)
- Mintel (2025/26 summary): **16–34s are most engaged** with snack bars and breakfast biscuits — [Mintel](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-snack-bars-and-breakfast-biscuits-market-report)
- Mintel (older, 2012 data): **women under 35**, large households and households with children had the highest cereal bar consumption. The over-55s had the lowest — [AAFC/Mintel PDF (2014)](https://www.agr.gc.ca/resources/prod/Internet-Internet/MISB-DGSIM/ATS-SEA/PDF/6446-eng.pdf)
- Babybel Protein survey (Jul 2026): **42% of women have increased protein intake in the last five years**, and **51% look for protein in snacks** (brand-commissioned) — [Female First](http://www.femalefirst.co.uk/health/women-choosing-protein-part-healthy-balanced-diet-study-finds-1447580.html)
- Unverified aggregator claim: women aged 25–44 increased protein bar consumption by 40–60% since 2019. No source is named, so do not rely on it — [IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/store/united-kingdom-kw-protein-bar-snack-840-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/)
- Mintel UK sports nutrition press release (date not confirmed in extract) on Brits' growing appetite for sports nutrition — [Mintel](https://www.mintel.com/press-centre/brits-build-up-eager-appetite-for-sports-nutrition/)

### Inferences
- Historically, protein bars under-indexed with women while conventional cereal and snack bars over-indexed with younger women. A natural, protein-plus-fibre bar sits between the two: the protein benefit without the "gym bro" or UPF image. The male skew of traditional protein bars suggests women are under-served rather than uninterested.
- Highest engagement is under 35, but menopause and perimenopause (protein for muscle), GLP-1 users and gut-health seekers suggest a second, older female segment worth testing.

### Gaps
- No 2024–26 Kantar/Worldpanel, Circana or NIQ data was found on gender share of UK protein or snack bar shoppers. This would need direct access to panel data or Mintel reports.
- No UK data was found on menopause- or perimenopause-related snack purchasing.
