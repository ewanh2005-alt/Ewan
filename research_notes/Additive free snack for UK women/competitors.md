## Which brands lead UK protein bar sales and which are growing fastest?

### Takeaway
Grenade is still the long-time UK volume leader but is shrinking. Barebells is the standout grower. The overall category grew strongly in 2025 despite debate about ultra-processed food (UPF). I found no current independent brand-share table.

### Cited Findings
- The Grocer's Sports Nutrition 2025 ranking: protein bar category value sales up 16% on units up 5.2%. "Market leader Grenade has seen value slide 16.2% and unit sales slump 13.1%." Fulfil and PHD "also suffered significant losses." The headline is "Protein bars surge despite UPF debate". Read via search snippet only. — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/rankings/sports-nutrition-2025-protein-bars-surge-despite-upf-debate/712930.article)
- Warrior's founder blames price sensitivity: shoppers don't want to spend £3 on a protein bar. — [The Grocer (snippet)](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/rankings/sports-nutrition-2025-protein-bars-surge-despite-upf-debate/712930.article)
- Grenade, Barebells, SIS and Fulfil dominate the UK sports bar fixture, alongside Warrior, Tribe, N!ck's and Misfits. UK protein bars have grown close to double digits annually over five years. — [Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/features/stay-the-pace-protein-bars-face-threats-to-growth/)
- Ownership: Mondelez owns Grenade and Ferrero owns Fulfil. Barebells owner Vitamin Well is merging with its manufacturer EMPWR, whose CEO credits "the phenomenal success of Barebells". — [Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/news/barebells-protein-bars-owner-merger/)
- Barebells is one of the fastest-growing snack bar brands: Circana shows dollar sales up 173% and units up 139% year on year. This is likely US data. — [Bakery & Snacks, June 2026](https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/06/11/the-next-wave-of-functional-food-taste-meets-nutrition/)
- Historic data: Grenade was the UK number one at £23.4m (2018). A Grenade-sponsored piece claims 51% share of the protein bar market. This is brand-sourced and undated. — [The Grocer, 2018](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/analysis-and-features/how-grenade-exploded-into-confectionery-mainstream/597294.article); [Grocery Trader](https://grocerytrader.co.uk/raising-the-bar-hot-off-the-shelves-grenade-announces-its-outstanding-growth-results/)
- The protein bar market was cited as £144m in an older, undated Grocer Vision post. — [X / The Grocer Vision](https://x.com/TheGrocerVision/status/1789898541906706811)

### Inferences
- Value is moving from Grenade and Fulfil towards Barebells. That suggests consumers reward taste and texture over "macro maximalism", and that premium pricing near £3 meets resistance.
- Newer "gut-health" and "women's health" bars (Bio&Me, ZOE, Fem, Evelyn) are small but launched in 2025–26. They signal a functional-for-women sub-segment forming outside the sports fixture.

### Gaps
- No 2026 brand-level UK value-share data (Circana or NielsenIQ) for Barebells, Myprotein, Misfits, nakd or Trek. The full Grocer ranking article could not be fetched.
- No sales data for Bio&Me Daily Boost or the ZOE bar.

## Which segments does each brand own: young gym-going women vs older health-conscious women?

### Takeaway
Young gym-going women are served by sweetened, high-protein, taste-led bars (Barebells, and probably Vieve, Misfits and Myprotein). Older, health-conscious, gut-focused women are being claimed by dietitian- and science-led brands (Bio&Me, ZOE) with lower protein (7–9g) and high fibre. Cycle and hormone needs are being claimed by Fem, Evelyn and Mena.

### Cited Findings
- Bio&Me Daily Boost is explicitly targeted at women and gut-health led, positioned against "protein bars marketed to men" and against artificial sweeteners and UPF ingredients. — [FoodBev](https://www.foodbev.com/news/bio-me-launches-new-protein-and-fibre-bars-targeting-female-consumers)
- ZOE prioritises fibre over protein (8g fibre, 7g protein), priced around £2.33/bar and sold DTC to its app or science audience. — [NutraIngredients](https://nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/06/25/zoe-targets-fiber-gap-with-new-gut-health-bar); [ZOE](https://zoe.com/en-gb/buyguthealthbar)
- Fem targets menstrual-cycle symptoms (fatigue, bloating, cramping) at £2.25 in Holland & Barrett. Evelyn launched PMS Bars in November 2025. The Grocer questions whether this is "wildly innovative or slightly questionable". — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/period-nutrition-bar-secures-holland-and-barrett-listing/721686.article); [The Grocer comment](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/comment-and-opinion/tampons-to-trail-bars-womens-health-is-getting-creative/721875.article)
- Menopause: Mena (Scotland, DTC subscription, plant oestrogens, about £2.50/bar). No 2026 mass-retail menopause bar was found. — [Mena](https://www.mena-pause.co.uk/products/mena-protein-bars-1-1)
- Vieve uses soy, milk protein and gelatin for 15g protein, plus chicory and polydextrose. — [Gymfluencers](https://gymfluencers.com/unwrapping-the-new-vieve-protein-bar/)

### Inferences
- The young gym-going female segment has no additive-free option. Every 15–20g bar found uses polyols, sweeteners and/or inulin.
- The older, health-conscious segment is contested by Bio&Me (grocery reach) and ZOE (brand authority), but both cap protein at under 10g.
- Menopause and perimenopause is almost empty in mainstream grocery. Only a small DTC player (Mena) was found.

### Gaps
- Branding and audience for Myprotein, Fulfil, Flower & White, Graze, Nature Valley Protein, Battle Oats, Primal Pantry, Kind and Built Puff were not verified in this session.
- No consumer demographic data (e.g. Kantar or Mintel) splitting protein bar buyers by age and gender.

## Is any UK bar truly additive-free yet delivers 10g+ protein? What's the trade-off pattern?

### Takeaway
I found no mainstream UK bar that hits 10g+ protein with zero sweeteners, polyols, emulsifiers or inulin. The pattern holds. Clean-label bars sit at about 3–9g protein (nakd, Deliciously Ella, Trek, Bio&Me, ZOE). Bars with 15–23g rely on maltitol, sucralose, acesulfame K, polydextrose, chicory inulin or gelatin (Barebells, Grenade, Vieve, Misfits). The nearest "clean" exceptions (RXBAR at 12g, FRANK, Allrealnutrition at 16g) either aren't confirmed in UK retail or use chicory fibre.

### Cited Findings
- Barebells: maltitol, sucralose and acesulfame K at 20g protein. — [Barebells UK](https://barebells.co.uk/product/barebells-salty-peanut/)
- Grenade: maltitol (about 14g per bar on one listing), sucralose and xylitol at 20–23g protein, with a polyol laxative warning. — [Low Carb Canada](https://www.lowcarbcanada.ca/products/grenade-carb-killa-cookie-dough-1-bar); [MyFoodData](https://tools.myfooddata.com/nutrition-facts/101401158/wt1)
- Bio&Me: 9.2g protein with no sweeteners, but contains chicory root fibre. — [Sainsbury's](https://www.sainsburys.co.uk/groceries/product/bio-me-daily-boost-3-x-40g-fibre-protein-cocoa-flapjack-bars)
- ZOE: 7g protein, no emulsifiers or artificial sweeteners. — [NutraIngredients](https://nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/06/25/zoe-targets-fiber-gap-with-new-gut-health-bar)
- Trek: 9.2g protein, no intense sweeteners, but 14g sugar from rice syrup and cane sugar. — [Amazon UK](https://www.amazon.co.uk/Trek-Cocoa-Oat-Protein-Flapjack/dp/B00DW586NS)
- nakd Cashew Cookie: two ingredients and no additives, but only 3.6g protein. nakd Protein reaches about 7–9.5g (sources conflict). — [Lifeloving](https://lifeloving.co.uk/non-upf-snack-bars-uk/); [Tesco](https://www.tesco.com/groceries/en-GB/products/314115557)
- RXBAR: 12g protein from egg whites, dates and nuts, but 13g sugar and UK availability unconfirmed. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rxbar)
- One UK guide argues a 20g-protein confectionery-style bar is likely ultra-processed. — [Lifeloving](https://lifeloving.co.uk/non-upf-snack-bars-uk/)

### Inferences
- The structural trade-off: getting above about 10g protein in a 40–50g bar without isolates, polyols and binders is technically hard. Brands either accept lower protein (Bio&Me, ZOE) or accept additives (sports brands).
- Even the "gut health" leader (Bio&Me) uses chicory inulin, a common FODMAP trigger for IBS-prone consumers. A truly inulin-free gut-gentle bar is an open claim.
- Bars with a polyol laxative warning (Grenade-style) are directly at odds with "gentle on the gut". That is a usable contrast in positioning.

### Gaps
- Primal Pantry protein, Bounce, Battle Oats, Graze protein, Nature Valley Protein, Kind, Flower & White, Myprotein, Fulfil and Built Puff ingredients were not retrieved in this session and should be checked on Tesco, Ocado or H&B pages. Some (e.g. Nature Valley Protein, Kind) are commonly believed to use soy lecithin or glucose syrup, but I did not verify this.
- ZOE full ingredient list: chicory or inulin presence not confirmed.

## Where is the clearest whitespace?

### Takeaway
The clearest gap is a mainstream-grocery bar for women, especially 35–55 and perimenopausal women, that combines: 10–12g+ protein from whole or minimally processed sources, 5g+ fibre without chicory or inulin (low-FODMAP-friendly), no sweeteners, polyols or emulsifiers, and low added sugar. Pricing should come in under the £2.25–£2.50 of the new women's functional bars.

### Cited Findings
- Women-targeted bars all sit at under 10g protein (Bio&Me 9.2g, ZOE 7g) or don't disclose it (Fem, Mena). — [Sainsbury's](https://www.sainsburys.co.uk/groceries/product/bio-me-daily-boost-3-x-40g-fibre-protein-cocoa-flapjack-bars); [NutraIngredients](https://nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/06/25/zoe-targets-fiber-gap-with-new-gut-health-bar)
- Price points: ZOE about £2.33, Fem £2.25, Mena about £2.50. Price resistance around £3 is noted in the category. — [ZOE](https://zoe.com/en-gb/buyguthealthbar); [Holland & Barrett](https://www.hollandandbarrett.com/shop/product/fem-bar-salted-caramel-40-g-6100028861); [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/rankings/sports-nutrition-2025-protein-bars-surge-despite-upf-debate/712930.article)
- No mainstream menopause bar was found in UK grocery for 2026. — [search results incl. Mena / Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/comment-and-opinion/tampons-to-trail-bars-womens-health-is-getting-creative/721875.article)
- Category growth persists despite the UPF debate, so there is room for a non-UPF protein proposition. — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/rankings/sports-nutrition-2025-protein-bars-surge-despite-upf-debate/712930.article)

### Inferences
- Whitespace 1, "clean protein": 10g+ protein with a genuinely short, kitchen-cupboard ingredient list. The obvious protein sources are nuts, seeds, egg white, pea or whole legumes. Nobody in UK mass retail owns it, and RXBAR is the closest global analogue.
- Whitespace 2, "gentle gut": fibre from oats, flax, chia or psyllium rather than chicory inulin or polyols, positioned against both sports bars (polyol warnings) and Bio&Me (chicory).
- Whitespace 3, "midlife women": the perimenopause and menopause need state (protein for muscle, fibre, calcium) has only DTC micro-players. Bio&Me and ZOE speak to women and gut health but not to midlife specifically.
- Risk: incumbents could reformulate. Bio&Me has dietitian authority and multiple grocery listings already, and ZOE has a large app audience.

### Gaps
- No consumer research on how many women actively avoid sweeteners or inulin, or what price they would pay.
- No confirmation of whether any UK private label (e.g. M&S, Waitrose) has a clean 10g+ bar. Not searched.
