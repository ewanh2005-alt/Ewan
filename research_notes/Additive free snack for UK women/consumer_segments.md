# Consumer Segments: UK Women, Protein Snacks, Natural/Additive-Free Food and Gut Health (Segment A: gym-going women 18-26 vs Segment B: health-conscious women 35-55+)

Research conducted October 2026 via web search (17 tool calls). Several key sources (Mintel, Kantar, YouGov full reports, FSA Food and You 2 tables) are paywalled or did not surface; many figures come from secondary trade press summaries and are flagged as such. Two primary pages (askattest.com, tggplc.com) could not be fetched (DNS failure), so figures from them are taken from search summaries only and should be verified.

## 1. Protein interest and consumption by age and gender; motivations

### Takeaway
Protein interest is highest among under-35s in the UK (39% of under-35s rank "high protein" as a top-3 healthy-food factor vs 21% of all adults, Mintel 2024), and women now make up just over half of protein seekers globally. Younger women's motivation leans to fitness/body composition; midlife women's emerging motivation is muscle preservation, satiety and menopause, but UK-specific midlife data is thin (best data is Australian).

### Cited Findings
- Share of UK consumers naming "high protein" as one of their three most important factors in healthy food rose from 15% (2019) to 21% (2024); among under-35s it reached 39% (Mintel, 2024; reported via an aggregator, survey base not confirmed) — [rblind.com summary of Mintel](https://rblind.com/post/3717709/3507475)
- Women make up 51% of consumers globally seeking to increase protein intake (Euromonitor International, September 2025; GLOBAL, not UK) — [Fitt Insider / Euromonitor press release](https://insider.fitt.co/press-release/women-now-outnumber-men-globally-in-seeking-higher-protein-intake-euromonitor-international/); see also [FoodNavigator, 15 Oct 2025](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2025/10/15/women-are-shaping-the-next-frontier-of-protein-innovation/)
- Euromonitor noted the most protein-motivated female groups were women 30+ and 60+ in Asia-Pacific and North American teenage girls (15-19); no UK age breakdown was published in the summary (Sept 2025, NON-UK) — [Fitt Insider / Euromonitor](https://insider.fitt.co/press-release/women-now-outnumber-men-globally-in-seeking-higher-protein-intake-euromonitor-international/)
- 24% of UK consumers had eaten a sports nutrition product in the past three months, rising to 42% of men aged 16-24 (Mintel, cited by Just Food; article date unclear, likely pre-2023) — [Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/news/mars-expands-range-with-protein-powder-launch-in-uk)
- Historical baseline: protein powder use among UK women aged 16-24 rose from 7% (2015) to 18% (2017) (Mintel, 2017; OLD) — [Mintel press centre](https://www.mintel.com/press-centre/brits-build-up-eager-appetite-for-sports-nutrition/)
- Top reasons UK shoppers sought high-protein groceries: "to improve general wellbeing" (44%) and "to increase strength" (37%); awareness of benefits such as muscle retention was limited (Canadean; date not given, likely older than 2023) — [Ausfoodnews](https://www.ausfoodnews.com.au/?p=20059)
- Innova data (cited by Glanbia) lists energy/stamina as the top reason consumers give for eating protein, with building muscle tied for fifth (date unclear, global) — [Glanbia Nutritionals](https://glanbianutritionals.com/en-gb/node/5916)
- Women aged 40-55 fall about 35g/day short of optimal protein; only one in three reach target. In a separate survey of 1,303 women in a paid weight-loss programme, almost 8 in 10 reported weight gain at this life stage (CSIRO, September 2026; AUSTRALIA, non-representative survey) — [CSIRO](https://www.csiro.au/en/news/All/News/2026/September/CSIRO-highlights-the-importance-of-protein-during-menopause); [NutritionInsight](https://www.nutritioninsight.com/news/australia-csiro-menopause-women-health-protein-diet.html)
- Brands are launching products aimed at gender- and life-stage-specific protein needs (Euromonitor, Sept 2025) — [Fitt Insider / Euromonitor](https://insider.fitt.co/press-release/women-now-outnumber-men-globally-in-seeking-higher-protein-intake-euromonitor-international/)

### Inferences
- Segment A already "speaks protein": under-35s are nearly twice as likely as average to prioritise it, so a protein claim is table stakes for A, not a differentiator.
- Segment B's protein interest is newer and framed around ageing, satiety and menopause; it is less saturated with competing products, so education-led protein messaging ("protein for strength as you age") may differentiate more for B.
- Sports nutrition historically skewed to young men; young women's adoption has grown fast, but the category language (gym, macros) may not resonate with Segment B.

### Gaps
- No 2023-2026 UK figure found for the % of women actively seeking high protein, split by age. Mintel/Kantar full reports are paywalled.
- NDNS protein intake by age/sex for women was not retrieved in this pass.
- No UK study found linking menopause specifically to protein snack purchase.

## 2. Attitudes to ultra-processed food, additives, sweeteners and sugar alcohols; label reading by age

### Takeaway
UPF concern is very high across UK adults (76-78% worried, FSA tracker 2024-25) and rises with age: over-50s are markedly more likely to be "very concerned" (35%) than under-30s (22%). Younger adults are aware of UPF via social media but some report eating more of it. Label reading is higher among women and older adults, though recent UK age-split data was not found.

### Cited Findings
- 76-78% of respondents consistently worried about ultra-processed/over-processed foods, April 2024 to March 2025; UPFs were second only to food prices as a consumer concern (FSA Consumer Insights Tracker, England/Wales/NI; no age split in the summary) — [FoodNavigator, 10 July 2025](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2025/07/10/ultra-processed-foods-dominate-consumer-concerns)
- ~68% of UK shoppers concerned about UPFs, 27% "very concerned"; "very concerned" by age: 35% of over-50s vs 25% of 31-49s vs 22% of under-30s (Ask Attest shopper survey; date not confirmed, likely 2024-25; primary page could not be fetched) — [Ask Attest](https://www.askattest.com/blog/research/ultra-processed-foods-tackling-consumer-confusion.md)
- 60.1% of UK consumers said they had reduced UPF intake in the past year; of these, 75.3% cited health and 42.2% weight management. GlobalData says social media drives gut health, clean eating and UPF conversations, especially among younger consumers (GlobalData, survey of 2,000, August 2025) — [ESM Magazine](https://www.esmmagazine.com/retail/uk-shoppers-cut-down-on-ultra-processed-foods-globaldata-299800); [Asian Trader](https://www.asiantrader.biz/uk-shoppers-cut-ultra-processed-foods)
- Counter-trend: 40% of 18-24s and 42% of 25-34s reported increasing their UPF consumption (Appinio; date not confirmed) — [Appinio](https://www.appinio.com/en-gb/blog/insights/impact-of-ultra-processed-foods)
- 73% of 2,386 UK adults were aware of the term UPF; highest-income and highest-education participants were most likely to be aware of and avoid UPFs (Liverpool John Moores / University of Liverpool study, fieldwork Feb-Apr 2024) — [LJMU Research Online](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/23790/1/Consumer%20Awareness%2C%20Perceptions%20and%20Avoidance%20of%20Ultra-Processed%20Foods%20A%20Study%20of%20UK%20Adults%20in%202024.pdf)
- Mintel: in UK snack bars, preference is shifting to high-protein, high-fibre and natural ingredients, but taste remains the key driver despite UPF concern (Mintel UK Snack Bars report, March 2025) — [Mintel store](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-snack-bars-and-breakfast-biscuits-market-report-2025)
- Label reading: in a study of 573 men and 809 women aged 19-70, women checked label components more often than men, and adults aged 51-70 checked more than younger adults (Journal of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, c.2013; US, OLD) — [Prepared Foods](https://preparedfoods.com/articles/112751-women-read-labels-more)
- Sugar alcohols (e.g. erythritol) are linked to gas, bloating and abdominal pain because they are fermented by gut bacteria; erythritol can have a laxative effect in larger amounts (US poison-control explainer; undated) — [Poison.org](https://poison.org/articles/do-protein-bars-give-you-gas)

### Inferences
- Segment B is the stronger natural fit for an "additive-free / no UPF" proposition: concern rises with age and with income/education, which also aligns with premium price capacity.
- Segment A is aware of UPF (social-media driven) but behaviour is mixed; "clean" claims are a plus but less likely to be the primary purchase trigger versus taste and protein grams.
- "No sugar alcohols / no sweeteners" is a credible, concrete claim that links the UPF and gut angles for both segments.

### Gaps
- Food and You 2 label-reading tables by age/sex were not retrieved; no recent UK age-split for label reading.
- No UK survey found specifically on attitudes to artificial sweeteners or sugar alcohols by age.
- Ask Attest and Appinio survey dates/sample sizes need verification at source.

## 3. Gut health interest; bloating/IBS prevalence among women by age; protein bar bloating discussion

### Takeaway
Digestive complaints are near-universal among UK women and especially acute in midlife: 94% of ~600 UK women aged 44-73 report digestive symptoms, with bloating the most common (77%) and 82% saying symptoms started or worsened with menopause (2025). Women report GI ailments more than men across ages. Evidence of a specific "protein bar bloat" TikTok trend could not be verified in this pass, though the mechanism (sugar alcohols, added fibres) is well documented.

### Cited Findings
- 94% of nearly 600 UK women aged 44-73 reported digestive symptoms: bloating 77%, constipation 54%, stomach pain 50%, acid reflux 49%; 82% said problems started or worsened during menopause; formal diagnosis and effective treatment limited (study presented at The Menopause Society annual meeting, October 2025; lead researcher London dietitian Nigel Denby) — [The Menopause Society](https://menopause.org/press-releases/digestive-health-issues-more-common-during-perimenopause-and-menopause); [Powers Health, 21 Oct 2025](https://www.powershealth.org/about-us/newsroom/health-library/2025/10/21/gi-problems-common-in-menopause-study-says)
- 88% of UK women had experienced some GI ailment in the last year vs 83% of men; Mintel links part of this to menstrual-cycle symptoms (Mintel; release date not confirmed, likely pre-2023) — [Mintel press centre](https://www.mintel.com/press-centre/86-of-brits-have-suffered-from-a-gastrointestinal-problem-in-the-past-year)
- Mintel's 2025 UK Digestive Health report projects OTC GI remedy sales of £495m (+8.7% YoY) and flags "hormone bloating" for women as an opportunity (Mintel, 2025) — [Mintel store](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-digestive-health-market-report)
- University of Glasgow online questionnaire of 516 UK adults (85% female): 86% had GI symptoms in the past month, including flatulence (72%) and bloating (64%); food avoidance was more common among those with IBS (78%) (date c.2025; small, self-selected sample) — [University of Glasgow eprints](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/362554)
- Women are about twice as likely as men to live with IBS; 62% of respondents reported weekly "off days", rising to 73% among those with IBS (Symprove-commissioned survey reported by Stylist; date unclear, industry-commissioned) — [Stylist](https://www.stylist.co.uk/fitness-health/wellbeing/ibs-poor-gut-health-women-bloating/652251)
- UK consumers increasingly associate gut health with anti-bloating, but overall UK gut-health interest is reportedly cooling about 6% per year (Tastewise data, DairyReporter, 15 Sept 2026; platform data, not a representative survey) — [DairyReporter](https://www.dairyreporter.com/Article/2026/09/15/gut-health-consumer-expectations/)
- GlobalData says social media is driving gut health conversation, especially among younger consumers (Aug 2025) — [ESM Magazine](https://www.esmmagazine.com/retail/uk-shoppers-cut-down-on-ultra-processed-foods-globaldata-299800)
- Protein bars and gas/bloating: sugar alcohols are the most commonly cited cause; lactose, fructose and some sweeteners (stevia, monk fruit) can also contribute; protein itself is rarely the culprit (brand/explainer content; undated, not peer-reviewed) — [Poison.org](https://poison.org/articles/do-protein-bars-give-you-gas); [Chief Nutrition](https://wearechief.com/blogs/articles/why-do-protein-bars-make-you-fart)

### Inferences
- "Gentle on the gut / no bloat" is highly relevant to Segment B, where bloating prevalence is documented and linked to menopause, and where few targeted snack products exist.
- For Segment A, bloating is also common (Mintel menstrual-linked complaints; Glasgow sample mostly female), and "no protein-bar bloat" is a plausible hook, but the strength of the specific social-media conversation is unverified.
- Tastewise's "cooling interest" signal suggests gut health as a generic claim may be wearing out; a concrete, symptom-led claim ("no bloat", "no sugar alcohols") is likely stronger than "gut health".

### Gaps
- No verified data on the scale of TikTok/Instagram "protein bar bloat" discussion (view counts, hashtags); searches returned only brand explainers.
- No large, representative 2023-2026 UK survey of bloating/IBS prevalence by age among women (the Rome Foundation global epidemiology study would be the next source; not retrieved).

## 4. Gym participation, strength training/Pilates trends among young UK women; social media influence

### Takeaway
Women's fitness participation in England is growing faster than sport (+1.7% vs +0.7%, 2024-25), Gen Z are a core gym demographic (c.40% of The Gym Group members), and Pilates/strength are the fastest-growing formats. Sex-disaggregated UK Gen Z data was not found; most figures are operator- or vendor-reported.

### Cited Findings
- Number of women in fitness activities rose 1.7% between 2024 and 2025 vs 0.7% for sport (Sport England data cited by ukactive in parliamentary written evidence, 2025) — [UK Parliament written evidence BPS0039](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/166594/html/)
- Girls' gym and fitness participation up 11.4% since 2018 (ukactive citing Sport England; secondhand, trade body) — [UK Parliament written evidence BPS0039](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/166594/html/)
- Gen Z make up about 40% of The Gym Group's UK members; membership up 5% YoY at H1 2025; Gen Z spend an average £48.81/month on memberships, apps and equipment (company-reported, 2025) — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/gym-group-reports-gen-z-strong-growth/); [The Gym Group Gen Z report 2025](https://www.tggplc.com/media/4luo4kpk/tgg-pr-gen-z-survey-report-2025.pdf)
- 44% of Gen Z rank fitness as their first or second discretionary spending priority (Gen Z survey cited in Les Mills/Gym Group coverage, 2025) — [The Gym Group Gen Z report 2025](https://www.tggplc.com/media/4luo4kpk/tgg-pr-gen-z-survey-report-2025.pdf)
- 36% of Gen Z go to the gym; 27% do studio workouts twice a week; 40% work out at home (Unidays Q1 2025, cited by Les Mills; likely UK student base) — [Les Mills](https://www.lesmills.com/clubs-and-facilities/research-insights/research/wellness-wrapped-fresh-research-reveals-the-2025-member-mindset)
- Pilates bookings up 84% on 2023 and the most-booked workout of 2024; low-impact training bookings up 109% (Les Mills; vendor data, likely global) — [Les Mills](https://www.lesmills.com/clubs-and-facilities/research-insights/research/wellness-wrapped-fresh-research-reveals-the-2025-member-mindset)
- Gen Z women are more engaged with sport than previous generations (EY Sports Engagement Index, reported by Health Club Management) — [Health Club Management](https://www.healthclubmanagement.co.uk/health-club-management-news/Gen-Z-women-are-more-engaged-with-sport-than-previous-generations-finds-EY-Sports-Engagement-Index/353334)

### Inferences
- Segment A is a large, growing, high-spending fitness audience that is easy to reach via gyms, studios and social media; the Pilates boom also bridges toward a "wellness" rather than "bodybuilding" aesthetic, which fits pastel premium branding.
- Strength training plus Pilates suggests young women want protein framed as "strong, not bulky" and wellness-adjacent.

### Gaps
- No Sport England Active Lives breakdown of strength training by sex and age 18-26 retrieved.
- No quantitative data found on TikTok/Instagram "gym girl" content influencing UK women's food purchases.

## 5. Price sensitivity, willingness to pay, snacking occasions and "sweet treat" behaviour

### Takeaway
About half of UK consumers say they would pay more for healthier snack attributes (52%, early 2026), and the youngest adults snack most often (79% of 16-24s at least daily vs 62% of over-55s). Age-split willingness-to-pay data was not found; inference is that Segment B has more disposable income and Segment A higher snacking frequency.

### Cited Findings
- 52% of UK consumers would pay more for healthier snack attributes like better quality ingredients despite cost-of-living pressures (attributed to Mintel; Speciality Food, 20 January 2026; attribution not independently confirmed) — [Speciality Food](https://www.specialityfoodmagazine.com/food-and-drink/healthy-snacks-uk)
- 79% of 16-24s snack once a day or more vs 62% of snackers over 55; 25-34s are the age group most engaged with healthy snacking (The Grocer; date not confirmed) — [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/analysis-and-features/nutritional-needs-taste-perceptions-and-natural-protein-10-charts-explaining-uk-attitudes-to-healthy-snacking/605136.article)
- Mintel UK Consumer Snacking 2025 report based on 2,000 internet users aged 16+, May 2025 (age-split WTP not visible in listing) — [Mintel store](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-consumer-snacking-market-report)
- UK snack bars market recovered to over £700m in 2024; forecast £845m by 2029 (Mintel, March 2025) — [Mintel store](https://store.mintel.com/report/uk-snack-bars-and-breakfast-biscuits-market-report-2025)
- Historic: 22% would pay more for all-natural snacks, 19% for low calories, 18% for nutritional benefits (Mintel, c.2016; OLD) — [Mintel press centre](https://www.mintel.com/press-centre/brits-relax-on-snacks-consumers-cut-down-on-snacking-as-less-than-a-third-29-now-eat-sweets-between-meals)
- GlobalData: 42.2% of those cutting UPFs cited weight management (Aug 2025) — [ESM Magazine](https://www.esmmagazine.com/retail/uk-shoppers-cut-down-on-ultra-processed-foods-globaldata-299800)

### Inferences
- Segment A: high snacking frequency and high fitness spend (£48.81/month) but student/early-career budgets; likely to trade between premium bars and cheaper mainstream protein bars (multi-packs, discounters).
- Segment B: fewer snacking occasions but higher income and stronger UPF concern, and UPF avoidance skews to higher income/education, so premium "clean" pricing is more defensible.
- "Sweet treat with benefits" occasions (afternoon slump, post-gym) are plausible for both, but no UK data quantified them by age.

### Gaps
- No UK 2023-2026 data on willingness to pay for premium/natural snacks split by age or gender.
- No data quantifying "sweet treat" snacking occasions by age among women.

## 6. What each segment dislikes about current protein bars

### Takeaway
Taste and texture (chalky, grainy, hard) are the most-cited dislikes; digestive discomfort from sugar alcohols and perceptions that bars are "candy in disguise" or over-processed are secondary. Almost all evidence is US or industry-sourced; no UK age-split survey was found.

### Cited Findings
- Top texture complaints are "grainy", "hard" and "chalky"; casein linked to chalky/dry texture, plant protein to grittiness and bitter/beany notes (Glanbia Nutritionals, supplier; US-focused) — [Glanbia Nutritionals](https://www.glanbianutritionals.com/en/node/1031)
- 64% of US bar consumers say texture is moderately or very important when choosing a protein bar (Glanbia survey; US) — [NutritionInsight](https://www.nutritioninsight.com/news/consumers-seek-health-and-indulgence-balance-in-protein-bars-glanbia-nutritionals-exec-details.html)
- After price, taste is the largest bar purchase barrier; nearly one in three non-buyers say bars don't taste good (Mintel, US; date unclear) — [Bakery and Snacks, 24 June 2021](https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2021/06/24/Formulating-the-nutrition-bars-that-consumers-crave)
- A brand campaign claims protein bar shoppers feel bars under-deliver on taste (brand-commissioned; US) — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/370279/one-bar-asks-why-eat-chalky-protein-bars.html)
- Commercial high-protein bars are not as healthy as consumers perceive, and protein needs can be met through a varied diet (safefood, Ireland/NI public body; date not confirmed) — [safefood Protein Bar report](https://www.safefood.net/getmedia/eda09a49-074c-458f-845a-c80d87a666f6/Protein-bar-report.aspx?ext=.pdf)
- Dietitians note some bars contain sugar comparable to sweets and recommend low added sugar, recognisable ingredients and some fibre (US; undated) — [Rescripted](https://rescripted.com/spotlight_items/are-protein-bars-just-candy-in-disguise)
- Sugar alcohols in bars linked to gas and bloating (undated explainer) — [Poison.org](https://poison.org/articles/do-protein-bars-give-you-gas)

### Inferences
- Segment A likely prioritises taste/indulgence and protein grams; dislikes probably centre on chalkiness, aftertaste and "boring" flavours, with bloating a secondary pain point.
- Segment B likely dislikes long ingredient lists, sweeteners, "gym-bro" branding and digestive side effects; taste/texture still matter but "natural" credibility is the gatekeeper.
- A bar that solves taste/texture without sugar alcohols directly addresses the most-cited complaints across both segments.

### Gaps
- No UK survey of protein bar dislikes split by age or gender found; the best route would be primary research (survey, review mining of UK Amazon/Tesco reviews, or TikTok comment analysis).
- Calorie and sugar-content complaints by segment not quantified.
