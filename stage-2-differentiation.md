## 4. Recommended hero: the dose-to-bodyweight snap bar

### What it is
An ~85–90 g whole-food recovery bar moulded into **4 snap segments**. Each segment carries a fixed dose. The wrapper chart says **how many segments your body weight needs**.

**Per-segment target (updated 6 Oct for higher fibre; see `findings-report.md` §C):** ~5 g protein · ~10 g carbs · **~2.7 g fibre** · ~86 kcal. **Whole bar (~98 g):** ~20 g P · ~42 g C · **~11 g fibre (incl. ~2 g oat beta-glucan)** · ~345 kcal.

### The dose maths (target 0.25–0.33 g protein/kg, evidence 0.25–0.31) [V for the range; bands are my design]

| Body weight | Segments | Protein | g/kg at band edges | Example athlete |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≤ 60 kg | 3 | 15 g | 0.25 (at 60 kg) | Distance runner |
| 61–75 kg | 4 (whole bar) | 20 g | 0.33 → 0.27 | Midfielder, cyclist |
| 76–90 kg | 5 (bar + 1) | 25 g | 0.33 → 0.28 | Centre-back, rower |
| 91–105 kg | 6 (bar + 2) | 30 g | 0.33 → 0.29 | Rugby forward |

Every band falls inside ~0.25–0.33 g/kg. **One product covers the 60 kg runner and the 100 kg forward**, which resolves your "both solo and team" requirement.

**Carbs, honestly:** fast glycogen refuelling needs far more carbohydrate than any bar provides. The GB-authorised claim itself says 4 g/kg within 4–6 h, i.e. 280 g for a 70 kg athlete. The bar **doses protein precisely and kick-starts carbs**. The QR calculator tells athletes what to add (e.g. "+ 500 ml milk + banana"). Turning this mandatory claim wording into a useful tool is a regulatory burden made into a feature.

### Why it wins on the DVS judging criteria

| Criterion | How the hero feature helps |
|---|---|
| Problem & market research | Ties to a researched number (0.3 g/kg), so you can show that **Styrkr's 15 g and Forza's 12 g under-dose most adult men** (75 kg × 0.3 = 22.5 g) [V maths] |
| Innovation & value proposition | Visible, demonstrable, not found in the UK market [A – keep checking]. You can **snap it on stage** |
| Feasibility & business model | Kitchen-feasible with a segmented silicone mould (~£20–40 [A]). One SKU serves all sizes. Bigger athletes eat 1.5 bars, which raises revenue per athlete |
| Team capability | Comes straight from your coaching: you've watched a 60 kg winger and a 100 kg prop get the same handout |
| Impact & scale | Precision dosing reduces waste and overconsumption. The approach scales to youth or masters ranges and other countries |
| Pitch & communication | One-line hook: **"Every athlete gets the same bar. No athlete is the same size."** |

### Risks and how to test them (Weeks 6–9)

| Risk | Test | Pass threshold [A – set by you] |
|---|---|---|
| Athletes don't care about precision ("I just eat the bar") | Interview Q: "How do you decide how much to eat after training?" Then show a mock-up | ≥50% of interviewees say they'd use the chart |
| Leftover segments get messy or go stale | Pilot: count how many leftover segments are actually eaten later; resealable wrapper vs 2 × half-bar packs | ≥70% of leftovers eaten within 24 h |
| Segments break unevenly | Kitchen trials with 2 mould designs | Segment weight within ±10% |
| Someone has done it | Keep searching UK IPO designs, Amazon, US/AU markets | No direct equivalent before Week 10 |
| Claim risk in the chart wording | Use dose language only ("protein per segment"), no benefit language. Check with Newcastle Trading Standards (Stage 3) | Written OK, or wording adjusted |
