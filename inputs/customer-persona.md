## Experts

| ID | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Strength & conditioning coach, Team Durham | Athletes are advised to eat whole foods. Reckons a good-texture, good-tasting bar with a high protein:carb ratio and some fibre for digestion would be ideal. Said students were once given boxes upon boxes of Grenade Oreo protein bars, and the students really struggled to eat them |
| E2 | Professor (nutrition) | Aim for about 100 kcal for every 10 g of protein, with quality protein sources, and high carbs for post-exercise recovery |
| E3 | Professor, sport & exercise nutrition, Durham University (added 19 Oct 2026; may be the same person as E2, to be confirmed by Ewan) | Athletes find it a challenge to get some sort of food in after training; all sports need some food after training, and she has done many projects on this. Refrigeration could be an issue: something that can stay in a bag all day is "defo the way". Allergies could be an issue. Likes the concept: she admires the idea if all the boxes can be ticked (a good protein level, carbs etc.). Stressed the importance of carbs, which get a bad rep. Current protein bars don't taste like real food and are hard to digest; she called them "dense". Existing options don't show obvious dissatisfaction. Protein is "blasted all over shop floors". Uni athletes are a good target. Advised speaking to people in food: manufacturers and feasibility professionals |
