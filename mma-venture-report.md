**Reflexivity (bias in the founder's data)**
- **Convenience sample:** all 10 customer interviews come from Ewan's own network (gym, sport, university).
- **0 of 10 customers are MMA fighters.** The persona is therefore a hypothesis, not a finding.
- **Self-selection:** YouTube polls reach people who already watch an MMA channel. They over-represent fans and under-represent fighters who don't watch YouTube.
- **Founder-as-customer:** Ewan has the problem himself. That is a strength for insight and a risk for confirmation bias ("everyone is like me").
- **Interview notes are paraphrases.** They are never presented here as quotes.
- **Fiction:** Section G hypotheses come from a fictional practice set. They are things to test, never evidence.

## 1. Executive summary (≤300 words)

**Problem.** Amateur MMA fighters often train twice a day around work or study. Between and after sessions they need food that carries protein *and* carbohydrate, survives a kit bag, and doesn't upset the stomach before sparring. Today they choose between lab-style protein bars (built on sweeteners and polyols, low in carbs), sugary sports bars, or nothing. `[ASSUMPTION — 0/10 interviewees are fighters]`

**Customer.** UK amateur and semi-pro MMA athletes doing double sessions, especially lighter divisions and fighters in camp.

**Why it could be a migraine.** MMA athletes under-eat carbohydrate (~3.3 g/kg/day vs 6–10 g/kg guidance) `[EVIDENCE: Bueno et al. 2024, B]` and almost all cut weight `[EVIDENCE: Hillier et al. 2019, B]`. **But** the expert view (E3) is that existing options "don't show obvious dissatisfaction", so pain is unproven.

**Gap.** Of 21 UK products checked on five tests, **none** combined ≥10 g protein, ≥20 g carbs, ≤5 g sugar, low saturated fat and no sweeteners or polyols with whole-food protein `[EVIDENCE: founder audit + repo matrix, D]`.

**Why now.** UPF concern is mainstream (~78% concerned; FSA tracker, March 2025, `[B]`); the market leader's (Grenade's) unit sales fell 15.9% in 2025 as protein bars came under public scrutiny (Channel 4, Oct 2025; causation contested) `[C]`; UFC has just launched its own bar, but in the US only `[D]`.

**Why Ewan.** A fighter with ~25k YouTube subscribers, gym access, and 13 evidence-driven recipe iterations.

**Riskiest assumption:** *fighters feel this gap strongly enough to change what they do.*
**Test this month:** 8–10 Mom Test interviews with MMA fighters at the gym, plus the Tuesday QR survey. **Pass:** ≥6/10 describe the post-session food problem unprompted **and** ≥4/10 already spend money or effort on a workaround. **Fail:** reposition (e.g. towards BJJ or university athletes) before Week 13.

## B. The need

### B1. Post-exercise fuelling guidance

| Guidance | Content | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily carbohydrate | 3–5 g/kg (moderate), 5–7 (moderate–high), 6–10 (high volume), 8–12 (extreme) | Thomas, Erdman and Burke, 2016 (ACSM/AND/DC) | A |
| Rapid refuelling (<8 h between sessions) | 1.0–1.2 g/kg/h carbohydrate for the first 4 h | Thomas, Erdman and Burke, 2016; Kerksick et al., 2017 | A |
| Protein per feed | ~0.25–0.3 g/kg (≈20–25 g for a 70–80 kg athlete) | Jäger et al., 2017; Moore, 2019 | A / A |
| Daily protein | 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day for most exercising people | Jäger et al., 2017 | A |
| Combat sports specifically | Individualised; carbohydrate periodised to training load; post-weigh-in refuelling | Reale, Slater and Burke, 2017 | A (review) |

**What this means for a 50 g bar (15 g protein, 22.5 g carbs):** for a 70 kg fighter between sessions, one bar gives ~0.21 g/kg protein (just below a full feed) and ~0.3 g/kg carbs (a quarter to a third of the first hour's target). **It is a bridge, not a full refuel.** Say so; it's honest and it sells a 2-bar pack.

### B2. Weight management in MMA
- Rapid weight loss is near-universal: ~97% of male and 100% of female MMA athletes, mostly in the final week, with coaches as the main influence `[EVIDENCE: Hillier et al., 2019, B]`.
- Extreme rapid weight loss and regain has been documented in UK MMA athletes, though in small samples `[EVIDENCE: Matthews and Nicholas, 2017, B (small n)]`.
- Fighters regain substantial mass after weigh-in (average >8% in Californian data on 512 pro fighters) `[EVIDENCE: CSAC data via Combat Sports Law, 2021, E/D (grey data)]`.
- Methods (dehydration, food restriction, sauna) and harms (impaired performance, kidney stress, deaths in extreme cases) are reviewed in `[EVIDENCE: Barley, Chapman and Abbiss, 2019, A (narrative review)]`.
- **Out of camp:** chronic leanness and low carbohydrate intake are plausible but under-measured in UK amateurs `[GAP: no UK out-of-camp intake study found]`.

### B3. Low energy availability (REDs)
- The IOC 2023 consensus applies REDs to males as well as females and flags weight-class sports as high risk `[EVIDENCE: Mountjoy et al., 2023, A]`.
- MMA carbohydrate intake (~3.3 g/kg/day) sits below the guideline range for heavy training `[EVIDENCE: Bueno et al., 2024, B]`. A 2025 review agrees (as supplied) `[not re-checked]`.
- **Caution:** don't claim the product prevents REDs. That is a disease-risk-style claim with no authorisation, and one snack cannot do it.

### B4. GI tolerance
- GI complaints are common in athletes. High fibre, fat, protein and FODMAPs (which include polyols) close to exercise are risk factors `[EVIDENCE: de Oliveira, Burini and Jeukendrup, 2014, A (review; older landmark, still the standard reference)]`, `[EVIDENCE: Monash FODMAP, D]`.
- **GB law:** foods with **>10% added polyols** must carry *"excessive consumption may produce laxative effects"* `[EVIDENCE: Reg. (EU) 1169/2011 Annex III as retained, B (legislation)]`. 5 leading UK bars carry it (founder audit, D).
- **Grappling or sparring after eating:** no study found on eating before or after grappling `[GAP: searched in the repo literature and PubMed terms "grappling" + "gastrointestinal"]`. **This is a primary-research opportunity:** a 2-week gut-comfort diary with 10 fighters.
- **Own product risk:** egg white, oats and puffed rice are low-FODMAP in normal portions `[ASSUMPTION — check Monash app]`. Oat fibre could still bother some people near sparring.

### B5. Supplement contamination and anti-doping
- UKAD: athletes are strictly liable; no supplement is guaranteed contamination-free; use batch-tested products `[EVIDENCE: UKAD, D (regulator guidance)]`.
- The IOC consensus on supplements describes contamination risk and recommends a food-first approach `[EVIDENCE: Maughan et al., 2018, A]`.
- **Relevance:** most UK amateur MMA fighters are not in a UKAD testing pool `[ASSUMPTION]`. So H4 is a **small** segment (semi-pro and pro). "Food-first" may *feel* safer, but a home-baked bar is not tested either. **Don't claim "safer for drug testing".**

### B6. Status quo: what UK fighters eat now
- Peer-reviewed: dietary intake data exist (Bueno et al., 2024, mixed sample) but no UK study of *post-session* snacks `[GAP]`.
- Media, podcasts and YouTube content: not systematically reviewed `[GAP]`. **Action:** Ewan should code 20 "what I eat in a day" videos from UK fighters (grade E) as a research video (L3).
- Observed by founder: gyms stock Grenade-type bars `[E, single observer]`.

### B7. Migraine test

| For "must-have" | Against ("nice-to-have") |
|---|---|
| Double sessions + weight management + low carb intake = a real physiological gap (A/B evidence) | **E3: existing options "don't show obvious dissatisfaction"** (E) |
| Workarounds exist (cooking, bananas, shakes): a sign of an unmet job | Only 3/10 interviewees mention price, 3/10 taste. **No interviewee described spending money to solve this** |
| C7 and C10 describe no time to cook around training (E) | Cheap substitutes exist (banana + milk at <£1) |
| Students struggled to eat boxed Grenade bars (E1, E) | 0/10 interviewees are fighters, so the pain in the target group is untested |

**Verdict today: unproven, probably a "vitamin" for most gym-goers and possibly a "painkiller" for fighters in camp.** The Mom Test interviews (L1) decide it. In the pitch, say exactly this; judges reward it.

**So what for the pitch:** the science gives a credible *reason* for the problem; only fighter interviews can show the *pain*.
**Confidence:** medium for the physiology, low for the felt pain.
**Evidence mix:** A ×6, B ×4, D ×2, E ×4.

### I1. Ambient stability

| Product type | Typical water activity (aw) | Source | Grade |

**Pathogen and spoilage thresholds (widely used food-safety values)**
- Most pathogenic bacteria stop growing below **~0.91–0.94**; *Staphylococcus aureus* can grow down to **~0.86** (toxin production needs higher). `[EVIDENCE: FSS shelf-life guidance, 2025, B — check the exact table]`
- Most moulds stop below **~0.80**; xerophilic moulds can grow down to **~0.61**. Below **0.60**, no microbial growth `[EVIDENCE: standard food-microbiology values; confirm against FSS/Campden BRI, B]`.

**What this means for v13 (~16% moisture):** moisture % does not equal aw. A low-sugar egg-white bar at 16% moisture could plausibly sit anywhere from **~0.55 to ~0.80** `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- **1 day ambient:** likely acceptable if aw < 0.85 and handled hygienically `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- **1 week:** needs aw ≤ ~0.70 and good packaging `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- **1 month:** needs aw ≤ ~0.60–0.65 plus a challenge or storage trial `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Only a lab test can confirm any of this.** Until then, say "recipe in testing" and give pilot bars a short use-by (e.g. 3 days) with a hygiene plan.

- **Register with Durham County Council (or the council where the kitchen is) at least 28 days before starting**: free; applies to home kitchens and online sales `[EVIDENCE: FSA campaign and council notices, B]`.
- **Free samples / pilot:** registration is triggered by a food business, which includes activities "whether for profit or not" with continuity and organisation `[EVIDENCE: Reg. 178/2002 definition, as retained, B]`. A **repeated gym pilot** is very likely a food business, even if free. A one-off tasting among friends is a grey area. **Ask Durham Environmental Health in writing.** `[GAP: no FSA statement on free samples found today]`
- **Training:** Level 2 Food Hygiene (online, ~£15–25) `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- **HACCP:** use FSA **MyHACCP** (free) or "Safer Food, Better Business". Key hazards: raw egg (*Salmonella*) → validated bake temperature; post-bake contamination; moisture/aw → mould.
- **Home kitchen:** allowed if registered and inspected; allergen control is needed (separate equipment, cleaning).

### J2. Claims eligibility for v13 (as formulated, calculated)

v13 per 100 g (×2 of per-50 g): **protein 30 g, carbs 45 g, sugar 5.8 g, fat 2.8 g, ~330 kcal**. Thresholds are from the Annex to Reg. (EC) 1924/2006 as retained in GB.

| Claim | Condition | v13 | Eligible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source of protein | ≥12% of energy from protein | 15 × 4 / 165 = **36%** | ✓ |
| High protein | ≥20% of energy from protein | 36% | **✓** |
| Low sugars | ≤5 g/100 g (solids) | **5.8 g/100 g** | **✗** (fails by 0.8 g) |
| Sugars-free | ≤0.5 g/100 g | — | ✗ |
| No added sugars | No added mono/disaccharides or sugar-containing food used for sweetness | Contains honey | ✗ |
| Low fat | ≤3 g/100 g | 2.8 g/100 g (calc.) | ✓ (borderline; needs lab) |
| Low saturated fat | Sat fat ≤1.5 g/100 g **and** ≤10% of energy | Estimated ~0.3–0.5 g/100 g `[ASSUMPTION]` | ✓ likely (needs lab) |
| Source of fibre | ≥3 g/100 g or ≥1.5 g/100 kcal | Estimated ~3 g/100 g `[ASSUMPTION]` | Borderline |
| High fibre | ≥6 g/100 g or ≥3 g/100 kcal | — | ✗ |
| Protein health claims (Reg. 432/2012) | e.g. "Protein contributes to the maintenance of muscle mass"; food must at least be a source of protein | ✓ | **✓ (authorised wording only)** |
| Carbohydrate / muscle-function claim | Carbs contribute to the recovery of normal muscle function after highly intensive and/or long-lasting exercise; must state 4 g/kg in the first 4 h and no later than 6 h; polyols excluded | 22.5 g/bar | Legally possible but awkward; **don't use** |

**Fix for "low sugar":** cut or remove the honey (8 g honey ≈ 6.5 g sugar per batch ≈ 1.6 g per bar). Removing it should bring sugar to ~1.3 g/50 g ≈ 2.6 g/100 g `[ASSUMPTION: oats and rice contribute the rest]`. **Confirm by lab analysis before claiming.**

**Non-claims:** "No artificial sweeteners" and "no sugar alcohols" are voluntary statements. They're allowed if true and not misleading (Reg. 1169/2011 Art. 7), but **don't imply other products are unsafe**.

**Compliant wording (pitch, landing page, pack)**

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "Recovery bar", "speeds recovery", "repairs muscle" | "An after-training snack" · "Protein contributes to the maintenance of muscle mass" (pack only, once lab-verified) |
| "Fuel", "boosts energy" | "Carbohydrate from oats and wholegrain rice" (a factual statement) |
| "Gut-friendly", "won't upset your stomach" | "No artificial sweeteners or sugar alcohols" |
| "Low sugar" (currently ineligible) | "Around 3 g sugar per bar" **only after lab analysis**; "recipe in testing" until then |
| "Safe for drug-tested athletes" | Nothing. Not until Informed Sport certification |
| "Prevents REDs" / "healthy weight cut" | Nothing; disease-risk-style claims are not authorised |

### J3. Labelling (prepacked food)
Mandatory: name of the food; ingredients in descending order; **allergens emphasised** (bold) in the list (egg; oats → "**oats** (gluten)", with the cereal named); quantity of ingredients if emphasised (QUID, e.g. "egg white 60%"); net weight; best-before or use-by; storage conditions; business name and UK address; nutrition declaration (energy, fat, saturates, carbohydrate, sugars, protein, salt per 100 g). **PPDS (Natasha's Law)** applies only if packed on the premises where it's sold (e.g. made and sold at the same gym or kitchen). "May contain" statements are voluntary and should follow a real risk assessment (FSA precautionary allergen labelling guidance).

### J4. Nutrition testing
- Calculated values (e.g. from McCance and Widdowson's composition data) are legally allowed for the declaration if done properly; **for claims, lab analysis is safer** `[ASSUMPTION — confirm with Trading Standards]`.
- UKAS-accredited labs (RSSL: 7-day turnaround) offer nutrition panels; **no UK price found today** `[GAP]`. The US benchmark of ~$180–900 is from an unverified source (E). **Budget £150–400** `[ASSUMPTION]` and get 3 quotes.

## Update log (20 Oct 2026, after source-library Batches 1–17)

Corrections and additions from `source-library/` (see `RUN-LOG.md` for source IDs):
1. **Grenade decline:** don't attribute it to the documentary. Channel 4's *Joe Wicks: Licensed to Kill* (6 Oct 2025) put protein bars under scrutiny, but causation is contested (B3-007/008).
2. **Gut argument is overstated.** One lab bar (13–18 g polyols) is far below maltitol laxative thresholds (~60–92 g). Use "no sugar alcohols" plus the laxative-warning fact, and test H2 in interviews (B10-003).
3. **Between-session science favours carbs.** Protein doesn't speed glycogen refill, and intake should start immediately (B9-001/007/008). Consider a 2-bar session pack or a higher-carb between-session variant.
4. **Carb guidance:** say "well below the 3–10 g/kg/day range most athletes are advised" until the ACSM 6–10 band is checked (B9-003).
5. **RXBAR UK precedent:** Kellogg launched it via D2C and 200+ gyms (2018), then exited within ~2 years. Prepare the judge answer (B12-009 to B12-011).
6. **JNCK:** liquidated after a Tesco *listing*; a delisting is unconfirmed (B12-008).
7. **BJJ:** 1,000+ clubs, ~100k participants (UKBJJA 2025 AGM estimate) (B5-006).
8. **Performance angle:** drop it. Moderate cuts don't impair performance, and regain doesn't predict winning (B7-007/008).
9. **Shelf life:** add texture tracking (egg-white hardening) and a packaging barrier; target aw ≤0.65 (B15-007/010).
10. **Free samples:** FSA says free supply can make you a food business; register before a regular pilot (B17-005).
