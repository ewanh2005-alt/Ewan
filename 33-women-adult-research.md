## Women's current issues (secondary)
| Issue | What the evidence says | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| **Taste comes first** | 66% of women name taste as important in protein bars or shakes, vs 52% of men | `[EVIDENCE: WA1, Grocer]` |
| **Many don't use protein bars at all** | 25% of women never have a protein bar or shake for workouts, vs 14% of men. Only 14% of women (10% of 55–64s) ate protein bars weekly | `[EVIDENCE: WA1; WR57 (>5 yrs)]` |
| **Marketing feels male** | Protein historically marketed to men for muscle; Myprotein calls breaking that stereotype a priority. Brands sell near-identical powders to women on "weight loss/toning" | `[EVIDENCE: WA2, WA3]` (industry voices, grade D) |
| **Competitor already moving** | **Bio&Me launched women-targeted protein and fibre bars (Oct 2025)**, positioned as a "less male-oriented alternative"; 9 g protein per 40 g bar | `[EVIDENCE: WA4, brand claim]` |
| **Interest in protein is rising among women** | Women are now **51%** of consumers globally seeking more protein (Euromonitor, Sept 2025). UK 16–24 women using protein powder rose from 7% to 18% (2015–17) | `[EVIDENCE: WA5 (global); WA6 (>5 yrs)]` |
| **Strength training is growing, mostly in younger women** | 13% of female gym members name muscle building as their main goal; 25% of strength-training women are 25–34 (YouGov via Jetts) | `[EVIDENCE: WA7, operator press release]` |
| **More women in fitness** | Women in fitness activity +1.7% (2024–25) vs +0.7% for sport; +12% using leisure centres (ukactive citing Sport England) | `[EVIDENCE: WA8]` |
| **Stomach comfort** | Sugar alcohols are commonly linked to bloating and gas (general, not women-specific) | `[EVIDENCE: 25-secondary-research.md, M24–M28]` |
| **UPF: confusion, not just concern** | One survey found women more likely than men not to know what "ultra-processed" means (45% vs 33%). No sex breakdown found in the FSA tracker | `[EVIDENCE: WA9]`; `[GAP]` |
| **Life-stage needs (40–60)** | Protein needs rise around menopause, but women feel this as a slow worry, not a daily pain. A "vitamin", not a "migraine" | `[EVIDENCE: 02d, WR19, WR70]` |
| Women's protein intake vs need (UK NDNS) | Not found in this search | `[GAP]` |

**So what:** women's problem with current bars is **taste and a male-coded, "lab" image**, not an urgent unmet need. A great-tasting natural bar fits, but **Bio&Me is already targeting women**, and the need is a nice-to-have unless tied to a specific moment (after strength training, busy workdays).

## Recommendation
**Lead with MMA fighters, and keep women as the scale story.**
- MMA wins on 5 of 6 criteria because of Ewan's unfair advantage and the sharper evidence. Women win only on market size.
- In the pitch: "Start with MMA fighters, where I have access and they accept function over taste; then expand to **women who strength-train**, a fast-growing group that dislikes male-coded, sweetened bars."
- **A bridge niche worth testing:** **women in combat sports and strength training** (women's MMA classes, BJJ, lifting). It combines both findings, and Ewan's gym has access.

## How to decide with data (2 weeks)
1. Run the landing page with **two versions** (fighters vs women who train), using tracking links. Compare sign-ups and the share who'd pre-order.
2. **Women interviews (Mom Test, 6–8):** "What did you eat after your last workout?" "Have you ever stopped buying a protein bar? Why?" "What would make you trust a bar?" Record each person's age, activity and current snack.
3. Get exact counts from the 8 fighters ("current options are fine": how many?).
4. Choose the segment with **more commitments**, not more compliments.
