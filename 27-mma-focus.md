## What MMA fighters may NOT already have (hypotheses, ranked)
Every one of these is a **`[ASSUMPTION]` until fighters confirm it.**

1. **A snack between double sessions that doesn't sit heavy on the mats.** Being crushed in BJJ or wrestling on a full stomach is miserable. Bars with sugar alcohols can bloat (polyol laxative warning, `25-secondary-research.md`). Fit: a light, real-food bar with no polyols.
2. **A fight-camp snack with exact, trustworthy macros for making weight.** Fighters weigh everything. A fixed 50 g portion with known protein and calories, and no "hidden" sugar, fits strict cutting. Fit: the bag bar's weighed portions.
3. **Carbs without guilt.** Evidence suggests fighters under-eat carbs (X3). A snack built around oats and rice plus protein could fill that gap. That matches the sport nutrition professor's "carbs get a bad rep" point.
4. **A low-risk option for tested fighters.** Real food (egg, oats, rice) carries no protein-powder contamination worry. Careful: don't claim "doping-safe". It's just "no supplement powders".
5. **A post-weigh-in refuel snack:** easy-to-digest carbs plus protein for the 24 hours between weigh-in and fight. This is a different product, so park it unless fighters bring it up.

**Recommendation:** test #1 + #2 together as the core idea: *"a real-food snack you can eat between sessions in fight camp without feeling heavy, with exact macros for making weight."* Let the interviews decide.

## How to find out exactly what they want (primary, this week)
### A. YouTube Community poll (fast, big sample, but fans ≠ fighters)
Post these in order. The first one filters the audience.
1. "Do you train MMA / BJJ / boxing? Compete · Train, don't compete · Just watch"
2. "Between two training sessions, what do you usually eat? Nothing · Protein bar · Proper meal · Shake · Fruit/other"
3. "Ever felt sick or heavy rolling/sparring after eating? Often · Sometimes · Never"
4. "In fight camp, what's hardest about food? Making weight · Time to cook · Feeling heavy training · Cost"

Polls allow 4–5 options. Screenshot the results with the date and vote count. **They are self-selected**, so report them as an indication, not proof.

### B. 8–10 interviews at the MMA gym (the real evidence)
Use the Mom Test (`19-mom-test-questions.md`): past behaviour, not opinions about your idea.
- "Walk me through a double-session day in your last fight camp. What did you eat, and when?"
- "When did you last feel bad training because of what you'd eaten? What was it?"
- "How do you make weight? Who tells you what to eat?"
- "What do you eat between sessions now? What do you hate about it?"
- "What have you tried that didn't work? Why did you stop?"
- "How much did you spend on food and supplements in your last camp?"
- **Only at the end:** show the bar and let them taste it. "Would you take it to training tomorrow?" Ask them to **pre-order or join the list** (that's commitment, not compliments).

Write each one up like E1–E3 (paraphrased, never invented). Count the themes in code (`research/17_analyse_responses.py`).

## USP proposal (21 Oct 2026, to test, not proven)
**Recommended USP:** *the fight-camp snack, made by a fighter, in two versions matched to your training day.*
- **"Double Session":** higher carb + protein, for heavy days.
- **"Single Session":** fewer calories, still high protein, for lighter days in camp.
- Exact fixed-portion macros, real-food protein, no sweeteners or sugar alcohols; fighter-made and fighter-tested; collected at the gym or sent in a weekly camp box.
- **Why it's meaningful:** fighters manage weight in camp and out (X1); they under-eat carbs (X3); coaches, not dietitians, advise them (X1); the 8 fighters asked for natural, high protein, high carb, low sugar `[EVIDENCE: C11–C18]`.
- **Why it's hard to copy:** the big brands sell one bar to everyone through supermarkets. The UFC's Main Event is US-only and isolate-based. The founder's gym, 25k MMA channel and fighter feedback loop are his own.
- **Claims caution:** avoid names like "Cut", "Lean" or "Light". "Light" is a regulated nutrition claim, and weight-loss wording is restricted.
