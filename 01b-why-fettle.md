## The one-line difference
> **fettle is the only bar we found in the UK that's high in protein *and* low in sugar using nothing but kitchen ingredients: no protein powders, no sugar alcohols, no sweeteners, no syrups.**

Checked across **32 products** (`02-competitors.csv`, incl. Warrior RAW, Myprotein Re-Fuel, Huel and Perfect Bar). On the sugar vs protein map (`02-positioning-map.html`), every other bar with ≤6 g sugar and ≥10 g protein is a lab bar built on protein isolates or blends, most with polyols or sweeteners. Every whole-food or "natural" bar carries 9.5–38 g sugar `[EVIDENCE: 02-competitors.csv]`.

**Read honestly:** we lose on protein and price, and we need a fridge. We win on the one thing none of them offers: **low sugar with nothing but kitchen ingredients.** That only matters if athletes value it. That's what the pilot tests.

## Why it needs to be made: the problem, in five steps

### 1. Athletes are told to eat real food, and most prefer it
- The Team Durham S&C coach says athletes are advised to eat whole foods `[EVIDENCE: E1]`. A rugby player is "advised to only eat whole foods" `[EVIDENCE: C7]`. 4/10 interviewees value whole foods or object to "bad ingredients" `[EVIDENCE: C1, C2, C4, C7]`.
- **Carey, Doyle & Lucey (2023)**, *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living* (Ireland), n = 405 athletes and active adults: when shown popular performance and recovery products, **whole-food-type products got the most first-preference and top-3 picks**. Top priorities were nutritional profile (3.4/4), taste (3.2/4) and accessibility (3.1/4). Most sought-after claim: muscle recovery (25% first choice) `[EVIDENCE: doi 10.3389/fspor.2023.1088979]`.
- **Forsyth & Mantzioris (2023)**, *British Journal of Nutrition* (Australia), n = 140 athletes: compared with sports foods, **everyday foods were rated cheaper, tastier and lower risk of banned substances**, but **less convenient and more likely to spoil**. **51% were concerned about the health effects of ultra-processed food**, yet 95% had used sports foods in the past year `[EVIDENCE: PMC10551470]`.

**→ Athletes want real food but reach for processed sports foods because they're convenient. That's the gap.**

### 2. But real food is hard to fit around training
- Two of three performance athletes interviewed struggle to cook: one is on the road a lot (C7); one trains twice a day and works (C10) `[EVIDENCE]`.
- **Canadian university athletes (USPORT survey): 80% said their daily schedule limited their ability to cook and prepare meals**; time, money and campus food access were common barriers `[EVIDENCE: York University, yorkspace]`.
- **UK university students (n = 590, 2025 preprint, not yet peer-reviewed):** 45% report barriers to healthy eating; top barriers were cost, lack of time and stress `[EVIDENCE: preprints.org 202504.0057]`.

### 3. The convenient options don't fit what athletes want
| What's on the shelf | The problem | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Low-sugar protein bars** (Grenade, Barebells, PhD, Fulfil, Misfits, Warrior RAW, Huel, Myprotein Re-Fuel) | Protein isolates, blends or gelatine plus polyols or sweeteners. Grenade carries 16 g polyols and a laxative warning; PhD 20 g polyols; Re-Fuel's first ingredient is maltitol; Warrior and Huel carry laxative warnings | `[EVIDENCE: Boots/Ocado/H&B listings; Tesco Misfits ingredients]` |
| **"Natural" / recovery bars** (Forza, Nākd, Styrkr, Trek) | Real ingredients, but 10–28 g sugar, because dates and syrups hold them together. Styrkr's first ingredient is glucose syrup | `[EVIDENCE: retailer listings]` |
| **Real food** (chocolate milk, a sandwich) | Needs a fridge or a kitchen; spoils in a kit bag | `[EVIDENCE: Forsyth & Mantzioris 2023]` |

- **Gut comfort matters to athletes:** **30–50% of athletes report gastrointestinal complaints** around exercise (de Oliveira et al. 2014, *Sports Medicine*); a 2025 scoping review found 11.5–80% across elite samples `[EVIDENCE: PubMed 24791919; LJMU scoping review]`. Polyols pull water into the gut and can cause bloating or diarrhoea at around 20 g+ in one sitting `[EVIDENCE: secondary sources; check primary studies]`. Bars with 16–20 g polyols sit right at that level.
- **Contamination risk with supplements:** a 2025 Australian Sport Integrity survey found **35% of 200 supplements contained a WADA-prohibited substance** `[EVIDENCE: sportintegrity.gov.au]`. Food made from eggs, oats and milk carries no added-supplement risk `[ASSUMPTION: still not batch-tested]`.

### 4. Athletes don't actually eat the lab bars they're given
- The Team Durham S&C coach said students were given **boxes upon boxes of Grenade Oreo bars and really struggled to eat them** `[EVIDENCE: E1]`.
- Taste was a concern for 3/10 interviewees (C3, C8, C9) `[EVIDENCE]`. Taste is the #2 priority in Carey et al. (2023) `[EVIDENCE]`.

### 5. The bars cost too much for what they are
- 3/10 interviewees raised price: "too expensive" with "bad" ingredients (C1, a professional footballer); price is important (C5); priced out (C6) `[EVIDENCE]`. UK students cite food cost as the #1 healthy-eating barrier `[EVIDENCE: 2025 preprint]`.
- Lab bars sell at £2.50–2.95 `[EVIDENCE: 02]`; fettle costs £0.77–0.84 to make in a kitchen `[EVIDENCE: 04-costing.csv]`, which leaves room to price fairly.

### Why now
- **76–78% of UK adults say they're concerned about ultra-processed food**, the second-biggest food concern after prices (FSA Consumer Insights Tracker, Apr 2024–Mar 2025). Among 16–34s it's 72% `[EVIDENCE: FSA via Food Manufacture / FoodNavigator, Jul 2025]`.
- UK sports nutrition is ~£1.1 bn and growing ~19% a year (Circana via trade press) `[EVIDENCE: inherited, secondary]`.
- UK HFSS rules restrict promotion of "less healthy" foods. fettle scores **−4 (not HFSS)**; high-sugar natural bars likely don't pass `[EVIDENCE: 04-nutrition.py]` + `[GAP: confirm category scope]`.

| Nutrient | What the evidence says | fettle v6 (hero, per 90 g) |
|---|---|---|
| **Protein** | ~0.25–0.3 g/kg per meal, or 20–40 g (ISSN 2017; Moore 2018). Professor (E2): ~10 g per 100 kcal, quality sources | **16.7 g** (≈0.25 g/kg for a 67 kg athlete); 25% of energy; **6.2 g per 100 kcal**: higher than any whole-food bar found, below isolate lab bars (8.4–10.5). Eggs and milk are high-quality complete proteins |
| **Carbs** | Fast glycogen refill needs ≥1.2 g/kg/hour; when carbs are lower (≤0.8 g/kg/h), adding protein helps glycogen restore (Alghannam et al. 2018) | **23 g** from oats. A **kick-start**, not a full refuel; pair with a meal. Honest framing; no recovery claim |
| **Sugar** | Sugars are usable carbs after exercise, but buyers and the FSA worry about sugar; polyols (the lab bars' fix) cause gut issues at ~20 g+ | **1.9 g** (low sugar ✅), with **no polyols or sweeteners** |
| **Fibre** | Athletes should build to ~30 g/day (Mancin, Burke & Rollo 2025); keep it low *before* exercise (ACSM). Coach (E1): "some fibre for digestion" | **5.8 g** from oats, oat bran and chia: "high fibre" ✅. Eat it after training, not before |
| **Fat** | Slows digestion; adds calories | 11 g (eggs, peanut butter, seeds) |

## Honest counter-evidence (say it before the judges do)
- **Interest is not demand.** 7/10 liked the idea; **0/10 committed** to anything yet.
- **The studies aren't UK athletes.** They're Irish (mixed athletes), Australian (n = 140) and Canadian. UK athlete-specific evidence is a gap. Your interviews are the UK evidence.
- **RXBAR**, a simple-ingredient egg-white bar, was withdrawn from the UK. Simple ingredients alone didn't win.
- **Lab bars still have more protein per calorie** (8.4–10.5 vs 6.2 g per 100 kcal) and they don't need a fridge.

## Does a fully natural bar with 20 g protein, high carbs, high fibre, ~200 kcal, ~6 g sugar and ~6 g fat exist? (checked 8 Oct)

### 1. Not at 200 kcal: the maths rules it out
Energy per gram: protein 4 kcal, carbs 4, fat 9, fibre 2. At 200 kcal, **20 g protein (80 kcal) + 6 g fat (54 kcal) + 6 g fibre (12 kcal) leaves room for only ~13.5 g carbs**. That's not high carb. Every extra 10 g of carbs costs ~40 kcal.

| Energy | Carbs that fit (20 g P, 6 g fat, 6 g fibre) |
|---|---|
| 200 kcal | ~13.5 g |
| 220 kcal | ~18.5 g |
| **250 kcal** | **~26 g** |

So the realistic version of the target is **~240–250 kcal: 20 g protein, ~25 g carbs, ~6 g fat, ~6 g fibre, ≤6 g sugar**.

### 2. Nothing natural on the market does it
| Product | Protein | Natural? | Why it misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warrior RAW, Barebells, Grenade, PhD, Huel, Myprotein Re-Fuel | 15–22 g | ❌ | Isolates/blends or gelatine + polyols or sweeteners |
| The Whole Truth (India), PROBAR, Rise (US) | ~18–20 g | "Natural" on pack | Reach 20 g with **whey or pea/rice protein powders**; not UK-stocked as far as found `[EVIDENCE: search 8 Oct; fwdfuel, fitlyfast]` |
| Julian Bakery egg white bar (US) | 20 g | ❌ | **Egg-white protein powder + tapioca fibre + monk-fruit sweetener** `[EVIDENCE: Vitacost]` |
| Perfect Bar (US, chilled) | 17 g | "Whole food" | Milk/egg/rice protein powders; 19 g sugar |
| RXBAR (egg white + dates) | 12 g | ✅ | Only 12 g protein, 13 g sugar; **withdrawn from the UK** |
| Roam beef bar (UK) | 20 g | ✅ | Savoury meat snack, almost no carbs, not a recovery bar `[EVIDENCE: roamfood.com guide]` |
| Veloforte Forza, Nākd, Styrkr | 7–15 g | partly | Too little protein, 13–28 g sugar |

**One guide put it plainly: natural bars rarely reach 20 g without protein isolates or powders** `[EVIDENCE: search extract]`.

### 3. What hasn't been done: our differentiation
> **A sweet bar with 20 g protein from whole foods (eggs, egg whites, quark, oat bran) with no protein powder, plus real carbs from oats, high fibre, ≤6 g sugar, ~6 g fat and no sweeteners or sugar alcohols.**

Nobody in the 32-product set, or in the "natural protein bar" searches, combines all of these. Every 20 g bar either uses powders and sweeteners, or isn't a carb-containing recovery bar.

### 4. Can we make it? Yes on paper, with one trade-off (`04-nutrition.py`, recipe `T20_lean_target`)
| T20 lean target (per ~104 g bar) | Value |
|---|---|
| Protein | **20.1 g** (33% of energy) |
| Carbs | **24.2 g** |
| Sugar | **4.3 g** (low sugar ✅) |
| Fat | **6.0 g** |
| Fibre | **6.5 g** (high fibre ✅) |
| Energy | **244 kcal** |
| Pilot cost | £1.23 (whites from whole eggs; cartoned egg white would cut it) |

**Per tin (~8 bars):** 400 g egg whites (≈12 whites, or a carton), 300 g quark, 160 g oat bran, 2 whole eggs, 100 g oats, 25 g cocoa, 15 g chia, 15 g honey, pinch of salt. Bake low and long (160 °C fan, ~45–50 min).

**The trade-off:** that much egg white and quark makes a **soft, cake-like bar** (estimated ~42% moisture, assuming 36% steam loss in the oven). It needs a fridge and a texture test. If it's too soft, step back towards v6 (17 g protein, firmer), or use dried egg white for a firmer 20 g bar at the cost of "kitchen ingredients only".
