# 19: Mom Test interview questions (Graft whole-food snack)

**Date:** 13 Oct 2026 · **Method:** *The Mom Test* (Rob Fitzpatrick): ask questions that even your mum can't lie to you about.
**Judging link:** Problem & Market Research ("meaningful customer discovery"); sheet questions "How do you know? Proof about customers" and "Why will they care? (migraine)".
**Copy-into-a-doc version:** `20-interview-template-open.md` · **Write-up:** use `research/17_interview_template.csv`; analyse with `research/17_analyse_responses.py`.

---

## The 3 rules
1. **Talk about their life, not your idea.** Don't mention the snack until the end.
2. **Ask about specifics in the past, not opinions about the future.** "Would you buy…?" gets a polite yes; "When did you last buy…?" gets the truth.
3. **Talk less, listen more.** Use "Why?" and "Tell me more about that."

## Bad question → good question
| ❌ Bad (invites a polite lie) | ✅ Good (gets a fact) |
|---|---|
| "Would you buy a real-food protein bar?" | "When did you last buy a snack after training? What was it?" |
| "Do you think protein bars are too processed?" | "What did you eat after your last session? Why that?" |
| "Is it hard to eat well after training?" | "Tell me about the last time you couldn't get a proper meal after training." |
| "Would you pay £2.50?" | "What did you spend on snacks or shakes last week?" |
| "Do you like the idea?" | "What have you already tried to fix this?" |
| "Would your coach approve?" | "What has your coach actually told you to eat?" |

## Athletes and gym-goers (persona and early adopters)

**Their routine**
1. Talk me through your last training day, from waking up to bed.
2. What did you eat in the 2 hours after that session? Where did it come from?

**The problem**

3. When was the last time you couldn't eat a proper meal after training? What happened?
4. What did you eat instead? How did you feel about it?
5. How often did that happen last week?

**What they do now**

6. What's the last protein bar, shake or snack you bought? Why that one?
7. Have you bought it again? Why or why not?
8. What do you dislike about the options you've tried? Give me an example.
9. Have you ever tried to fix this yourself (meal prep, baking your own, carrying food)? What happened?

**Money and advice**

10. Roughly what did you spend on snacks, shakes or bars last week?
11. What has your coach or PT told you to eat? Do you actually do it?

**Digging deeper (any time)**
- "Why?"
- "Can you give me an example?"
- "What happened next?"
- "How did you deal with it?"

## Coaches and S&C staff
1. What do you tell your athletes to eat after training?
2. What do they actually eat? How do you know?
3. Have you ever given out snacks or bars to a squad? What happened to them?
4. Who decides what food the squad gets, and who pays?
5. What would you need to see before you'd let a new snack near your squad?

## Only at the end: the product and a commitment
Show the card: egg, quark, oats; ~15 g protein; no sweeteners; kept in the fridge. Then ask:
- **Time:** "Could I bring some to your next session for a free taste test?"
- **Reputation:** "Would you introduce me to your coach or captain?"
- **Effort:** "Can I add you to the trial list for the first batch?" Take their email or number there and then.

**A real "yes" to any of these is worth more than ten "sounds great"s.**

## What to ignore and what counts
| Ignore | Counts |
|---|---|
| Compliments ("Love it!") | A specific story of the problem happening |
| "I'd definitely buy that" | Money or time they already spend on a workaround |
| "Most people would…" | They've already tried to solve it themselves |
| Promises about the future | A commitment now: a taste-test slot, an intro, their contact details |

## After each interview, write one line
`Who / group · how often the problem happens · what they use now · what annoys them · what they committed to · one verbatim quote`

Mark paraphrases as paraphrases. Never write up a quote you didn't hear.
