## B. The need: who needs this and why

### B1. What the science says after hard training
| Guidance | Key point | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| ACSM/AND/DC 2016 | Carbs 3–10 g/kg/day by load (6–10 for 1–3 h/day high intensity); protein 1.2–2.0 g/kg/day, ~0.3 g/kg a dose | `[EVIDENCE: S14]` |
| ISSN protein 2017 | 20–40 g (0.25 g/kg) a serving | `[EVIDENCE: S3]` |
| **ISSN position stand on MMA and combat sports (Ricci et al. 2025, *JISSN* 22:2467909)** | 16 points. Fight-camp length and the weigh-in-to-fight gap shape nutrition. Stay **12–15% above division weight off camp**. Use **gradual, planned** calorie cuts in camp. Prioritise protein while losing weight (secondary summary) | `[EVIDENCE: U20]` |
| Reale, Slater & Burke 2017 (IJSPP; EJSS) | Decision tree for acute weight loss. Cut food volume **without cutting energy or macronutrients**. Recover after the weigh-in | `[EVIDENCE: U21]` |
| IOC REDs consensus 2023 (Mountjoy et al., *BJSM*) | **Low carbohydrate availability** is now named as a REDs factor; male athletes included; prevalence 15–80% depending on sport | `[EVIDENCE: U22]` |
| Carbs + protein for short recovery | Co-ingestion helps when carbs are below ~0.8 g/kg/h; matters most for **two-a-days** | `[EVIDENCE: S2, S24, S25]` |
| UFC Performance Institute "performance plate" | Carbs (oats, rice, potatoes) are "the main fuel source" for high-intensity sessions; the plate changes with load | `[EVIDENCE: U40]` (organisation guidance, not peer-reviewed) |

**Graft fit:** at 15 g protein and 22.5 g carbs (calculated), one bar gives ~0.2 g/kg protein and ~0.3 g/kg carbs for a 75 kg fighter. That is a **contribution, not a complete recovery feed** `[ASSUMPTION, arithmetic]`. Two bars, or bar plus milk, gets closer.

### B2. Weight cutting
- **Prevalence:** 97.2% of men and 100% of women (Hillier 2019, n = 314). Pros lost more in the final week than amateurs (5.9% vs 4.2% of body mass). Methods: water loading 72.9%, fluid restriction 71.3%, sweat suits 55.4% `[EVIDENCE: U25]`. Across combat sports, 60–80% cut weight, with **boxing and MMA highest** `[EVIDENCE: U24]`.
- **UK fighters:** Matthews & Nicholas 2017 (UK, n = 7) found a ~5.6 kg (8%) loss, then a **7.4 kg (11.7%) regain** in ~32 h. **All were dehydrated** at weigh-in (57% dehydrated, 43% severely). The methods were "comparable to those linked to previous fatalities" `[EVIDENCE: U23]`.
- **Pros:** 98% gained weight between weigh-in and fight (CSAC, 512 fighters) `[EVIDENCE: X2]`.
- **Day to day, in camp:** a planned calorie deficit. **Out of camp:** stay 12–15% above the limit `[EVIDENCE: U20]`. So fighters watch sugar and fat **all year**. That is Ewan's premise, and the ISSN supports it indirectly. How fighters actually eat out of camp: `[GAP]`, ask them.

### B3. Under-eating and who advises fighters
- Energy intake is below requirements and carbs are ~3.3 g/kg/day in amateurs and pros out of competition (n = 24) `[EVIDENCE: X3]`. A 2025 systematic-review abstract (17 combat-sport studies) found most athletes below energy and carb guidelines, falling further before competition `[EVIDENCE: U26, conference abstract]`.
- **Advisers:** coaches are the main influence on weight-making `[EVIDENCE: U25, X4]`. Student athletes get most nutrition information from social media, then coaches `[EVIDENCE: S18]`. **This matters for Graft:** coaches and YouTube are the channels, and that is where Ewan already is.

### B4. Digestion
- 30–50% of athletes report GI complaints during exercise `[EVIDENCE: M30]`. Outside endurance sport the range is 11.5–80% `[EVIDENCE: M31]`.
- **No study measures GI symptoms in grappling or MMA** `[GAP]`. A 2026 gut-microbiome review in combat sports found only 8 small studies `[EVIDENCE: U41]`.
- Coaching blogs advise avoiding fat and big meals before rolling, and suggest a small carb snack within an hour of training `[EVIDENCE: U42, anecdote]`.
- **Polyols:** foods with >10% added polyols must carry "excessive consumption may produce laxative effects". 5 of the 7 big UK bars were confirmed to carry it `[EVIDENCE: M22–M28]`. GB Annex III wording not yet seen directly `[GAP: M23]`.

### B5. Anti-doping
- UKAD: **no supplement is guaranteed free of prohibited substances**, and strict liability applies `[EVIDENCE: X5]`.
- UKAD figure: **~44% of UK positive tests in 2012** were thought to come from supplements (old, quoted in a 2016 statement). 10–25% contamination rates were found in UK/EU/US supplements (HFL 2013) `[EVIDENCE: U27, dated]`. 68% of NADO leaders call supplements high-risk (Informed Sport 2026, commercial source) `[EVIDENCE: U27]`.
- **Relevance:** amateur MMA in the UK is rarely drug-tested (the Safe MMA scheme covers medicals and blood tests, not anti-doping) `[ASSUMPTION from U28]`. So contamination is a **minor buying reason for amateurs** and a bigger one for pros and tested judo or wrestling athletes. **Never claim "doping-safe".** Say "no protein powders or supplements" (true for option a) `[ASSUMPTION]`.

### B6. What fighters eat now, and what they dislike
- **No fighter interview, forum thread or survey was found** naming specific dislikes of protein bars `[GAP]`. Reddit is blocked here.
- Fighter-nutrition sites suggest Greek yoghurt, cottage cheese with fruit, toast, nuts and fruit, and some mention protein bars as a camp snack `[EVIDENCE: U43, low]`.
- Interview proxies (non-MMA): bars "too expensive" with "bad" ingredients (C1); whole foods only (C2, C4, C7); "dense" and hard to digest (E3); students struggled to finish boxes of Grenade bars (E1) `[EVIDENCE: F4, F5, paraphrased]`.
- **Ewan's observation** that MMA gyms stock Grenade-type bars: `[ASSUMPTION / founder observation]`. Photograph the fridge.

**So what for the pitch:** the science is strong. Fighters cut weight almost universally, under-eat carbs and are advised by coaches, so a **measured, low-sugar carb-plus-protein snack** has a real physiological case. What is missing is the fighter's own voice.
**Confidence: medium.** Peer-reviewed and recent (2017–2025), but samples are small and mostly not UK. No study on grappling GI symptoms or fighters' snack preferences.

### D4. Why doesn't the gap already exist?
1. **Water activity and shelf life:** sugar, glycerol and polyols bind water, so a bar stays ambient for months (aw ≤0.65) `[M39]`. Remove all three and you lose that preservation. A real-food egg bar risks mould at aw 0.6–0.85 `[M42]`.
2. **Protein density against taste and texture:** 20 g protein in a small bar needs isolates. Real egg and oats give ~10–15 g in 50 g `[04f, v13 calc]`.
3. **Retail economics:** supermarkets need 6–12 months' life and big minimum orders (10–25k, P15). JNCK was delisted from Tesco and liquidated in Sept 2025 `[H3]`.
4. **Low sugar plus high carbs is hard:** carbs without sugar means starch (oats, rice), which is bulky and dry. Most "recovery" bars use syrups (TORQ, Styrkr, Forza).
5. **Niche:** MMA is too small for big brands to target with a product. The UFC chose the US, with isolates.

**How a small founder gets round them:** make to order weekly, sell through a **gym fridge** (shelf life measured in days, not months), use **YouTube D2C pre-orders** in batches, and run a subscription for fighters in camp. Do a water-activity test before any ambient claim `[N14, N15]`. Precedent: Mooski self-made 2–3k bars a day before moving to a co-packer `[H9]`. Perfect Bar built a chilled category `[H2]`.
