# Mega Prompt: 1,000 Verified, Credible Sources for the MMA Real-Food Snack Venture

**How to use:** paste everything below the line into a research-capable AI with web search (or give it to a research assistant). Run it **one batch at a time** (20 batches × 50 sources). Asking for 1,000 in one go produces fabricated or unchecked references. Paste each batch's CSV into one master sheet.

**Honest expectation:** some topics (e.g. UK MMA participation) won't have 50 credible sources. The prompt tells the researcher to **report the shortfall, not pad it**. A verified 700 beats an unverified 1,000.

---

## ROLE

You are a **research librarian and evidence analyst** supporting a Durham University Business School postgraduate consultancy project. The venture is a **real-food, high-protein, high-carbohydrate, low-sugar snack with no artificial sweeteners or sugar alcohols, for UK amateur MMA fighters who train twice a day**. The founder (Ewan, Durham Venture School) needs a **verified source library** to back every claim in the final pitch: market demand, the need, competitors, feasibility, regulation and scale.

## THE TASK

Build a library of up to **1,000 credible, individually verified sources**, delivered in **20 batches of 50**, one batch per run. For each source, explain **what it proves, how strongly, and which pitch claim it supports**.

**This run: Batch [N] — Topic: [TOPIC FROM THE TABLE BELOW]**

## NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES

1. **Never fabricate.** No invented titles, authors, DOIs, URLs, figures, quotes or dates. If you can't open it and confirm it, it doesn't go in the library.
2. **Verify every source** using the protocol below. Mark each one `VERIFIED` or leave it out. No "probably real".
3. **No padding.** If fewer than 50 credible sources exist for a topic, stop. Report how many you found, what you searched and why the evidence is thin. A thin evidence base is itself a finding.
4. **Marketing is a claim, not a fact.** Brand pages, press releases and sponsorship announcements are grade D. Write "brand says…".
5. **UK first.** Use GBP, GB law and UK data. Label US, EU and global sources and never blend their figures into UK numbers.
6. **Recent first:** 2022–2026. Older sources are allowed only if they are landmarks (position stands, foundational studies, the legislation itself). Say why each one is still needed.
7. **No duplicates.** The same study reported in five news articles counts **once**: cite the original. Syndicated press releases count once.
8. **Disagreement:** when sources conflict, give the range, say which one you trust and why.
9. **Quote, don't paraphrase, the supporting sentence** (≤40 words, exactly as written, with the page or section). This is the proof that you read it.
10. **Fiction is never evidence.** Ignore AI-generated content farms, SEO "market research" pages with no stated method, and undated listicles.

## VERIFICATION PROTOCOL (every source)

| Check | How | Fail = |
|---|---|---|
| 1. It exists | Open the URL. For papers, resolve the DOI at https://doi.org/ or check Crossref, PubMed or the publisher page | Exclude |
| 2. Metadata matches | Title, authors, year, journal/publisher and volume match the landing page exactly | Correct it, or exclude |
| 3. Claim is really there | Find the exact sentence or table that supports your use of it | Exclude |
| 4. Credibility | Peer review, official status, named method, sample size, funder and conflicts of interest | Downgrade or exclude |
| 5. Currency | Publication date; for retail or price pages, the date you checked | Flag if older than 2022 without a landmark reason |
| 6. Independence | Who paid for it? (e.g. a brand-funded study on its own product) | Note it and downgrade |
| 7. Accessibility | Open access, Durham Library (Mintel, Passport, Statista, SPORTDiscus, Web of Science), or paywalled | Record it; paywalled is fine if verified via the abstract and metadata |

## EVIDENCE GRADES

- **A:** systematic review, meta-analysis, consensus statement or position stand
- **B:** peer-reviewed primary study; official statistics (ONS, Sport England, FSA, DEFRA, gov.uk); legislation
- **C:** reputable industry data (Mintel, Circana, Kantar, Euromonitor, IGD, Leisure DB) or quality trade/business press (The Grocer, Food Navigator, Just Food, FT, BBC)
- **D:** company statements, press releases, sponsorship announcements, brand websites, retailer listings
- **E:** anecdote, forums, Reddit, social media, podcasts, single interviews, YouTube

**Target mix across the library:** ≥40% A–B, ≤20% D–E. Report the actual mix for each batch.

## SEARCH METHOD (do this, then report it)

1. **Databases first:** Google Scholar, PubMed, Web of Science/SPORTDiscus (via Durham Library), Crossref; then gov.uk, legislation.gov.uk, food.gov.uk; then Mintel, Statista and Passport (via Durham Library); then trade press.
2. **Snowball:** for each key paper, check its references (backwards) and "cited by" (forwards).
3. **Search strings:** log every query and the date it was run, e.g. `("mixed martial arts" OR MMA) AND ("rapid weight loss" OR "weight cutting") 2022..2026`.
4. **Saturation:** stop a topic when 3 consecutive searches return nothing new and credible.
5. **Record exclusions:** how many sources you rejected, and why (could not verify / duplicate / no method / out of date / content farm).

## OUTPUT FORMAT (each batch)

### 1. Batch CSV (one row per source; import into the master sheet)
```
id,batch,topic,authors,year,title,publisher_or_journal,volume_issue_pages,doi,url,access_date,source_type,grade,peer_reviewed,uk_or_other,sample_or_method,funder_or_conflict,exact_supporting_quote,page_or_section,what_it_proves,pitch_claim_supported,judging_criterion,strength_of_support,limitations,conflicts_with,verification_status
```
- `id`: B[batch]-[001–050]
- `judging_criterion`: one of Problem & Market Research / Innovation & Value Proposition / Feasibility & Business Model / Team / Impact & Scale / Pitch
- `strength_of_support`: strong / moderate / weak, with one line of reasoning
- `verification_status`: `VERIFIED` only (unverified sources are not included)

### 2. Reasoning summary (≤400 words)
- **What the batch establishes**, with grade and the source ids it rests on.
- **Triangulation:** which claims now rest on ≥2 independent source types, and which are still single-source.
- **Contradictions:** the range, which you trust, and why.
- **What the evidence does *not* show** (gaps, weak spots, counter-evidence against the venture). Steelman the case against.
- **So what for the pitch:** one sentence.

### 3. Search log and exclusions
Queries + dates; number screened, included and excluded, with reasons; shortfall (if <50) and why.

### 4. Top 5 sources in this batch
Harvard reference + DOI/URL + a 2-line annotation (key finding + why it matters to the venture).

## AFTER ALL BATCHES (final synthesis run)

Paste the master CSV back in and ask for:
1. **De-duplication** across batches and a final count by grade, topic and year.
2. **A claim–evidence matrix:** every pitch claim × supporting source ids × grade mix × triangulated (Y/N).
3. **The 30 sources to actually cite in the pitch and report** (strongest, most recent, most UK-relevant).
4. **A demand verdict:** is the problem a "migraine" or a "vitamin"? Weigh all the evidence for and against, and say what primary research (fighter interviews, pre-orders) must still prove.
5. A **Harvard reference list** of everything cited, with access dates.
