## 6. How does fettle help?

| | |
|---|---|
| **The problem we're solving** | No convenient after-training bar is **high in protein, low in sugar AND made from real food**. The ones that are convenient have "bad" ingredients (C1) or don't get eaten (E1); the whole-food options mean cooking, which isn't always possible (C7, C10) |
| **Why it matters to Jack** | Jack trains regularly (8/10 go to the gym), wants protein (4/10) and real food (4/10), and is trying to eat better (8/10) |
| **What's different** | T20: **~21 g protein, ~26 g carbs, ~5 g sugar, ~7 g fibre**, from eggs, quark, oats and oat bran only. No protein powder, sweeteners or sugar alcohols. Every other low-sugar bar we found relies on those (`01b`) |
| **What could stop Jack** | **Taste** (3/10) and **price** (3/10). Lab bars cost as little as ~£1 (Warrior RAW). The bar must taste good and be priced fairly; the pilot tests both |

## Name and test question
### **Jack, the committed gym-goer**
> **"Would Jack buy this?"** *Would Jack, who trains most days and is trying to eat better, choose a fettle bar over a Fulfil or Grenade bar after the gym, and buy it again, because it tastes good and it's real food with the protein Jack wants?*
**Today:** interest is strong (7/10 liked the concept). **Commitment is unproven (0/10).** Taste and price are the deal-breakers.
