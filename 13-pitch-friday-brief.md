## 3. Why do people *need* this? (the problem, the "migraine")
**Answer:** after training they need protein and carbs, often can't cook, and every quick option is a compromise.

| Reason | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **No time to cook** | 80% of university athletes say their schedule limits cooking `[EVIDENCE: S29]`; C7, C10 |
| **Athletes fall short on carbs, not protein** | Team-sport athletes meet protein targets but miss carbohydrate and energy (review of 21 studies) `[EVIDENCE: S16, Jenner 2019]` |
| **The quick options all fall short:** | |
| fruit | little protein |
| lab bars | protein powder, sweeteners, sugar alcohols (e.g. maltitol is Myprotein Re-Fuel's first ingredient) `[EVIDENCE: deck slide 4; 02d WR43–45]` |
| "natural" bars | sugary: Nākd Protein 13 g, Trek flapjack 14 g, Styrkr 28 g `[EVIDENCE: 02d WR47, WR49; deck]` |
| **It happens after nearly every session** | Once a day for Jack, so 5–7 times a week `[ASSUMPTION from training frequency]` |
| **Fibre gap** | Only ~4% of UK adults reach 30 g a day `[EVIDENCE: S19]` |

**The pushback you'll get, and your answer**
- **The pushback:** "The post-workout window is a myth." One Reddit user said they can easily wait 2–4 hours `[EVIDENCE: R14]`, and total daily protein matters more than timing `[EVIDENCE: S28]`.
- **Your answer:** "Agreed, it's not about a magic window. It's about having real food to hand when you can't cook, instead of a lab bar or nothing. Carbs do matter if you train again within about 6–8 hours, which squads often do `[EVIDENCE: S23]`."

**Say it (~20 s):** "After nearly every session, athletes need protein and carbs. Eighty per cent say their schedule stops them cooking. So they grab fruit with no protein, a lab bar full of powders and sweeteners, or a 'natural' bar full of sugar. Their coach says eat real food, and nothing convenient is real food."

## 4. Why do people *want* this? (the desire)
**Answer:** they want **the convenience of a bar with the ingredients of home baking.**

| Reason | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Athletes prefer whole food** | Whole-food products were the #1 choice among 405 athletes `[EVIDENCE: S7, Carey 2023]` |
| **Real food wins on everything except convenience** | Athletes rate everyday food tastier, cheaper and safer; sports foods win only on convenience `[EVIDENCE: S6, Forsyth & Mantzioris 2023]` |
| **Bars feel like a compromise** | A Reddit user called protein bars "a necessary evil" and said they'd rather eat whole foods `[EVIDENCE: R06]` (online post, weak evidence) |
| **They liked the idea** | 7/10 interviewees `[EVIDENCE: 03-persona.md]` |
| **The wider trend** | 71% of UK adults try to avoid ultra-processed food `[EVIDENCE: WR53, Mintel 2025]` |
| **Taste brings people back** | Every repeat-buy reason on Reddit was taste (6/13) `[EVIDENCE: 02c]`. That's why the taste test comes before anything else |

**Say it (~15 s):** "Athletes already prefer real food: it was the number one choice in a study of 405 athletes, and they rate it tastier, cheaper and safer. Sports bars only win on convenience. Graft gives them both. Real food for grafters."

## 5. Judge Q&A (short answers)
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Why not just eat a quark pot?** | You can, at home. A pot needs a spoon and a fridge and isn't a grab-and-go after training. Quark is in our bar; we make it portable, with carbs from oats |
| **Why not Barebells or Grenade?** | 20 g protein, but from milk protein powders with maltitol and sucralose `[EVIDENCE: 02d WR44–45]`. Athletes and coaches want real food |
| **Only 15 g protein?** | Per 50 g bar. Real food holds water, so we eat two: ~29 g protein, ~28 g carbs. Two bars cost about the same as one protein bar `[ASSUMPTION: price £2–2.50 untested]` |
| **Why the fridge? That's a weakness** | No preservatives, so it's fresh, like Simmer Eats' meals. "Baked this week, not 12 months ago." It can be frozen |
| **Does it taste good?** | Honestly: early bakes failed on texture. I've fixed the method and I'm testing v6 this week, then a blind taste test vs Barebells (target ≥7/9) |
| **Why not women 40–60?** | I researched them: a real need, but it feels like a nice-to-have for them, and Bio&Me already targets them. Scorecard: athletes 30/40 vs women 21/40 `[EVIDENCE: 02d §8]`. I'm testing them as a second segment |
| **How do you make money?** | Squad orders first: one drop for 25 athletes. Then North East universities, then a national weekly box `[EVIDENCE: 10-scale-plan.md]` |
| **Why "Graft"?** | British slang for hard work ("a grafter"), used most in the North. It's for people who put the work in, and it isn't a sporty name, so it can grow beyond athletes |
