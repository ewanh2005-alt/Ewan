## 1. Headline findings (read this first)

1. **Styrkr BAR+ is not a whole-food bar.** It is marketed as "all natural", but its listed ingredients start with **glucose syrup**, then **soy protein crispies (isolated soy protein)**, caster sugar, vegan caramel, inverted sugar syrup and **soy protein isolate**. It has **~28 g sugar** and only **~0.7 g fibre** per 74 g bar. **[V – Sigma Sports / Bike24 listings]** *Your gap against Styrkr is real: whole-food protein, real fibre and less added sugar.*
2. **Your closest competitor is Veloforte Forza, not Styrkr.** Forza is UK-handmade from apricots, almonds and **egg white** (no isolate) with honey and agave. It is positioned for recovery at 3:1, with **12 g protein, 38 g carbs and 7.4 g fibre** per 70 g. **[V – Sigma Sports / Condor listings]** So "natural + fibre + recovery" is **already taken**. Forza's weakness is **protein: 12 g is low**.
3. **No UK bar we found combines ≥20 g protein + ≥40 g carbs + whole-food protein sources + ≥5 g fibre.** Every bar with 20 g protein is either carb-light (Barebells, Grenade, PhD, Fulfil, Veloforte Protein Crunch) or uses isolates and syrups (Myprotein Flapjack, Clif Builders). This is the most defensible white space. It also comes with a **feasibility problem** (Section 6).
4. **The real competitor is probably "nothing", a meal, or chocolate milk, not another bar.** Customer discovery has to establish whether amateur and university athletes **pay for recovery products at all** before the pitch can claim a "migraine problem".
5. **Claims risk is an opening.** The GB-authorised carbohydrate recovery claim comes with heavy mandatory consumer information (4 g/kg within 4–6 h). Several competitors use loose wording like "supports muscle recovery" **[A – needs a legal check]**. A compliant, evidence-led brand can turn that into part of its credibility story.

## 6. Section 3 tensions — first pass

### 6a. "Styrkr already does this"
**No, and here is the evidence.** Styrkr is high-carb, but its protein is soy isolate, its first ingredient is glucose syrup, and it has 0.7 g fibre. **Pitch line [draft]:** *"Styrkr proved athletes will pay ~£2.80 for a recovery bar. We're building the one a sports dietitian would actually recommend."* Turn their success into your market validation.

### 6b. Whole food vs high protein: definition tiers

Proposed honest definition: **"whole-food" = every protein source is a recognisable food or a single-ingredient dried food, with no isolates, crisps, collagen or blends.**

Rough protein maths for a ~80 g bar. Ingredient protein values are typical label figures **[A – confirm against supplier specs in Stage 3]**.

| Tier | Protein sources allowed | Example build (g → protein) | Est. protein | Honest? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 Strict whole food** | Oats, nuts, seeds, dates, fruit | Oats 25 g (13%) → 3.3 · Peanut butter 15 g (25%) → 3.8 · Pumpkin seeds 10 g (30%) → 3.0 · Dates/honey 25 g → 0.5 | **~10–11 g** | Yes, but can't beat Styrkr on protein |
| **T2 + dairy powder** | + skimmed milk powder (~35% protein) | T1 + SMP 15 g → 5.3 (replacing 15 g dates) | **~15–16 g** | Yes (milk is a whole food, just dried) |
| **T3 + egg white powder** | + dried egg white (~80% protein; Veloforte's route) | T2 + egg white 8 g → 6.4 (replacing some oats) | **~20–22 g** | Yes, defensible ("dried egg white" is one ingredient) |
| **T4 + single plant protein** | + pea protein (~80%) | Like T3 | ~20–22 g | Borderline: pea protein is a processed fraction. Avoid "whole food" wording |
| **T5 isolates / crisps** | Whey/soy isolate, protein crisps | — | 20 g+ easily | No, this is the "lab bar" |

**Mass-balance reality check [A]:** 20 g protein + 40 g carbs + ~6 g fibre + ~10 g fat ≈ **76 g of solids**, so the bar will weigh **~85–90 g at ~350 kcal**. That is ~20% heavier than Styrkr. Options for Stage 2/3: (i) accept a bigger "meal-sized" bar, (ii) target 18 g protein / 40 g carbs in ~80 g, or (iii) **segmented "dose" bar** (candidate hero feature). **Recommendation: Tier 3 (milk powder + egg white) is your most credible route to ~20 g while staying honest.** It also explains why nobody has done it: it's hard.

### 6c. Fibre vs fast recovery
- The paper is real: **Mancin L, Burke LM, Rollo I. "Fibre: The Forgotten Carbohydrate in Sports Nutrition Recommendations." *Sports Medicine*, Jan 2025.** Authors at LJMU, Australian Catholic University and the Gatorade Sports Science Institute. It is an opinion/review piece, not a trial **[V – [summary](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/la-fibra-el-carbohidrato-olvidado-en-nutricion-deportiva/), [index](https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn:443/openlb/publication/OLB-PM-39775524)]**. *Get the full text through Durham library before quoting numbers such as "30 g/day".*
- **Pushback:** fibre is **not** novel in this category. Forza (7.4 g), Myprotein Flapjack (7.8 g) and Veloforte Crunch (12 g) already have it. Fibre alone can't be your differentiator.
- **What can be novel is the framing:** "built for the 24-hour recovery window" (e.g. oat beta-glucan, slower dairy protein), not "eat within 30 minutes". Stage 2 scores this against athlete appeal and claim risk.
- **Working target [A]:** ~5–6 g fibre per bar. Enough for a "source of fibre" claim, low enough to limit gut risk. Gut comfort must be measured in the pilot.

### 6d. Claims — what Stage 1 found

| Claim | Status in GB | Conditions | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|

Pack must state the benefit comes from **4 g/kg body weight of carbs from all sources, in doses, within the first 4 h and no later than 6 h** after exercise. **Adults** after glycogen-depleting exercise only | Usable, but your pack must carry the 4 g/kg wording. A 70 kg athlete needs 280 g carbs; one bar is ~15% of that. Be honest about it |
| "Protein contributes to a growth in / maintenance of muscle mass" | Authorised **[A – confirm exact wording and condition on the GB register]** | Food must be at least a "source of protein" (≥12% energy from protein) **[A – confirm]** | Easily met |
| "High protein" | Nutrition claim | ≥20% of energy from protein **[V – [Reg. 1924/2006 Annex](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2006/1924/annexes/2006-12-20)]** | 20 g P in 350 kcal = 80/350 = **22.9% ✓**. 15 g P in 285 kcal (Styrkr) = 21% ✓ |

| "Speeds recovery", "reduces soreness", "repairs muscle", "anti-inflammatory" | **Not authorised** [A – none found on register] | — | **Never say these on pack, website or in the pitch** |
| "Supports normal muscle recovery" (Styrkr's wording) | **Possibly non-compliant** [A] | Not an authorised wording as far as found | Get a trading-standards or regulatory view (Stage 3). Don't copy it |
| Beta-glucan claims (cholesterol, blood glucose) | Authorised, with dose conditions (e.g. 3 g/day oat beta-glucan for cholesterol) [A – confirm] | — | Probably not worth it: off-message for athletes and hard to dose in one bar |

## 8. Talking to the ecosystem (Step 2) — competitor-focused question bank

The full athlete survey and Mom-Test interview script come in Stage 4. These are the **competitor-intelligence** questions to fold into every conversation now.

**Athletes (past behaviour only)**
1. Think about your last hard session. What did you eat or drink in the 2 hours after? Why that?
2. Have you ever bought a recovery bar or shake? Which one, where, what did it cost, did you buy it again?
3. What made you stop using [product]? (taste, price, gut, hassle?)
4. Have you ever had stomach trouble from a bar or supplement? What was it?
5. Are you drug-tested, or could you be? Do you check for Informed Sport?

**Sports dietitians / nutritionists (e.g. Team Durham, Newcastle Falcons/United academies, SENr registrants)**
1. What do you actually recommend to athletes after training? Any brands you recommend or warn against, and why?
2. What do you think of Styrkr BAR+ / Veloforte Forza as recovery foods?
3. Where is fibre helpful or harmful in the 24 h after training?
4. Would you trust a whole-food bar without batch testing for a tested athlete?

**S&C coaches / club coaches**
1. What do your athletes eat after sessions? Who pays for it, the club or the athlete?
2. Has the club ever bought nutrition in bulk? What, from whom, what price?

**Suppliers / co-manufacturers**
1. Do you make bars for any sports brands? (They often won't say which. Ask anyway.)
2. MOQ and lead time for a cold-pressed or baked bar? Can you work with egg white and milk powder?
3. What causes most new sports bars to fail at scale-up?

**Retailers / gyms / cycle shops** (an addition to the DVS list; retailers see sell-through)
1. Which recovery bars sell, and which sit on the shelf? Why?
2. What price point moves? What margin do you need?

**Investors / mentors / trade bodies** (e.g. ESSNA, the European Specialist Sports Nutrition Alliance)
1. How crowded is sports bars from an investor's view? What would make a new brand investable?
2. What happened to recent UK bar brands that failed?

### Step 4 — engage a competitor directly?
**Recommendation: not yet.** Approaching Styrkr or Veloforte now risks tipping them off to an unbuilt idea for little gain. **Cheaper equivalents:** (a) the Styrkr founder's podcast interview (linked above) for their story; (b) ask cycle-shop staff about sell-through; (c) buy and blind-taste the bars in your pilot. **Revisit in Week 9–10** once you have a prototype, when a "fellow founder" conversation about co-manufacturing or channels has more upside. Questions to ask then: *How did you find your co-manufacturer? What MOQ did you start at? What was the hardest part of getting into Sigma or Halfords? Why didn't you go with whole-food protein?*

## 9. End of Stage 1

### Key takeaways
1. Styrkr's "natural" bar is isolate- and syrup-based. **Your differentiation against Styrkr is real and provable from its ingredient list.**
2. **Veloforte Forza is the real head-to-head competitor.** Its weakness is protein (12 g).
3. **The white space is "full-dose protein + full carb refuel + whole-food protein + fibre"**, and it exists partly because it's hard to make (bar weight ~85–90 g).
4. Fibre is **not** a differentiator by itself. The **24-hour recovery framing** might be, which is Stage 2's job.
5. The biggest competitor is likely **chocolate milk, a banana, or nothing**. Prove athletes will pay before claiming a migraine problem.

### Risks
- **Demand risk:** amateur athletes may not pay ~£2.80 for recovery at all.
- **Feasibility risk:** 20 g P + 40 g C + whole-food protein + fibre may be too big, too dense or too dry, and shelf life with egg/milk powder is untested.
- **Me-too risk:** Veloforte could launch a "Forza 20" quickly. Your defence must be something beyond macros (channel, certification, community, segment).
- **Claims risk:** recovery wording is tightly regulated; copying competitor language could be non-compliant.
- **Data risk:** this stage relied on retailer listings, so check packs before pitching.

### Open questions for you
1. **Fill in the blanks:** today's DVS week, budget (£), hours per week, pitch length, and your interview/survey targets.
2. **Vegan or not?** Tier 3 (milk + egg) is the most credible protein route but excludes vegans, a segment Styrkr owns. Are you OK leaving vegans to Styrkr?
3. **Bar size:** would you accept an ~85–90 g, ~350 kcal "meal-sized" recovery bar, or is ~70 g a hard requirement?
4. **Beachhead hunch:** endurance (runners, cyclists, triathletes, where Styrkr and Veloforte already sell) or **team sport (football, rugby, university clubs)**, where none of these brands has a footprint and where your refereeing and coaching network gives you access? *My early read: team sport is less contested and plays to your strengths. Stage 2 will score it.*
5. Do you have DVS access to **Mintel** (via Durham library) and the **full text** of Mancin et al. (2025)?
6. Did DVS give you a competitor template? If so, paste it and I'll re-cut the matrix.

### What this stage adds to the pitch
- **"How do you know? — proof I know the competitors":** the matrix, the ingredient-list comparison with Styrkr, and the positioning map, all ready for one slide.
- **Problem slide (support):** athletes are offered either sugar-plus-isolate "natural" bars or polyol lab bars. Nothing gives a full, real-food recovery dose.
- **Innovation slide (foundation):** the precise gap statement in Section 5.
- **Coachability evidence:** "Our first hypothesis was 'natural high-carb recovery bar'. Research showed that's taken, so we pivoted to the protein-dose gap." Judges like to see this.
