## Headline findings (read this first)

1. **Styrkr BAR+ is not a whole-food bar.** It is marketed as "all natural", but its listed ingredients start with **glucose syrup**, then **soy protein crispies (isolated soy protein)**, caster sugar, inverted sugar syrup and **soy protein isolate**. It has **~28 g sugar** and only **~0.7 g fibre** per 74 g bar. **[V – Sigma Sports / Bike24 listings]**
2. **Your closest competitor is Veloforte Forza.** Forza is UK-handmade from apricots, almonds and **egg white** (no isolate). It is positioned for recovery at 3:1, with **12 g protein, 38 g carbs and 7.4 g fibre** per 70 g. **[V]** So "natural + fibre + recovery" is **already taken**. Forza's weakness is **protein: 12 g is low**.
3. **No UK bar we found combines ≥20 g protein + ≥40 g carbs + whole-food protein + ≥5 g fibre.** Every 20 g-protein bar is either carb-light (Barebells, Grenade, PhD, Fulfil, Veloforte Protein Crunch) or uses isolates and syrups (Myprotein Flapjack, Clif Builders). This is the white space (§6). Your decisions on size and vegan make it **feasible** (§7b).
4. **The two segments have different incumbents.** *Endurance:* the natural brands (Styrkr, Veloforte, TORQ) all sell through **cycle retail**. *Team sport:* the **big lab brands own pro clubs** (SiS supplies 100+ pro football clubs and partners Man Utd, Celtic, Aston Villa and Saracens; Myprotein partners Sale Sharks; Grenade partnered Scottish Rugby) **[V/C – club and brand press releases]**. **Nobody visibly serves grassroots and university team sport with a natural recovery bar [A – test in interviews].** That is your access advantage as a referee and coach.
5. **The real competitor is probably "nothing", a meal, or chocolate milk.** Customer discovery must establish whether amateur athletes **pay for recovery at all** before the pitch can claim a "migraine problem".
6. **Claims risk is an opening.** The GB-authorised carbohydrate recovery claim carries heavy mandatory consumer information (4 g/kg within 4–6 h). Loose wording like "supports muscle recovery" may not be compliant **[A]**. A compliant, evidence-led brand can turn that into part of its credibility story.

> **Lab-bar takeaway:** these bars use 15–20 g of polyols to hit 20 g protein with "<2 g sugar". That is **the wrong design for glycogen refuelling**, because polyols are excluded from the GB carbohydrate recovery claim. ## 2. STEP 2 — Talk to the ecosystem

DVS says the best insight usually comes from people **around** the market. Every conversation should touch the five DVS questions:

| DVS question | Ask it this way (past behaviour, not opinions) |
|---|---|
| What problem is being solved? | "Tell me about the last time recovery went badly for you or your athletes. What happened?" |
| How credible is the solution? | "Have you tried [Forza / BAR+ / chocolate milk]? What happened next? Did you buy it again?" |
| What barriers to adoption exist? | "What stopped you using a recovery product regularly?" (price, taste, gut, hassle, testing risk) |
| How developed is the market? | "Who in your club or squad uses recovery products? Who pays: the athlete, the club or a sponsor?" |
| What alternatives are customers considering? | "What do you have in your kit bag right now?" |

### Question bank by group

The full athlete survey and Mom-Test interview script come in Stage 4. These are the **competitor-intelligence** questions to fold into every conversation now.

**Athletes (past behaviour only)**
1. Think about your last hard session. What did you eat or drink in the 2 hours after? Why that?
2. Have you ever bought a recovery bar or shake? Which one, where, what did it cost, did you buy it again?
3. What made you stop using [product]? (taste, price, gut, hassle?)
4. Have you ever had stomach trouble from a bar or supplement? What was it?
5. Are you drug-tested, or could you be? Do you check for Informed Sport?

**Sports dietitians / nutritionists (e.g. Team Durham, Newcastle Falcons/United academies, SENr registrants)**
1. What do you actually recommend to athletes after training? Any brands you recommend or warn against, and why?
2. What do you think of Styrkr BAR+ / Veloforte Forza as recovery foods?
3. Where is fibre helpful or harmful in the 24 h after training?
4. Would you trust a whole-food bar without batch testing for a tested athlete?

**S&C coaches / club coaches**
1. What do your athletes eat after sessions? Who pays for it, the club or the athlete?
2. Has the club ever bought nutrition in bulk? What, from whom, what price?

**Suppliers / co-manufacturers**
1. Do you make bars for any sports brands? (They often won't say which. Ask anyway.)
2. MOQ and lead time for a cold-pressed or baked bar? Can you work with egg white and milk powder?
3. What causes most new sports bars to fail at scale-up?

**Retailers / gyms / cycle shops** (an addition to the DVS list; retailers see sell-through)
1. Which recovery bars sell, and which sit on the shelf? Why?
2. What price point moves? What margin do you need?

**Investors / mentors / trade bodies** (e.g. ESSNA, the European Specialist Sports Nutrition Alliance)
1. How crowded is sports bars from an investor's view? What would make a new brand investable?

## 7. Section 3 tensions — first pass

### 7a. "Styrkr already does this"
**No, and here is the evidence.** Styrkr is high-carb, but its protein is soy isolate, its first ingredient is glucose syrup, and it has 0.7 g fibre. **Pitch line [draft]:** *"Styrkr proved athletes will pay ~£2.80 for a recovery bar. We're building the one a sports dietitian would actually recommend."* Turn their success into your market validation. **But the head-to-head slide should compare against Veloforte Forza**, the closer and stronger product: *"Same real-food approach, with a full protein dose: 20 g vs 12 g."*

### 7b. Whole food vs high protein: definition tiers

Proposed honest definition: **"whole-food" = every protein source is a recognisable food or a single-ingredient dried food, with no isolates, crisps, collagen or blends.**

Rough protein maths for a ~80 g bar. Ingredient protein values are typical label figures **[A – confirm against supplier specs in Stage 3]**.

| Tier | Protein sources allowed | Example build (g → protein) | Est. protein | Honest? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 Strict whole food** | Oats, nuts, seeds, dates, fruit | Oats 25 g (13%) → 3.3 · Peanut butter 15 g (25%) → 3.8 · Pumpkin seeds 10 g (30%) → 3.0 · Dates/honey 25 g → 0.5 | **~10–11 g** | Yes, but can't beat Styrkr on protein |
| **T2 + dairy powder** | + skimmed milk powder (~35% protein) | T1 + SMP 15 g → 5.3 (replacing 15 g dates) | **~15–16 g** | Yes (milk is a whole food, just dried) |
| **T3 + egg white powder** | + dried egg white (~80% protein; Veloforte's route) | T2 + egg white 8 g → 6.4 (replacing some oats) | **~20–22 g** | Yes, defensible ("dried egg white" is one ingredient) |
| **T4 + single plant protein** | + pea protein (~80%) | Like T3 | ~20–22 g | Borderline: pea protein is a processed fraction. Avoid "whole food" wording |
| **T5 isolates / crisps** | Whey/soy isolate, protein crisps | — | 20 g+ easily | No, this is the "lab bar" |

**Mass-balance reality check [A]:** 20 g protein + 40 g carbs + ~6 g fibre + ~10 g fat ≈ **76 g of solids**, so the bar will weigh **~85–90 g at ~350 kcal**. That is ~20% heavier than Styrkr. **Decided (6 Oct):** no 70 g limit and not vegan, so we **go with Tier 3 (milk powder + egg white) at ~85–90 g**. Working target for Stage 3: **~20 g protein / ~45 g carbs / 5–6 g fibre / ~350 kcal [A]**. A segmented "dose" bar is still a candidate hero feature for Stage 2. It would also let a 60 kg runner eat part of the bar and a 100 kg forward eat all of it. Tier 3 also explains why nobody has done this: it's hard.

### 7c. Fibre vs fast recovery
- The paper is real: **Mancin L, Burke LM, Rollo I. "Fibre: The Forgotten Carbohydrate in Sports Nutrition Recommendations." *Sports Medicine*, Jan 2025.** Authors at LJMU, Australian Catholic University and the Gatorade Sports Science Institute. It is an opinion/review piece, not a trial **[V – [summary](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/la-fibra-el-carbohidrato-olvidado-en-nutricion-deportiva/), [index](https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn:443/openlb/publication/OLB-PM-39775524)]**. *Get the full text through Durham library before quoting numbers such as "30 g/day".*
- **Pushback:** fibre is **not** novel in this category. Forza (7.4 g), Myprotein Flapjack (7.8 g) and Veloforte Crunch (12 g) already have it. Fibre alone can't be your differentiator.
- **What can be novel is the framing:** "built for the 24-hour recovery window" (e.g. oat beta-glucan, slower dairy protein), not "eat within 30 minutes". Stage 2 scores this against athlete appeal and claim risk.
- **Working target [A]:** ~5–6 g fibre per bar. Enough for a "source of fibre" claim, low enough to limit gut risk. Gut comfort must be measured in the pilot.

### 7d. Claims — what Stage 1 found

| Claim | Status in GB | Conditions | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Carbohydrates contribute to the recovery of normal muscle function (contraction) after highly intensive and/or long-lasting physical exercise…" | **Authorised** (Reg. 2015/7, retained in GB) | Carbs metabolised by humans (**polyols excluded**). Pack must state the benefit comes from **4 g/kg body weight of carbs from all sources, in doses, within the first 4 h and no later than 6 h** after exercise. **Adults** after glycogen-depleting exercise only | Usable, but your pack must carry the 4 g/kg wording. A 70 kg athlete needs 280 g carbs; one bar is ~15% of that. Be honest about it |
| "Protein contributes to a growth in / maintenance of muscle mass" | Authorised **[A – confirm exact wording and condition on the GB register]** | Food must be at least a "source of protein" (≥12% energy from protein) **[A – confirm]** | Easily met |
| "High protein" | Nutrition claim | ≥20% of energy from protein **[V – [Reg. 1924/2006 Annex](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2006/1924/annexes/2006-12-20)]** | 20 g P in 350 kcal = 80/350 = **22.9% ✓**. 15 g P in 285 kcal (Styrkr) = 21% ✓ |
| "High fibre" / "Source of fibre" | Nutrition claim | High: ≥6 g/100 g **or** ≥3 g/100 kcal [V]. Source: ≥3 g/100 g or ≥1.5 g/100 kcal [A – confirm on Annex] | 6 g fibre in 88 g = 6.8 g/100 g → **"high fibre" ✓** |
| "Speeds recovery", "reduces soreness", "repairs muscle", "anti-inflammatory" | **Not authorised** [A – none found on register] | — | **Never say these on pack, website or in the pitch** |
| "Supports normal muscle recovery" (Styrkr's wording) | **Possibly non-compliant** [A] | Not an authorised wording as far as found | Get a trading-standards or regulatory view (Stage 3). Don't copy it |
| Beta-glucan claims (cholesterol, blood glucose) | Authorised, with dose conditions (e.g. 3 g/day oat beta-glucan for cholesterol) [A – confirm] | — | Probably not worth it: off-message for athletes and hard to dose in one bar |

## 9. End of Stage 1

### Key takeaways
1. **Veloforte Forza is the primary competitor.** Its weakness is protein (12 g). Styrkr proves the category but is isolate- and syrup-based.
2. **White space: a ~20 g protein + ~45 g carb + ≥5 g fibre bar with whole-food protein (milk + egg).** Your decisions (no 70 g limit, not vegan) make it feasible at ~85–90 g.
3. **Channel gap:** natural brands live in cycle retail and lab brands own pro clubs. **Grassroots and university clubs, team and solo, look unserved [A].**
4. Fibre isn't a differentiator by itself. The 24-hour recovery framing might be (Stage 2).
5. The biggest competitor is likely **chocolate milk, a banana, or nothing**. Prove willingness to pay.

### Risks
- **Demand:** amateurs may not pay ~£2.75–3 for recovery.
- **Feasibility:** a ~90 g bar with milk and egg powder raises texture, water-activity and shelf-life questions (Stage 3).
- **Me-too:** Veloforte could launch a higher-protein Forza. The defence is channel, evidence and the hero feature, not macros alone.
- **Claims:** recovery wording is tightly regulated.
- **Two segments split focus:** mitigated by running the pilot as a solo-vs-team comparison.
- **Data:** this stage relied on retailer listings, so verify packs.

### Open questions for you
1. **Still missing:** current DVS week, budget (£), hours per week, pitch length, and interview/survey targets. Stages 3, 6 and 7 depend on them.
2. **Pilot groups:** which **one** running/cycling club and **one** team squad can you realistically get access to through your network?
3. Can you get **Mintel** (Durham library) and the **full text** of Mancin et al. (2025)?
4. Do you have a **working brand name**? Run the UK IPO trade mark check (Step 1 action).

### What this stage adds to the pitch
- **"Proof I know the competitors":** the DVS-method write-up, the Step 5 scorecard, the Styrkr ingredient comparison and the positioning map.
- **Problem slide (support):** athletes get either sugar-plus-isolate "natural" bars or polyol lab bars. Nothing gives a full real-food recovery dose.
- **Go-to-market logic:** "We won't fight in cycle shops. We'll win clubs."
- **Coachability evidence:** "We started at 'natural high-carb', found it was taken, and moved to the protein-dose and club-channel gap." Also: "I adopted a mentor's challenge to test solo vs team as an experiment."
